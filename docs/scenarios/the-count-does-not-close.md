# The Count Does Not Close

An Act I scenario for Sigurd Gustavsen. Built from his character sheet outward.

---

## 1. Who is Sigurd, dramatically

Not his skills. His engine:

> **He is always right and nobody listens to him.**

It is on the sheet in three places and they all agree. The Read tells him what
a person is about to do and never why, so he is certain and unable to explain.
Perception 67 and Insight 37 both go a grade easier on behaviour, so he sees
more of a room than anybody in it. And Influence 22 with CHA 7 means that when
he opens his mouth, it does not land.

The passions are the fault line, deliberately: **"I knew. I always know." 60**
against **"Hate (being talked over)" 55.** Five points. Not much to carry for a
career.

**His asset is testimony.** That is the only currency he has. He cannot
persuade, he cannot charm, he cannot pull rank, he is fifteen and belt-born and
four days on the station. The one thing he can do is *see a true thing and say
it out loud where a mind can hear it* — and in this setting that is permanent,
public and unerasable.

So: the antagonist is not somebody who can out-fight him or out-think him. He
would win. **The antagonist is somebody who makes being right worthless.**

## 2. The two antagonists

### Marek Sollen — the antagonist of his voice

Already built. Rising man in the 61 Cygni assembly, sixties, big, warm, funny,
remembers your mother's name and means the asking. Passions: *Children are
children, and somebody must stand between them and the galaxy* **85**. *To be
thanked, by name, in public* **65**. And the one that is aimed straight at
Sigurd: ***A fetch belongs beside a child, never before the assembly* 70.**

**Why him, for this character.** Sollen never has to refute Sigurd. He only has
to be *kind* about him in public. *He is fifteen. He is belt-born. He has been
with us four days. And his fetch told him.* Every word of that is true, none of
it is cruel, and it ends the matter. Sollen's power is credibility, and
credibility is the exact thing Sigurd cannot buy.

He is also the man who would legislate Pitch out of being a witness, which
makes him the enemy of the only relationship Sigurd has — and Pitch's own wound
is nine hundred years of nobody asking. Sollen proposes to make that permanent
and calls it child protection.

And he was tested as a child and not chosen. Every first-through he sees is the
one he was not. **He cannot be hated cleanly**, which is the point: an hour in
his company is genuinely pleasant and you walk out having agreed to something.

### Mereth Vane — the antagonist of his body

New. Nineteen, lieutenant of the Late, and **she has the Read.**

The Late put the Read in lieutenants because knowing what the person in front
of you is about to do, and nothing about why, suits the work. So she has
Sigurd's knack, four years of using it to kill, and a living blade.

**Why her, for this character.** She cancels his one advantage. He knows what
she is about to do; she knows what he is about to do; neither of them gets *why*
from the knack. The fight is therefore decided by the thing the knack does not
give — and Sigurd has a fetch who can read a door from the rim, and she has
cut her flash out and nobody carries for her any more.

She is nineteen. She has months. She took the offer because there is something
afterwards, and nobody has told her what afterwards is. **She does not know she
is food.** That is the secret the player can get and she cannot, and it is a
better weapon than a stunner.

## 3. What they are both already doing — the engine

The campaign premise: the old doors are failing faster than age explains.

Here is why, and it was already in the files. The Quorum moves his instructions,
his timings and his people the only way anything moves between stars: in a
child's hands, through a door. His traffic is **uncounted**. And a door being
worked harder than the count says shows up in exactly one place — the **load
bed**, which absorbs how hard a door is driven and has to be re-balanced when
the compensation starts fighting itself.

Luhman 16: three re-balances in thirty-one years. Nineteen years, then
**twenty-two months.**

Nobody has ever assembled this, because each hall's mind holds its own figure,
no mind can tell another anything, no attestation crosses a door, and no fetch
has ever been asked to carry a maintenance statistic. *The shape of his network
is already in what the minds hold, and the question that would show it has
never once been put to them.* — [[The Quorum]]

**Sigurd put that question to Oluf Varre on his first morning, by accident,
because he pointed at a patch of newer grey on a floor.**

He is now one asked question away from the thing sixty years of concealment
rests on. And he is about to say it out loud at the rail, in a hull where a mind
hears everything and nothing said can ever be deleted.

Which sets the shape of the whole scenario: **they cannot unsay it. They can
only stop him saying it, or make it not count when he does.** Sollen takes the
second job. Vane takes the first. Neither of them is coordinating with the other
and that is why it works.

## 4. The third party, who is worse than either

**Tallyman Captain Brace.** Agenda: *Find the leak at the desk* (0/8). Passions:
*Everyone is accounted for* **80**. *The count must close, whatever it costs*
**70**.

He is the one adult who will **believe Sigurd immediately**, because a count
that does not close is the whole of his life. And he is forty-six, carries eight
restraint loops and a tool that strips a first-through's kit in under a minute,
and his stated position is that somebody has to be willing to put hands on a
child.

So the person who believes him is the frightening one. An ally who is a danger
is worth four allies who are nice, and this campaign had fourteen of the second
kind and none of the first.

## 5. The scenario, in sequences

Each sequence has a dramatic question, who moves first, the reversal, and the
cost. If a sequence's cost is blank it does not get run.

### Sequence 1 — He says it at the rail

- **Question:** does saying a true thing out loud do anything at all?
- **Moves first:** Sigurd, and it is the last time in this scenario that he does.
- **Reversal:** it works perfectly. The Old Man takes it, confirms the Nine's own
  nine doors show the same shape, names three of them, and volunteers that
  nobody has ever asked him to compare. The player gets a real, enormous,
  verified win in about ninety seconds — **and the mind says it in a hall with
  forty people in it, because that is what attestation is.**
- **Cost:** his privacy and his anonymity, both permanently, and he chose it.
  Everyone in that drum now knows the belt-born kid said something about the
  doors. That is how it reaches both antagonists by dusk.

### Sequence 2 — Sollen is already on the station

- **Question:** will an adult with power take this seriously?
- **Moves first:** Sollen, who was aboard before any of this and is here for his
  own business. He hears about it within a watch and sends for Sigurd — warmly,
  properly, through Calloway, with no coercion anywhere.
- **Reversal:** he is *delighted*. He agrees with every word. He thinks it is
  exactly the kind of thing children should not be carrying alone, and he will
  take it to the assembly himself, and he will not be naming Sigurd, and he
  would like Pitch to wait outside.
- **Cost:** the finding. It is his now, it will be correctly reported, and
  Sigurd will never be connected to it. He is not lying and he is not stealing.
  He is helping, which is worse.

### Sequence 3 — The second question, and Pitch

- **Question:** can Sigurd get something Sollen cannot?
- **Moves first:** Sigurd, if the player has learned the lesson of the first two
  sequences — that he cannot win on credibility and must win on access.
- **Reversal:** Pitch has been able to feel whether a mouth is pinned or
  slipping, from the rim, without instruments, for nine hundred years, and has
  never been asked. Pitch volunteers nothing. If the player asks, they get the
  only sense in human space that reads a stray before it happens.
- **Cost:** Pitch's position. The moment a fetch is the source of a finding,
  Sollen's passion fires at **70**, and the thing Sigurd did to make his friend
  matter is the thing that puts his friend in front of an assembly.

### Sequence 4 — Brace takes an interest

- **Question:** what does it look like when somebody powerful believes you?
- **Moves first:** Brace, physically. He does not send for Sigurd; he is waiting
  at the bottom of the stair, and he walks him somewhere, with a hand flat on
  his shoulder, which is how he counts.
- **Reversal:** he believes all of it and it is terrible. He wants the uncounted
  traffic and he will take the station apart to find it, starting with every
  courier whose crossings do not reconcile — which includes a great many
  children who have done nothing, and at least one of Calloway's.
- **Cost:** somebody innocent. A first-through the player has met is stopped,
  stripped of kit in a hall, and held, because the count does not close around
  them. Calloway cannot stop it. This is the act's first real loss and it is
  caused by the player being right.

### Sequence 5 — Vane arrives

- **Question:** are they actually in danger?
- **Moves first:** Vane. One person, one at a time, through a door, with no
  lading and a scar in the cloth where the flash was, at the worst hour.
- **Reversal:** she is not there for Sigurd. She is there for **Varre** — the one
  human being who holds the figure first-hand and said it out loud in a hull
  whose mind cannot tell anybody. A mind cannot be killed. A sixty-two-year-old
  tallyman can, and that closes the hole.
- **Cost:** Varre, unless the player crosses and is there, and the only person
  who can cross is a child. Played at full lethality. **This is the sequence
  where violence must be able to win.**

### Sequence 6 — Two Reads in one room

- **Question:** what is left when your one advantage is cancelled?
- **Moves first:** whoever the dice say, because both of them know.
- **Reversal:** the knack gives neither of them *why*. Sigurd has a fetch who
  can feel the mouth behind him and tell him which way the door is going.
  Vane cut her flash out and nobody carries for her.
- **Cost:** stated honestly before the player chooses it. She is better than
  him at every part of this except one.

### Sequence 7 — She is nineteen

- **Question:** is there anything afterwards?
- **Moves first:** the player, if they have the secret and choose to use it.
- **Reversal:** the truth works on her better than a stunner, and it is the one
  thing she has never been given. *You cannot be overwritten while a door will
  still take you. The day the hall turns you back is the day you become
  takeable. That is what afterwards means.*
- **Cost:** whichever way it goes. Tell her and she may break, and a broken
  Late is hunted by her own. Do not tell her and she is back, and next time she
  is not alone.

## 6. What this act takes

Per `story.md`, every act takes something that does not come back. Act I now
takes **three**, and all three are caused by Sigurd being right:

1. **His anonymity**, in Sequence 1, chosen.
2. **An innocent first-through**, in Sequence 4, not chosen.
3. **Varre or Vane**, in Sequences 5–7, depending.

whether the player acts or not, and each answers within a watch when the player
does.

## 8. The cast role table

Per `CAST.md` §1. The cast is a function of Sigurd's engine, not a list. These
five roles are **always filled**; who fills them is disposable and is invented
on demand by the three-minute recipe.

| Role | What it does to him | How often | Currently |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The ambient majority** — talk over him, no malice in it | makes the engine true continuously, for free, in any crowd | every scene, unnamed, one clause each | the kettle argument; the queue at four |
| **The one who listens, and is dangerous for it** | converts his only asset into jeopardy | once an act | **Tallyman Captain Brace** |
| **Better than him at his own thing, and generous about it** | denies him the consolation of being special | standing | **Ferren** socially; **Mereth Vane** at the knack |
| **Needs exactly what he can do and does not know it** | gives him somewhere to be the protagonist | one live at all times | **Oluf Varre**, who has had the answer for thirty-one years |
| **Does not need him at all** — complete, busy, uninterested | stops the world existing to serve him | often, and cheap | **Hesk**; **Sennet**, who has her own job |

**The sixth role, specific to this PC and the most important one:** somebody who
**asks him something**. Nobody has, in his whole life, which is why *"you could
have asked"* is Pitch's key line and why the Read is unbearable. The first adult
who asks Sigurd a question and waits for the answer owns him, and both
antagonists are capable of working that out. Sollen will do it on purpose.

## 9. What to invent next, and where

Density before breadth. The Nine has forty thousand people and the player has
met seven.

- **The innocent first-through Brace strips in Sequence 4** must be somebody the
  player has already met and liked, which means inventing two or three
  bunkroom-mates in the next session and letting the player pick a favourite
  before anybody knows what it is for. Do not decide which one in advance.
- **Someone in the queue at four** who crosses to Luhman 16 regularly and has
  never once stood it, because they are a courier and standing is the dull part.
  Edge: they are the reason nobody has reported door four since Tuesday.
- **Varre's counterpart at Procyon**, if the player ever asks the same question
  twice. Edge: they are the reason an existing fact is true.
