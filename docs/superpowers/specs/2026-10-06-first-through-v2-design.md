# First Through — design, v2

**Date:** 2026-10-06
**Status:** Current. Supersedes `2026-10-05-first-through-design.md`.
**Repo:** `fourth-wall-gaming/first-through-campaign` (plugin `first-through`)
**Engine:** mythras-gm, system `mythras`
**Campaign id:** `myth-campaign-first-through-s1`

v1 described a campaign about failing infrastructure in which nobody could die,
run by a politically powerless service in a galaxy governed by a counting house.
It was coherent, grey, and not a game anybody wants to be fifteen in. Everything
below replaces it. The section "What changed and why" at the end is the audit.

---

## 1. What this is

A published campaign plugin a 10-15 year old installs and plays solo with Claude
as GM. **Space opera.** The register is Star Wars and Douglas Hill: velocity,
wonder, a home worth belonging to, villains worth hating, and a child who is
genuinely the most important person in the room.

The astronomy is real and that is a feature, not the point. If it ever competes
with the adventure, the adventure wins.

## 2. The premise in six lines

- Nine hundred and seventy-five years ago the **First Reach** spent an entire
  civilisation building three thousand entangled anchor pairs and launching half
  of each at a twentieth of lightspeed. It worked, and it finished them.
- A door moves matter instantly. **No signal can ever cross one** — entanglement
  carries no information, which is a theorem. So nothing outruns a walking child.
- **Only the young can cross**, and a crossing only happens because a
  first-through holds it open. The holding frays them. Around twenty-two you are
  **landlocked**, under whatever star you were standing under, for life.
- The fraying is also where the **knacks** come from. Power and damage are one
  process seen from two ends.
- **Nothing dead crosses.** No freight, no tools, no clothes. You arrive naked
  and everything you wear is local and borrowed. Information rides in genomes,
  carried by **fetches** — five sentient peoples who cross for free.
- Doors do not fail. They **stray**: a mouth is pinned to a gravitational
  potential, not a place, and when the arithmetic branches it jumps. That is the
  campaign.

## 3. Tone rules — binding

1. **The Threshold is a home worth belonging to.** Loud, funny, fed, loyal.
   [[Calloway]] is the best boss these kids will ever have and is written that
   way. Belonging to it should feel good every session.
2. **The dysfunction is external and has faces.** The Tally is a legitimate
   traffic authority and an arguable obstacle, not a villain. **The Second Hand**
   are criminals and everybody says so. **The Quorum** is the big bad.
3. **Fetches are people with full standing**, settled across the Causeway.
   Anybody who treats one as freight is telling you what they are. No background
   civil-rights misery.
4. **Heroism is available.** The player can win, save people, and be thanked.
5. **The job is wonderful.** A door strays, somebody goes to see where it is now,
   and they are the first human being ever to stand there. Lead with that.

## 4. Danger

**People die, including the player's character.** Mythras unmodified, nothing
softened behind the screen.

The protection a child gets is **information, not immunity**: a character may
die only from a risk the player could see when they chose it. Name the danger
before it is live, never spring a lethal surprise, answer *can this kill me*
straight, say out loud that Luck Points exist, and let a death land.

Full rules: `TABLE-ADDENDUM.md`.

## 5. The crossing, exactly

| | |
|---|---|
| **Solo** | seconds. One unit of fraying. |
| **A fetch** | **free.** They are the complementary half of the door and cross in its wake. No limit anybody has found. |
| **A child carried** | minutes, in contact. Costs the carrier; the child is fine. |
| **An adult carried** | minutes. Costs the carrier several crossings' worth. The passenger is ill for a week; the old or frail may not survive. |
| **Three or four carries in a row** | the carrier is finished for days. |

So adults are not barred — they are **expensive**, because every adult crossing
spends a measurable piece of a named child's life. The Threshold's proudest rule
is one line: *nobody carries for convenience*. The Second Hand carries for money.

**Consequences that matter at the table:** no army can ever be delivered, so a
single carried individual is the whole instrument of interstellar force — which
makes assassination the primary political weapon, the manifest an
anti-assassination measure rather than bureaucracy, and the cost of sending an
envoy *the diplomatic message itself*. And every conspiracy in human space needs
a first-through who will say yes.

## 6. Straying

A mouth holds the **gravitational potential** of where it was made. Masses move,
the matching surface moves, and occasionally the solution branches and the mouth
jumps. Four seconds.

- The **route never changes** — paired to Gliese 887 means Gliese 887 forever.
  What changes is where in that system you arrive.
- Usually the same world at the same depth in its well; sometimes another body;
  **never another star.**
- **Rock closes it.** A throat cannot establish into solid matter, so the door
  goes dark rather than killing anybody: closed, not destroyed, and it can stray
  again.
- **Vacuum and water do not close it.** Which is why **the Listen** is the most
  valuable knack there is.
- A **stutter** precedes a jump — days of it, working perfectly in between, which
  is why settlements argue instead of leaving.
- Strays are predictable in principle. Nobody has built the model. Somebody has.

## 7. People

**First-throughs.** Tested at nine, most children fail. Taken up, the Yards at
twelve, working at fifteen, landlocked at about twenty-two. Your **number** is
your status. Four hundred of them at the Nine, no union, no vote, and completely
irreplaceable.

**Fetches.** Five sentient peoples — sixlimbs, fathoms, cairns, glints,
scatters — who keep what we carry, cross free, and are far better connected than
any human institution. Partnerships are mutual, negotiated and **transient**:
they form, hold, drift and end. The **quiet** grows between partners over years
and fades within a year of parting.

**Governing rule for all five:** physiology is fixed, culture is a default, the
individual is neither. Follies are negotiations, never malfunctions, surface once
a sitting, and the fetch gives information and access while the player decides.
See `lore/gm-guide/playing-fetches.md`.

## 8. Powers

Mythras Imperative **Superpowers**, INT (tech/training) branch. Three core powers
per pre-gen: one is kit (Life Support, with External Power Source), two are
**knacks** — the Listen, the Hold, the Quick, the Set, the Reckoning, the
Knowing, the Read.

**Teleport stays excluded.** Personal teleportation being impossible is why the
Causeway matters.

Fraying is tracked as a `crossings` count in the character's `extras` bag and
narrated; no engine change.

## 9. v1 scope

**Two scenarios, in order.**

**1. *How Many*** — the opener, entirely at the Nine. A door is stuttering with
people on the far side. Children can be carried cheaply; adults cannot. So the
adults over there are loading their children onto a door they themselves cannot
use, and the player is the only person alive who can bring them across — paying
for every one out of themselves. Winnable with courage and cost. Teaches the
whole setting by making the player live in it.

**2. *Kapteyn's*** — the first real adventure. Six hundred years of watch on a
dark housing that has never reported anything. It reports something. A buried
door has strayed again, there are eleven thousand people's descendants behind it,
and the jump should have been predictable — so somebody may have known.

Acts beyond that are outline only, in `lore/gm-secret/`.

## 10. Content rules — binding

1. **Astronomy is real and cited.** `data/systems.json` is authoritative;
   distances computed from SIMBAD parallaxes. If prose and data disagree, the
   data wins and the prose is a bug. There is a test.
2. **Life is invented but constrained** — unobserved surfaces only, consistent
   with the real system, flagged `invented: "life"`.
3. **No Douglas Hill IP.** Shape only, never names.
4. **No engine changes.** This repo's Python is confined to `tests/` and
   `maps/`.
5. **Seed invariants:** session 0, all beats pending, one `gm-note`,
   `current_scene` names no character.

## 11. What changed from v1, and why

| v1 | v2 | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Doors fail and die | Doors **stray** | Decay is a maintenance job. A network that rewires itself is an adventure engine, and "first-through" finally means what it says. |
| Nobody dies, in both directions | **People die**, risk named first | A danger that cannot kill is not a danger, and a ten-year-old knows it in one session. |
| Powers are kit | Powers are **knacks** from the fraying | Kids want powers. Deriving them from the thing that will end you is the bargain that makes the game. |
| Anyone can cross; doors pass bodies and boxes | Only the young; **nothing dead crosses** | Makes the child protagonist structurally necessary rather than a concession, and forces the whole economy. |
| Tolk, a companion animal | **Five sentient peoples** | A companion-creature is equipment with feelings. Also kills the Pokémon resemblance at the root. |
| The Tally quietly sinister; fetches with no standing | Tally legitimate; **fetches are people**; the Second Hand are criminals | v1 was Kafka in space with nowhere to be heroic. |
| Calloway, a haunted desk officer | Calloway, **a beloved leader** | The players need somewhere to belong and someone to follow. |
| Entry points: a dying anchor, a barricade | ***How Many***, then **Kapteyn's** | Both v1 openers were work orders. |
