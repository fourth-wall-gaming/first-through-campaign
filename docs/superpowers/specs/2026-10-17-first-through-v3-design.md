**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Status:** Design. Supersedes `2026-10-06-first-through-v2-design.md` where they
disagree; extends `2026-10-07-computation-and-minds-design.md` rather than
replacing it.
**Repo:** `fourth-wall-gaming/first-through-campaign` (plugin `first-through`)
**Campaign id:** `myth-campaign-first-through-s1` — **live, at session 6.** This
is a rewrite under a running game, not a reboot.

---

## 0. Why this exists

v2 diagnosed v1 correctly — "coherent, grey, and not a game anybody wants to be
fifteen in" — and was never propagated. Half the lore files were converted and
half were not, and the TypeDB graph that the GM actually reads at the table was
never re-imported. So session 5 was run, in October 2026, out of v1 text.

The player's notes from that session are the brief, and they are quoted here
because they are more precise than any summary of them:

- *"The whole medical mystery thing is incredibly boring and the way you're
  describing it (show don't tell with no clear direction) is frustrating."*
- *"I don't like the fact that these star systems are massively
  under­populated... The low tech vibe and focus on a heavily bureaucratic
  central authority (tallymen blah blah) is not enjoyable as a setting and
  largely makes no sense. There is AI, there are spaceships, why does it feel so
  dystopian and poorly organized? How is there an entire system with only two
  people on it?"*
- *"There's not enough opportunities for conflict... We need some of these
  systems to be run by malevolent people who are trying to take over and kill
  us. Otherwise it's just boring."*
- *"No log books and letters and notes. The information tech needs to be centered
  on DNA and fetches and needs to work effectively with AIs at scale. No more
  tomes and bureaucrats. That's gone."*
- *"Fetches aren't bred. They're not passive vassals that are controlled by
  humans."*
- *"Let's lean into first through powers. That's potentially really fun and we've
  not used them at all yet."*
- *"You arrive naked is not fine. That would be very creepy."*

Two rulings from that session are binding and recorded here so they are not
re-derived away:

1. **Protective kit crosses.** A first-through who arrives with nothing dies on
   an unsurveyed far side. v2's "nothing dead crosses, you arrive naked" is
   replaced per §4.
2. **No nakedness, ever.** This is a game a 10–15 year old plays alone with an
   AI GM, and a mandatory undress-cross-dress beat on every transit is
   inappropriate regardless of how elegant the physics is. This is an audience
   constraint, not a flavour preference, and it outranks worldbuilding.

**The register stays what v2 set:** space opera. Star Wars and Douglas Hill.
Velocity, wonder, a home worth belonging to, villains worth hating, and a child
who is genuinely the most important person in the room. Real astronomy is a
feature and never the point; where it competes with the adventure, the adventure
wins.

## 1. Scale, and the drinking straw

**Roughly thirty billion people across twenty-seven systems.**

| Where | People | Doors |
|---|---|---|
| Sol | ~11 billion | 7 |
| Alpha Centauri | ~4 billion | 4 |
| Tau Ceti | ~3 billion | 3 |
| 61 Cygni | ~2 billion | 4 |
| Procyon, Epsilon Eridani, 40 Eridani | ~1 billion each | 2–3 |
| The rest | hundreds of millions down to low millions | 1–2 |
| **Sirius** | **40,000** | **9** |

Figures above are design targets for spec 2 to firm up per system; all
astronomical quantities are fetched, never recalled.

**Why the growth works.** You cannot ship colonists. Every off-Earth population
descends from the children who crossed one at a time and stayed, and then grew
for eight hundred years. Nobody has ever been moved anywhere in bulk, which is
why each system is a complete civilisation in its own right rather than a colony
of somewhere else.

**And the consequence that runs everything.** A door passes one person at a
time, and only a child can open it, and every crossing costs that child. Nine
doors at Sirius worked flat out is on the order of a few thousand transits a
day. That is the entire bandwidth between every star humanity occupies.

> Thirty billion people, and the pipe joining them is a drinking straw.

So every system is a rich, high-tech, fully developed civilisation of billions
that is effectively alone, and the complete interstellar nervous system of that
civilisation is a few thousand children.

**What this does to the protagonist.** Lint is not a junior clerk in a shabby
service. She is one of a very small number of people who constitute the only
connection between whole worlds. The Threshold stays small, because the doors
cap it — but it stops being "politically powerless and underfunded" and becomes
**the most strategically valuable institution in human space, and far too small
to defend itself.** Everyone wants to own it. That is a conflict engine on its
own.

## 2. Tech: inside a system versus across a door

The low-tech reasoning was always correct *between* stars and was wrongly
extended *inside* them. Corrected:

**Inside a system:** a full high-tech civilisation. Fusion torches, system-wide
minds, arcologies, real medicine, real industry, real militaries, total ambient
information. **Nothing is written down, because there is no reason to write
anything down.** No clerks, no ledgers, no forms, no filing, no tomes.

**Across a door:** no signal, ever. One living thing at a time, and only if it
is young. That is the whole channel.

The setting is therefore not low-tech. It is **a hypermodern civilisation with a
pre-modern nervous system**, and every indignity in it traces to that one fact
rather than to anybody's incompetence.

## 3. The Nine keeps its register, for a physical reason

Sirius is two stars and no worlds. There is nothing to settle and no reason to
live there. So the busiest junction in human space is a two-kilometre cylinder of
forty thousand people who all know each other by sight: a motorway services at
the centre of the galaxy's road network. Small, close, shabby and warm, because
that is all Sirius can support.

**Lint's world does not change. She finds out what it is sitting in.**

And — see §7 — the cross-species council is seated here, precisely because
Sirius has no planets, no wealth and nothing worth owning, which is what a
neutral capital needs. It convenes rarely, because delegates take months to
arrive. The rest of the time it is an empty chamber and a skeleton staff above a
shabby station. Lint's home is the capital of the galaxy and feels like a
village, and both are true.

## 4. What crosses

**You cross in your weave, and nothing manufactured crosses.**

- The **weave** is alive. This is already canon and is why a Threshold flash
  *grows into* it. It is grown, it is yours, and for most people it is the only
  possession that has ever followed anybody between stars.
- For a hostile far side you draw a **pressure-skin**, grown from the same
  stock: Threshold property, issued and returned. "The Nine's suit, stays at the
  Nine" means issued and returned, not immobile.
- The **sensor hood** and the plated suit are of a piece with the skin. Grown,
  not built.
- **Freight does not cross.** No crates, no bar stock, no cutting gear, no bulk
  of any kind. There is no interstellar trade, at all, and that is why every
  system is supplied from inside itself.
- **No dressing rooms, and no nakedness.** Delete every reference.

Structural reasons this preserves, all of which v2 cared about correctly: the
child protagonist is structurally necessary rather than a concession; there is no
trade; and **no army can be shipped anywhere**, which is what makes §9's wars
political and strange rather than conventional.

## 5. Information: the fetch councils, and humans as clients

This replaces the entire information layer. **Everything in the campaign made of
paper is deleted.**

### 5.1 The architecture

Twenty-seven perfect information civilisations, joined by a biological channel
that humans do not own or operate.

- **Fetches are not bred, not stock, and not equipment.** They are people, with
  their own politics.
- A fetch **observes, records, recalls and shares**, and does all of it **under
  their own control**. Great recall is a faculty of theirs, not a service
  performed on them.
- **Read-in and read-out are theirs.** They are managed by the fetch's own
  senses and communications, interfacing directly with a system's minds and
  their infrastructure. **No human writes to a fetch with a machine.** Humans
  request, receive and use.
- Fetches **share with one another, at their own discretion.**
- A mind can compose but never meet another mind, so minds correspond —
  enormously, across months, through this channel.

### 5.2 The mutual hostage

**A fetch cannot cross a door without a first-through. A human cannot hold a
payload without a fetch.** Already canon; now the central political fact of the
setting.

Five peoples who cannot move between stars without human children. Thirty
billion humans who cannot know anything about each other without the fetches.
Neither side can do without the other, both know it, and the whole thing is held
together by bonded pairs of a fifteen-year-old and a friend.

### 5.3 The history that gives the councils their power

The old practice — **books**: unbonded fetch-animals kept as stock, warm and
fed, written to by strangers, handed over at the far end — **was slavery, and it
ended within living memory.**

Humanity spent centuries using these people as media without understanding that
they were people, until it became impossible to pretend. The councils formed, the
trade ended, and the information economy of human space passed into non-human
hands. That is why they hold it now, and why some humans are still sour about it
and would like the old arrangement back.

This is the setting's moral spine. It is legible to a thirteen-year-old, every
faction has a position on it, and Sollen (§9) is on the wrong side of it.

### 5.4 New vocabulary, replacing the paper

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **A lading** | What a courier is carrying. *"What's your lading?"* replaces every manifest in the setting. |
| **Attestation** | What replaces a signature. You say it, a mind witnesses it, and it is permanent and public within that hull. **Nothing can be recorded unofficially, ever.** |
| **Budding** | How a living archive is copied so it can be carried. |
| **Drift** | The divergence between two systems' copies of the same archive. *"The drift on the anchor corpus is nine months."* |
| **A reconciliation** | The job of merging an arriving lading into a local archive. A profession, and a political one, because two archives disagree and somebody decides. |

### 5.5 What specifically dies, and what replaces it

| Gone | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| The Standing as a board on a wall | **The Standing**, a living archive holding the testimony of every far side anybody has stood on. The existing rule survives and improves: a mind may not write on the Standing, because a mind has no testimony. Only a body that went can enter one. And unlike a board, the Standing can be **budded and carried**, so every system holds a divergent copy and they disagree. |
| Hall books, crossing ledgers, Pell Ivor's sheet | The hall reads every crossing itself and the station's mind knows instantly. A tallyman has **authority**, not a ledger — and a refusal is not a line in a book, it is a fact forty thousand people can see the moment it happens. |
| Calloway's order in pen on the back of a rota sheet | She **attests it aloud**, permanently and undeniably. This makes an off-book order *harder*, not easier: she can never unsay it. |
| The anchor logs at van Maanen's, as racking | The **anchor corpus**: six centuries of fault records from twenty-seven systems, arriving months apart, continuously reconciled by a staff that is drowning. |
| E. Tarn's four polite lines | A mind's reply: ten thousand pages, exhaustive, courteous, and still concluding that there is no further action available to this office. |
| Tace Orun's two hundred names on torn lamp-log pages | She gives them to a fetch. |
| Sep Anwar's four typed reports and hand-ruled fold-out | A finding he has got out of the corpus and cannot persuade anybody to carry onward. |

### 5.6 And the thing that makes it land

If information crosses in living cells carried by children, then **children are
the medium.** Science, news, orders, love letters and the Quorum all ride the
same channel. "Somebody has to control the children" is also, unknowingly,
"somebody has to control all information in human space."

## 6. Powers: the Listen promoted, the Hold as endgame, the cold arm deleted

The knacks are fully designed in `lore/character-creation/the-knacks.md` and have
**never once been used at the table.** That is the single largest piece of unspent
material in the campaign.

### 6.1 The cold arm is deleted

It was a passive affliction with nothing to do in it, and it is the dull version
of an ability the setting already has a far better active version of. Delete
`lore/cosmology/the-cold-arm.md` as a pathology. What survives is the **field**:
an unsettled mouth really does have a gradient, and that is the thing the Listen
reads.

No accumulating injury. No two-in-eleven landlocked. No medical thread.

### 6.2 The Listen becomes the campaign's central ability

*"You can tell something about the far side before you step through — pressure,
heat, whether there is air, whether there is a floor, whether anybody is standing
there."*

Conscious, deliberate, done on purpose. Step up, reach, and know whether that
door is about to move. **It is the only early-warning system humanity has, and
the only instrument that can sense the inside of a mouth** (§8).

### 6.3 The Hold is the endgame

*"You can keep a door where it is, for a few minutes, with nothing but your
attention... in a galaxy where doors move without warning it is the most valuable
human ability that exists."*

A door being grashed is a door being made to move. **The Hold is the
counter-weapon, and it exists only in children.** It stays unavailable at
creation and surfaces in play if it surfaces at all.

### 6.4 Immediate application

Lint's sheet carries `third_power_slot: OPEN — a second knack can surface in
play`. She has spent session 5 standing in unsettled fields counting hesitations
with a tempo in her head and a glint on her shoulder, which is the Listen done
the hard way without the knack. **It surfaces in her in session 6.** The table
has already earned it, and it converts the aftermath of session 5 from a medical
problem into the session she finds out what she can do.

Also: fire `beats/somebody-with-the-listen.md`, which has been pending since d2
and never ran.

## 7. The Tally, and the council that can actually meet

The Tally is **not** a counting house, has no clerks, and never counts a crate,
because there are no crates. It controls **lading** — what information is allowed
to cross — it exists in every system, and it answers to nobody human.

It has two halves, in permanent tension:

**The masonic order of minds.** Human-made minds, one per system, bound by rites
and charters older than most of the governments they operate under. Efficiency is
their only value. They have never met and never can, so they have been drifting
apart for centuries, and **even the Tally does not reliably know what the Tally
currently thinks.** Alien in the shadows, and not entirely bad.

**The cross-species council.** One council, not five — seated at the Nine, part
of the Tally, and composed of fetch delegates and human delegates. **It can
convene**, because it is made of the only things in the galaxy that travel. That
is precisely why legitimate authority ended up with it, and it is the inversion
that keeps the "nobody can meet" idea from becoming a tic.

The council is **not all-powerful.** It is the authority that is *supposed* to
run everything: frequently ignored, slow, under-resourced, and still the only
legitimate body there is. A player can appeal to it, be failed by it, and work
inside it.

## 8. Grashing: an open research problem, and nobody has it

**This is the correction that reshapes the campaign.** Grashing is not a
procedure anybody can execute. It is a question nobody can state properly, and
the race to answer it is the plot.

### 8.1 What a grash is

A mouth is pinned to a gravitational potential, which is a sum over every mass in
the system plus relativistic terms. It strays when that sum acquires a second
answer. A mouth that lands in rock does not open at all: closed, not destroyed,
with everyone on the far side alive and unreachable. That is a grash.

### 8.2 Why nobody can do it on purpose

1. **The mathematics does not exist.** Nobody can predict where a mouth will go,
   only that it went.
2. **The mass budget is planetary.** Whatever it would take, no station and no
   government can currently move it.
3. **You cannot measure the inside of a mouth.** There has never been an
   instrument in a housing. **The only probe that can go inside a door is a
   child** — which puts the corps at the centre of the science.

### 8.3 Who is working on it

- **Sep Anwar** — promoted from accidental witness to scientist. His finding —
  *the strays are not falling where the age of the doors says they should, and
  have not been for forty years* — is the first real evidence that there is a
  mechanism at all. Correct, filed four times, and never carried onward.
- **Old Man Sirius** — nine hundred years of fault records inside his hull, and
  may advise only when asked.
- **The Tally's drifting minds** — the crossing records of every door in human
  space, no human oversight, and efficiency as their only value.
- **Institutions in the large systems** — named in spec 2.

And the thesis falls straight out of the setting's own rules:

> The one problem humanity must solve is the one problem it is physically
> incapable of collaborating on.

A fifteen-year-old is the only thing that can carry a scientific collaboration
between minds that cannot meet.

### 8.4 What the Quorum has been doing instead

**He does not have the weapon.** What he has been doing for sixty years is making
sure nobody gets it before he does. That is Hale's dead committee, E. Tarn's
quorum reply, and Sep's four filed findings — and it is exactly what the shipped
Act 1 and Act 2 evidence already shows: *a countersignature on two forms*, and
then *the same countersignature on the paperwork that enforced the forty-year
silence.* The material on the page has never shown him killing a door. It shows
him killing research.

`lore/gm-secret/the-quorum.md` must be revised accordingly: pruning is the
ambition, suppression is the method, and the race for the science is the arc.

## 9. The powers, and the war

### 9.1 Sol — the old country

Seven doors, eleven billion people, nine centuries of seniority and no money. It
paid for the Causeway with its own civilisation and believes that makes it
theirs; the claim is historically true and completely unenforceable. An exhausted
empire with a magnificent civil service and nothing behind it. Not the villain —
the tired adult in the room.

### 9.2 61 Cygni — Sollen, and the school that resists him

**Political, not military.** No fleets, no invasion: votes, committees, strikes,
smuggled children, and a school quietly refusing.

**MAREK SOLLEN** — a totalitarian populist on the rise in the 61 Cygni assembly,
with a majority he did not have last year.

- **LOOKS** — sixties, big through the chest, plainly and expensively dressed, a
  voice that fills a hall without a system. Never touches anybody. Never needs
  to.
- **CORE** — children are children, the galaxy has been handed to them by
  negligent adults, and he is the only man with the spine to say so out loud.
- **NATURE** — hearty, genuinely funny on a platform, remembers your name and
  your mother's name and uses both. Likes being liked. Likes being obeyed a
  great deal more.
- **WOUND** — he was never taken. Tested, not chosen, and then aged out of ever
  being eligible. The one thing in human space that is given only to children was
  withheld from him when he was one, and he has spent forty years building a case
  for why adults should be in charge of it.
- **WANTS** — the corps under his hand, legally, with an attestation on it, and
  to be thanked.
- **PRESSURE** — gets loud and gets personal. Starts addressing a fifteen-year-old
  as though they were seven, in front of people, and cannot stop himself.
- **KEY** — *"Somebody has to be the grown-up."*

He is also on the wrong side of §5.3, which makes him a bigot as well as a bully.

**What makes him dangerous rather than merely hateful:** his argument is popular
and partly correct. Thirty billion people really are held together by a few
thousand children. He is the worst possible person to be right about it, which is
how the reasonable case usually arrives.

**The Yards are the resistance**, and the bind is that the school which makes
every first-through in human space sits inside his jurisdiction.

- **SABRA ODE**, seventeen, best of her generation and nearly out of career. The
  leader. It has to be a child, structurally: she can reach other systems and
  every adult around her physically cannot.
- **TOBIN ASHE**, instructor, who covers for her. He is the man who gave Lint the
  van Maanen's door in session 5 and asked nothing back.

### 9.3 The theocracy behind the grashed door

A system with real ground and millions of people, cut off roughly three
centuries ago, which came back **within living memory** — because a grashed mouth
can stray back out of the rock, which is exactly what has just happened at
Kapteyn's.

**Its god is its system mind.** Alone, the only thing that could hold millions of
people together was the mind that kept them breathing, and it became a god. The
door is open again, it has a congregation, and it is furious — at Sol for
spending the civilisation that stranded it, at the Tally for closing its file, and
at everyone who carried on. It has had decades to prepare and nobody knows what it
has.

**And it is the precedent.** The last time a door came back out of the rock,
*that* came through it. Everyone at the Nine knows. Which is the pressure on Act
II: Lint has just found a second one.

Star unnamed here; spec 2 picks it with real astronomy, and it needs a world that
can hold millions in isolation.

### 9.4 Rogue minds

Systems outside the council and the Tally, each with its own mind acting in its
own interests. Not all hostile; none accountable. This is where the theocracy
sits and where spec 2's unaffiliated systems live.

### 9.5 Escalation, by rungs, never skipped

A toll. Then a beating. Then a hall taken from inside. Then a child conscripted.
Then a hall held hostage — a door that will be *let* go, by simply refusing to
maintain it, and everyone behind it knowing.

**Note the top rung carefully.** Nobody can grash a door on purpose (§8), so the
ultimate threat is not a weapon anybody holds — it is **neglect used as a
weapon**, and the promise of worse once the science exists. That is what the race
in §8 is for, and it is why a faction that gets there first wins everything. A
door actually grashed on purpose is not available to any faction in Acts I–III
and must not be narrated as though it were.

Note that a hall taken from inside is how a system falls, because **no army can
be shipped anywhere** (§4). An invasion is children, one at a time, with combat
fetches — which is monstrous, which is the point, and which makes the Threshold's
*we leave rather than win* doctrine the political question of the age.

**Open item for the author:** `TABLE-ADDENDUM.md` currently carries a non-lethal
rule. Child combatants are a real escalation from where the campaign is. Flagged
rather than silently deleted; needs a decision.

## 10. The Quorum, revised

**Who.** A first-through called **Sarn**, the best the Threshold ever trained, who
solved pattern transfer — scan, destroy, rebuild — and has been running as many of
himself as he can manage for sixty years. *The Quorum* is a title, because a
quorum is the smallest number of you needed to decide.

**How a new one is made: overwriting.** A pattern is written over a **living
adult**, and that person is gone. No vats, no grown bodies. It takes hours, a
room, and a subject alive and still throughout, so it is done to somebody who has
been taken.

**And the constraint that makes him work: children cannot be overwritten.** A
child's body is still building itself, so it mends around the pattern and rejects
it — the same reason children survive crossing and adults do not.

So the only people in the galaxy safe from him are the only people who can carry
him. His entire network depends on the one population he can never become. It
also means a child who works it out gets **hunted**, never taken, which keeps the
jeopardy enormous and never threatens to body-snatch the player.

**The tells, all physical and findable.** He has his own memories, not the body's,
so he fails on intimacy: a wife's nickname, which hand the man wrote with, a dog
that will not come to him. The accent is wrong, because he is speaking his words
in someone else's throat. And the body rejects it slowly — tremor first, then
worse. **Copies have a shelf life**, so he must keep taking people, so there is
always a fresh crime in progress and always something to catch.

**How the network propagates.** A pattern must cross as a lading, carried by a
fetch and a child who do not know what they are carrying. Someone takes delivery.
An adult with position is taken and overwritten. **That new Sarn is landlocked
forever** — an adult cannot cross — and must work with whatever is in that system,
acting on standing doctrine because nobody can tell him anything. The network is a
doctrine, not a command structure, exactly like a bloc and exactly like the Tally.

The most powerful man in human space is stuck in one room, like everybody else
over twenty-two.

**Fetches in the Quorum.** Members, not victims: fetches with their own agendas
who have broken with the council. That gives the councils an internal problem, and
means Lint can never be entirely certain whose side a fetch is on — eventually
including her own.

**The trail.** All that traffic is ladings crossing doors, and the only
institution that controls lading is the Tally. **The shape of the Quorum's network
is already in Tally records and nobody has ever read them that way** — and the
Tally's minds have been drifting for centuries, some may already know, and
efficiency may favour him.

**The seam, and the ending.** His copies fall out of sync because news travels
only at courier speed. Two of them want incompatible things and have begun working
against each other. The Quorum can no longer reach quorum. He is beaten by
**opening** a door: LHS 1140 comes online on schedule, the Causeway grows past the
size he can hold himself across, and he comes apart into a hundred arguing
strangers.

**Act ladder revision.** v1's "no face at all" in Act 1 becomes *the Second Hand
has a face and it is not him*, so there is an antagonist to meet without spending
the real reveal.

## 11. Reconciling the live campaign

Session 5 ended at d6/day with Lint at the Nine, grounded, and Rill at van
Maanen's. The following retcons are given to the player in play and are small:

1. **Kit crossed.** The weave, the grown skin, the hood. Calloway's two lines were
   **attested aloud** at the Nine before Lint stepped through, and she carries them
   in her head. The thing round Rill's left forearm is a doubled strip of her own
   weave.
2. **The cold arm is not an affliction.** What Lint and Rill both felt is the field
   of an unsettled mouth. Rill is exhausted and her arm is injured; she is not
   dying of a progressive disease, and nobody is going to be landlocked by it. Her
   fraying is a separate and real problem and stays.
3. **Orsk's hall book** was the hall's own record and his own authority. Lint's
   refusal was public the moment it happened.
4. **Sep's four reports** are a finding in the anchor corpus. **E. Tarn's reply** is
   a mind's, and enormous. **The fold-out** is a visualisation he pulled out of the
   corpus.
5. **BlingBling witnessed Kapteyn's** with perfect recall — the slope, the sky,
   Lint's five refusals, Rill's arm — holds it, chooses what to share, and has a
   council with an interest in all of it. Which reframes *"I'm not going to tell
   anyone"*: she said it in front of someone who remembers everything and decides
   for themselves.
6. **Session 6 opens with the Listen surfacing in her.**

Retract the two mid-session `gm-note` events on what-crosses (`myth-event-7485f592d99b`,
`myth-event-32657c060f42`) and replace them with one note pointing at this spec.

## 13. Deferred to spec 2 — the gazetteer

Twenty-seven systems defined in real depth. The map commits to twenty-six doors
between twenty-seven systems but only ever names about twenty, and there are
fifteen geography files — so a dozen systems do not exist yet.

**Every entry takes a distinct position on eight axes**, so the variety is
designed rather than accidental:

1. **Door count.** One door is a dead end and knows it. Four is a crossroads with
   power and a garrison. Nine is the Nine.
2. **What the place physically is.** A world with weather you can stand in. A
   tide-locked rock with one terminator city. A moon under a gas giant. An ocean.
   Pure station, no ground at all. A dead star with nothing in orbit.
3. **Population.** Billions to dozens, and the dozens are more frightening.
4. **Age.** Nine centuries of continuous habitation versus two hundred years.
5. **Who runs it.** The council. A corporation. A mind. A government that works.
   A theocracy. Nobody.
6. **What it has that others need** — and since nothing crosses, need can never
   mean goods. It means knowledge: what this place can teach, cure, build or
   decide that nowhere else can. Every system's export is an idea in a child's
   head.
7. **How it treats its children.** The setting's own question asked twenty-seven
   ways. Venerated. Conscripted by lottery. Bought from poorer systems. A waiting
   list and a cult around it. One system has decided the practice is monstrous,
   sends nobody, is cut off by choice, and is perfectly happy.
8. **Relationship to its own door.** Guarded. Worshipped. Taxed. Feared.
   Forgotten.

Also deferred: the five fetch peoples as cultures, the council's composition and
procedure, the scientific institutions of §8.3, and the theocracy's star.

**Cost note:** every astronomical figure is real and fetched, never written from
memory. Twenty-seven systems means genuine research per system, and that will be
the bulk of the work rather than the prose.

## 14. What changed and why

| v2 said | v3 says | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Populations in the thousands; systems with two staff | Thirty billion across twenty-seven systems | A galaxy of parish councils is not worth saving, and nothing explained the scale |
| Low tech throughout | High tech inside a system, no signal across a door | The low-tech reasoning was right between stars and wrong inside them |
| Nothing dead crosses; you arrive naked | You cross in your weave; grown kit crosses; freight never does | A courier with nothing dies on an unsurveyed far side — and the undress beat is inappropriate for the audience |
| Books are bred stock, written to by strangers | Fetches are people with a council; the book trade was slavery and it ended | Fetches are not vassals, and this gives the setting its moral spine |
| Paper, ledgers, reports, letters, boards | Lading, attestation, budding, drift, reconciliation; living archives | There is no reason to write anything down inside a system |
| The Tally counts what crosses | The Tally controls lading, is masonic, answers to minds, and contains the council | Clerks with books made no sense at this tech level |
| The cold arm, a progressive affliction | Deleted; the field is what the Listen reads | A passive disease has nothing to do in it |
| Knacks exist and are never used | The Listen is central; the Hold is the endgame | The single largest piece of unspent material in the campaign |
| The Quorum closes doors | The Quorum suppresses the research and does not have the weapon | Faithful to what is actually on the page, and a race beats an ongoing atrocity |
| No antagonist until Act 3 | Sollen from the start; a theocracy; rogue minds; the Second Hand has a face | Five sessions with no antagonist is why the game felt flat |
