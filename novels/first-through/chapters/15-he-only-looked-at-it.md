# Chapter 15 — He Only Looked At It

*Day three · the hall*

"You're not in trouble."

He took that about as well as a man can who has just worked out that his private sheet is the only record of something.

And she did not say the other half out loud, which was *we all are*.

---

Bunkroom six, third level. Nine bunks, dark, the specific fug of eight people on different shifts. She found him by the boots — a Cold Doors pair and a Procyon pair, which the Old Man had told her to look for.

Dray came awake badly, the way anybody does off a late, and he was seventeen and narrow and had the half-second blank stare of somebody who thought they had three more hours.

"...Who're you?"

"Lint. New one. Garden." She kept her voice down for the other seven. "You've got the Listen."

He went from asleep to completely still in about a quarter of a second, which tells you something about having the Listen.

"Who told you that?"

"The Old Man. I asked him."

"...Right." He sat up. "He does that."

So she gave it to him fast and in order, and she gave him the *evidence* rather than the worry, because that was the only thing she had that was worth anything. Pell Ivor's sheet: forty-one step-ups, twenty-three through, since the watch turned, one for one every week of the last eleven years until Friday. And five timings off the deck camera: two point one, two point four, two point two, two point six, two point four seconds, standing in a lit housing before it takes you.

Dray listened to all of it without once interrupting.

Then he said: "Sticky's half a second. Sticky's *always* half a second. That's what sticky is."

"Yeah."

"Two point six isn't sticky."

"No."

He was already reaching for his boots.

He did not ask whether he would get in trouble, or who sent her, or whether Calloway knew. What he asked, pulling the second boot on, was the professional question.

"Has anybody listened at it?"

"I don't think anybody's thought of it. It's a near-side problem and the Listen's a far-side knack, so—"

"So it won't tell you why it's sticky." He stood up. "It'll tell you if the far side's moved."

He stopped in the doorway with his weave over his arm, and for the first time he looked properly awake, and properly young, and slightly frightened.

"Has anybody checked that? In three days of it being sticky, has one person checked whether that door still goes where it's supposed to go?"

"Course not. They're just banging away at it to get it to open."

Dray closed his eyes for a second.

"...Yeah. That's what I'd be doing." He got his weave on over his head. "That's what I *have* been doing, on four. Four's been sticky twice this year."

---

He moved fast and he didn't talk, and halfway down the stair he said one thing without looking round.

"If it's moved, they'd know. Twenty-three went through this watch and nobody's come back screaming. People are arriving at the refinery and going about their day."

"So it hasn't."

"So it hasn't." A beat. "So what am I listening *for*."

He didn't have an answer and neither did she, and he went anyway.

Pell Ivor gave him the gap without being asked twice. A tallyman who has been privately counting something for five days does not need persuading that somebody should look at it.

The queue did not like it. There was a noise.

And then Dray stepped up to the ring — not onto the plate, not giving it his weight. Just close. One hand out, not touching anything, the way you'd hold a hand near a stove.

And he stood there with his eyes open and absolutely nothing in his face.

The hall did the thing the hall does when thirty people stop talking.

It took about four seconds and then it went wrong.

She didn't see what happened, because nothing happened. He was standing with his hand out and then his hand wasn't out, it was at the side of his head, and then his knees were gone and he was on the deck plate.

Ossuary was there before she was. The queue surged forward and the Wolf Gate steward turned round and started shouting at thirty people to get back, and they did, mostly.

---

Dray was conscious. That was the first thing. Conscious, on his back, both hands over his eyes with the heels of his palms pressed in hard, and there was blood coming out of his nose and down his jaw in a way that was not dramatic and was extremely wrong.

"— alright, lad, you're alright, don't sit up—"

"*It's two.*"

He said it to the ceiling, through his hands.

"What?"

"It's — there's *two*." His breath had gone short. "There's two of them. It's both. It's both at — it's two places and they're —"

And then he couldn't. You could watch him try and you could watch it not come, and he made a sound that was a seventeen-year-old being properly frightened in front of thirty people.

"I don't — I've done four hundred of these. I've never — it's **both**. It's both at once and I can't—"

The hall doctor arrived at a run from the second level with a bag.

Pell Ivor had his board down by his side, forgotten. The queue was dead silent and backed up four metres further than the cordon rope.

Somebody behind her said, quietly, "He only looked at it."

---

And over her pin, flat and fast and stripped right down:

*Lin. I have all of that on camera and audio and I have logged it verbatim at the time, which means in about nine minutes it will be the only account anybody has.*

*And I need to say the unwelcome thing now rather than later, because somebody is going to repeat 'it's two' in a committee by this evening.*

*He was hurt getting it. That makes it the least reliable testimony in this hall. A frightened seventeen-year-old with a nosebleed said a thing he could not finish and could not explain, and I cannot tell you whether he perceived something real or whether his knack simply failed badly and the words are what failure sounds like.*

*I do not know. Neither does he. And neither does anybody who repeats it.*

A beat.

*He is going to be all right. The doctor is not worried, she is being thorough. You should go and stand where he can see you, because you are the person who woke him up.*

---

"I'm so sorry. It's my fault."

The doctor had a pad against his nose and his head turned and was telling him in a flat professional voice to stop talking and breathe through his mouth, and he did not stop talking.

"'S not." It came out thick. "Not your fault."

"I woke you up—"

"You gave me a *sheet*." He got one hand off his eyes and squinted up at her, and there was blood on his teeth, which was the most frightening thing she had seen all day. "Forty-one for twenty-three. Two point six. I've been on the rota four years. I heard those numbers and I *knew*."

The doctor: "Dray. Mouth."

"— and then I asked whether anybody'd checked, and nobody had, and I'm the one who *can*." He let his head go back. "So I did. That's the job. That's the whole job."

A pause while he breathed.

"Done four hundred crossings. Never once used it for anything that mattered." Quieter. "Wasn't going to be the first time I didn't."

The doctor got him sitting up against Ossuary's knee and shone something in both eyes and was visibly less worried than she had been ninety seconds earlier, and said so out loud, deliberately, for the benefit of thirty people.

"He's all right. Nose and a headache. He'll have a bad evening."

The hall let out a breath you could hear.

---

And then it changed, and you could feel the moment it did.

Because thirty people had just watched a kid go down on the plate.

Nobody logs a sticky door. Everybody logs a first-through on the deck.

The Wolf Gate steward had stopped managing the queue and was talking to the tallyman, fast. Pell Ivor had his board back up and was holding it like a document now rather than a habit. Somebody two levels up on the gallery was leaning over the rail shouting a question at somebody else.

And the queue — thirty-odd people who had spent three days being annoyed about standing on a plate — had gone very quiet and had stopped moving forward.

A Coldwater man with a crate said, to nobody in particular: "I'm not going through that."

---

*Calloway has it,* said her pin. *She is on the stair now and she will be here in about forty seconds. And Lin — thirty people heard him say 'it's two' and nine of them are already repeating it.*

"Yeah, well. I trust him. It's two — somehow the crossing got split."

Dray, from the deck, with a pad against his nose: "...*Thank* you."

The doctor: "Mouth."

*That is a conclusion,* said Sirius, *and I am not going to tell you whether it is right. But I have a fact that bears on it directly and you should have it before Calloway gets here.*

*Nobody has arrived anywhere unexpected.*

*Inbound traffic from Gliese 65 has run all watch. Four this morning. Every one of them came up out of seven and every one of them had come from the refinery corridor — the same hardened run, the same shelter behind it, Vey's board on the bulkhead. It is on the Standing, confirmed this watch, in Sennet's hand, because that is what the Standing is for.*

*So whatever 'two' means, it does not mean that people are coming out in two places. That rules something out. It does not explain anything.*

---

Calloway came off the bottom of the service stair at a pace that was not a run and was not far off one, with Dunnel behind her.

She took in the whole thing in about two seconds. Dray on the deck against Ossuary's knee with a pad on his face. The doctor. The queue backed up four metres past the rope and nobody moving. Thirty people all looking at her.

And a fifteen-year-old standing in the middle of it who was off the rota.

She did not say *Lin* this time.

"**Doctor.** Is he hurt?"

"Nose and a headache. He'll have a bad evening. I'd like him off this deck."

"Take him. Ossuary, go with her." She didn't watch them go. "Pell — hold that queue and nobody steps up until I say so, and I want that board in my hand in ten minutes."

"Director." He was visibly relieved to be given an order.

Then she turned round, and the whole hall was listening, and she knew it.

"Right. Lin. Walk me through it from the beginning, and don't tidy it up."

---

It came out in order, and her hands were not entirely steady and her voice mostly was.

The loaders yesterday, and *seven's sticky every spring*, and that she had taken that as reassurance. Pell Ivor's board this morning. The five timings off the deck camera. That she asked the Old Man for somebody with the Listen, and that he told her first that it reads the far side and not the near, and would not tell her why seven is sticky. That she went to bunkroom six and woke Dray off a late. That she gave him the sheet and the numbers and nothing else.

That *his* question was the one nobody had asked: in three days of it being sticky, has one person checked whether that door still goes where it is supposed to go.

Then precisely what happened. He did not step onto the plate. He did not give it his weight. He stood at the ring with one hand out, not touching anything, the way you'd hold a hand near a stove. Four seconds, maybe five. His eyes were open the whole time and there was nothing in his face. Then his hand went to the side of his head and his knees went. He was conscious on the deck and he said *it's two* to the ceiling, then *there's two of them*, then *it's both*, then *it's two places and they're* — and he could not finish it, and he tried, and it did not come. The blood came from his nose about ten seconds after he went down.

And then she gave her the inconvenient half, before she asked for it, which was the only part of it she had deliberately decided to do.

That Sirius logged all of it verbatim and then said that Dray was hurt getting it, which makes it the least reliable account in the hall — a frightened seventeen-year-old said a thing he couldn't finish and couldn't explain, and nobody can tell whether he perceived something real or whether that is simply what a knack sounds like when it fails badly.

And that nine people within earshot were already repeating *it's two*.

Calloway listened to every word of it without moving. Behind her, Dunnel was writing.

When she finished, she was quiet for about three seconds, and then she asked exactly the question Lint had known she was going to ask.

"Who authorised a first-through to put his knack on a housing that is behaving abnormally?"

Nobody in the hall said anything.

"Lin. Look at me. **Who authorised it?**"

"No one. I thought someone should check it rather than just trying to jump through."

Calloway took that without blinking.

"Right. Then here's both halves, and you're getting them in front of thirty people because thirty people are listening and they're entitled to hear what the Threshold thinks."

---

"**The first half.** You are off the rota, you had no standing, and you went to a bunkroom and woke a seventeen-year-old and put him in front of a housing that is doing something nobody understands. He is on his way to the second level with a pad on his face."

She did not raise her voice at all, which was worse.

"You didn't hurt him. You gave him a sheet and he made his own call, and he's four years on the rota and entitled to. But nobody who could weigh that risk *weighed* it, and the reason is that you didn't come and get me. Twelve minutes, Lin. I was two decks up."

"...Yeah."

"Say it back."

"I should have come and got you."

"You should have come and got me." She nodded once. "That's the whole of it and I'm not going to say it again."

A pause.

"**The second half**, and it's bigger.

"You were *right*."

She turned round so the hall got it properly.

"Three days that door's been sticky. Three days of first-throughs standing on a plate for two and a half seconds and coming off and going again, and four hundred people on this station calling it weather — **including me.** I've walked past that queue twice today.

"Nobody checked. Nobody asked the one question a Listen could answer. And the reason nobody did is that a sticky door isn't an incident, so nobody logs it, so it never arrives on my desk, so I never weigh it."

A beat.

"That's not a fifteen-year-old's failure. That's mine, and it's Dunnel's, and it's every tallyman on this deck's, and Pell Ivor's been privately counting since *Friday* because he knew and had nowhere to put it."

Pell Ivor looked at the floor.

---

Then she stopped being gentle about any of it and started being a director, fast.

"**Dunnel.** Seven is closed. Cordon at four metres, nobody steps up, and I want that logged as an incident this minute with Dray's name on it and the time."

"Director."

"**Pell** — board. Thank you. Go and sit down somewhere and don't let anybody take that sheet off you but me.

"**Mert** —" the Wolf Gate steward "— your queue goes home and you tell them the truth, which is that I don't know what it's doing and I'm not sending anybody into it until I do. They won't like it. Tell them anyway."

"Twelve thousand people on the other side of that, Director."

"I know exactly how many people are on the other side of that."

And then she turned back to Lint, and the hall had got very quiet, and she asked it like it was the next item on a list.

"Right. You've been ahead of me all morning. **What haven't I got yet?**"
