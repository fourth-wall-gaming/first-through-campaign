# Chapter 19 — Don't Brace

*Day three · the hall, thirteen hours and forty*

The hall filled in nine minutes.

Four hundred and six first-throughs is a great many people for one room, and they came all at once — the Wolf Gate kids first because it's their door, then Coldwater, then everybody, down the service stairs and over the gallery rails. Ferren arrived with his boots in his hand. Sen was already on her stool with the board live and a fresh column ruled.

Calloway didn't make a speech. She gave them the five facts in ninety seconds and then started assigning.

And she put Lint on the thing nobody else could do.

"You've crossed seven twice today and you're the only one who has. Every single one of them is going to step onto a plate that fights them and most of them have never felt it. **Teach them to ease.** You've got until it moves."

Four hundred faces.

She is a terrible public speaker, and it turned out not to matter, because she did not speak to four hundred people. She spoke to eleven, and told them to go and tell eleven each, which was the only thing she could think of.

What she gave them was the whole of what she knew and it took about forty seconds.

*It goes solid. Don't brace. Everybody braces and it's worse. Take the weight right off, let it come back on slow, and it has you.*

That's it. That's all of it. A loader on a stairwell gave her half of it yesterday and an eighty-one-year-old woman gave her the other half that morning.

It went round the hall in four minutes and came back changed — by the time it reached the far gallery somebody had turned it into a rhythm, three words, and two hundred people were saying it at once like a counting song.

**Don't brace. Take it off. Let it come.**

---

Ferren shouldered through to her with his boots still in his hand.

"Lin. The shed." He was out of breath. "Vey's lot can dig it out, right? But nobody's *stood in it* for two hundred years. What if the apron's gone, or the ring's seized, or—"

Rill, behind him, arms folded:

"Then the first one through finds out."

"Yeah. That's the job."

Rill didn't answer, but she moved to stand next to her rather than behind Ferren, which was new.

Thirteen hours and forty minutes.

Down in the ring, Dunnel taped a sheet to the deck plate and wrote **2.9** on it in pen, because the board was four levels up and nobody wanted to look away.

---

"What do you want to do?" said Rill, beside her, not looking at her.

"I want to be the one to go. Of course. What about you?"

Rill took a while.

"Me."

One word, and then the rest of it in one go, flat, like reading a number off a sheet.

"Because I've got a year and you've got twenty, and the first one into that shed finds out whether the apron's cracked or the ring's seized or there's rock sitting in it, and if it's rock then whoever's standing in it when they find out doesn't come back. That's not brave, Lin. It's just what the numbers are."

She finally looked at her.

"And everybody in this hall is about to spend a day being noble at each other about it, so I'd rather get in first and be boring."

Then, immediately, before Lint could say anything:

"Your glint's gone up to the rail with mine. Tace is teaching her something and I have never once seen Tace teach anybody anything."

"I just love glints. Just the best." And then, because it needed saying: "Look, Rill, you're just amazing. I'll back you up whatever. The main thing is to save those people. Everyone's coming back, yeah?"

"Mm," said Rill, which is not yes.

She didn't lie. She also didn't say the other thing.

What she did was knock her shoulder into Lint's, once, hard enough to shift her, and then look up at the handrail where Tace-of-the-Rail was doing something slow and precise and BlingBling was — for possibly the first time in her life — watching rather than performing.

"She's learning to hold an interval," Rill said. "That's not a glint thing. That's a *working* thing. Tace only teaches it to one in ten."

A pause.

"You've been here four days."

---

Over at the cordon, Calloway raised her voice: "**Who's first into the shed?**"

And about ninety hands went up.

Rill did not put hers up.

She walked — straight through the middle of ninety raised hands, to the cordon rope, in front of Calloway.

And she pushed her sleeves up.

Four years of nobody saying it out loud, and she put both forearms on the rope in the light where the whole hall could see them, and said nothing at all.

Calloway looked at them for a long moment.

Then she said, quietly enough that only the front two rows heard it: "Put your sleeves down, Rill."

And louder, to the hall: "**Noted. Everybody who raised a hand, Dunnel's taking names.**"

Rill came back with her sleeves still up.

---

Dunnel had a sheet on the bench and was taking names in two columns, which Lint only understood when she got close: one for *has crossed seven*, one for *hasn't*.

Hers went in the first column. There were four names in it.

"I know." Dunnel didn't look up. "You were the second one in today and Ferren was the first. Dray's in there too and he's not crossing anything." She wrote. "That's three."

"Who's the fourth?"

"Rill. Four years ago."

Behind her, somewhere up the gallery, the counting song was still going.

---

Then a shout from the ring.

One of the Wolf Gate couriers had been on the plate for a count of about twelve and came off it shaking her hands out, and she stepped up again, and came off again.

"— it's not *having* me—"

Dunnel's pen stopped.

Second courier stepped up. Same. Third.

Three in a row and nobody through, and the sheet on the deck plate said 2.9, and Koth from the Coldwater run was already saying out loud the thing everybody in the hall had just worked out at the same time.

"If it won't take anybody *now*, there's no relay. We're just waiting thirteen hours for it to go."

Calloway, flat and fast: "Somebody who's crossed it today. On the plate. **Now.**"

Ferren was on an early and asleep. Dray was on the second level.

There were two of them left in that column and Rill was thirty metres away at the cordon.

"Rill, you coming?"

She was beside her before Lint had got to the plate.

"Second shot's mine."

Ossuary cleared the ring. Nobody said anything. BlingBling locked on.

It set, and kept setting. Three goes, easing clean every time, and the fourth. Nothing.

Rill's hand was on her shoulder to move her off the plate.

Fifth attempt. She took the weight right off, let it come back slow — and it had her.

Hot metal. The corridor. Shift workers going the other way with bedding, because Vey was already moving twelve thousand people.

She was back on the Nine inside forty seconds.

"**It takes.**" She was saying it before her foot was off the plate. "Four refusals and the fifth one went. It's not shut — it's just *slow* and you have to keep going."

The hall made a noise.

Dunnel was writing. Calloway wasn't.

"Five attempts," she said. "How long did the five take?"

"...Ninety seconds? Maybe a bit over."

"Ninety seconds a crossing, sixty-one people." She was doing it out loud. "That's an hour and a half if every single one goes fifth time, and nothing else goes wrong, and the number doesn't rise."

Rill, beside her, sleeves still up: "It'll rise."
