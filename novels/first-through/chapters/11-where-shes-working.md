# Chapter 11 — Where She's Working

*Day two · port side, fourth level*

"OK. That's enough of the history. Give me a copy of the Low Stair. I'll read it somewhere."

The store had a stack of blank sheets by the door, because of course it did — monomer, thin as a leaf, and you touch one to the shelf index and it takes the text and holds it until you wipe it.

Sen did it for her. She did not ask whether Lint wanted her to.

"Right. So." She was extremely businesslike and she was also not quite managing it. "Don't read the last chapter first."

"I wasn't going to—"

"Everybody says that." She held the sheet a moment longer before she handed it over. "And don't skip the bit in the middle where nothing happens for forty pages and she's just counting people in the queue. That's the *best bit*. Everybody skips it."

"Why's it the best bit?"

Sen looked at her like she'd asked why water's wet.

"Because that's where she's *working*."

Then she gave her the sheet, and turned round, and squared up three things on a shelf that did not need squaring up, and said to the shelf:

"It's not very good at the start. Just so you know. It gets good."

And then she was off out of the store at speed with her pen behind her ear, and you could hear her going down the corridor at about twice the pace she came up it.

*She has recommended that to eleven people,* said Sirius, pin only, in the specific flat voice he uses when he is telling you a fact and declining to tell you what to do about it.

*You are the first one who took it.*

---

"I like Sen," Lint said. "She seems like she'd be a good friend."

From the ledge above her head, without opening — well. Without doing whatever it is she does instead of opening her eyes.

::: glint
She is.
:::

A pause, and then a slow amber that meant she was awake after all and had been for a while.

::: glint
She reads flash.
:::

"...She what?"

::: glint
Not well. About as well as you read a Coldwater manifest — slowly, and she gets the tenses wrong, and she cannot do colour at all so everything anybody says to her comes out flat. But she reads it. She is the only human on this station who does.

She sits on a stool at the busiest wall on the Nine, all day, every second and third watch, and every first-through who passes that board has one of us on their collarbone, and we talk constantly, and we have done it over her head since she was about six.

So she learned. On her own. Out of watching.

Tace-of-the-Rail says she started signing back when she was nine and got it wrong for two years and nobody corrected her because nobody realised she was doing it on purpose.
:::

A longer pause. BlingBling dimmed down a tone, which is her being careful.

::: glint
She cannot cross, Lin. She will never go anywhere. And she has taught herself the only language on this station that belongs to people who go everywhere, and she keeps the board that says where all of it currently is, and she is eleven.

So — yes. I should think she would be an extremely good friend.
:::

A flicker of gold.

::: glint
She has also never once told me to stop, and I have been showing off at her for three days.
:::

---

She had a spot. She'd found it on her grounded day without knowing she was looking for one.

Fourth level, port side, past where the Coldwater offices stop being offices — a maintenance gallery about thirty metres long that smells of grease and has a run of ports down one side that nobody has ever bothered to shutter, because nobody comes there and the view is in the wrong direction for anybody to care.

The wrong direction being: away from Sirius.

So there was no glare. Just the black, and four thousand points of it, drifting slowly with the spin.

She sat on a cable trunk with her back against the wall and the sheet on her knees, and before she read a word of it she did the thing she had done every time she had been in that corridor, which was three times now.

She found **Sol**.

It isn't hard once somebody has shown you. It's about as bright as Polaris was from the valley — a clear, unremarkable, perfectly ordinary star, in a part of the sky her grandmother would have called Hercules, which from there is bent slightly out of shape in a way that took her a while to work out and then annoyed her for a week.

Everything is nearly right and nothing is quite right. The Plough is there and has a star too many. Orion is behind you and inside out.

Eight point six light years. The light coming off that point now left when she was six, which means somewhere in it is the summer she fell off the wall, and her mother is in it, and the library in the next valley with the dust and the feet.

BlingBling came off her collar and sat on the trunk beside her hand, and did not get between her and the port, which she has never once done in that corridor.

---

Then she read.

And Sen was right: it isn't very good at the start. Forty pages of a girl called Oress at Alpha Centauri in the four hundreds, who failed her test at nine and works the queue at the Low Stair handing out numbered tokens, and the writing is flat and nothing happens and twice Lint nearly put it down.

And then somewhere around page sixty she starts counting.

Not because anybody asks her to. Because she is bored and she is standing there anyway, and she begins writing down who comes through and when — and she keeps doing it for eleven years, in the margins of a token book, while the whole of her life goes past the top of the stair.

It got good about forty pages after Sen said it would.

She was a hundred and ten pages in, and the spin had moved Sol about a hand's width across the port, when her pin went.

*Second watch in eleven minutes. The fathom has been in the gallery void since the first and is pretending it has not been.*
