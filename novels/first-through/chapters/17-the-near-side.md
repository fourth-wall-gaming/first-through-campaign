# Chapter 17 — The Near Side

*Day three · the hall, then the office*

Calloway finally straightened up.

"Right. Lin — go and find out which of my four hundred have the Listen, and do not let one of them near that ring. Names on a sheet, that's all."

She turned to Dunnel. "And get me Reader Haral. She's in this hall somewhere and she's been standing at the back of it for ten minutes."

---

*Fourteen.*

He had it before she had finished asking.

*Out of four hundred and six first-throughs currently at the Nine. Two or three per intake, as the Yards tells you. I will give you them in the order they are useful rather than alphabetically.*

She got a sheet off the bench and wrote while he talked, which was faster than it sounds because he paced himself to her hand without being asked.

*Dray Enns, seventeen, Cold Doors and Procyon. On the second level with the doctor and not to be asked anything by anybody for the rest of the day.*

*Then the three who have actually worked seven and know what it feels like on a good day — Marn Sethe, twenty; Obbie Laurel, nineteen; Ciss, sixteen, who goes by Ciss and will correct you.*

*Then eight who have never worked it: Teal, Harran Voss, Nim, Edda Bray, two Castels who are cousins and will both answer to Castel, Pel Orro, and Juun.*

*And two I am going to name separately. Ferren Dask has a trace of it — not a knack, a trace, enough that he can sometimes tell whether there is air and nothing else, and he has never told anybody because it is embarrassing to be nearly good at something. And Rill has it properly and has not used it in four years.*

"...Rill?"

*Rill has it properly.* And he did not elaborate, which was him declining to hand her somebody else's business.

Fourteen names, in order, with the three who know seven at the top. It took ninety seconds.

She put it in Calloway's hand.

She read it, and her eyebrows went up once, at the bottom of the page.

"Right. Good. That's quick." She folded it. "And nobody goes near that ring."

---

"Call — I'm not off the rota."

She looked up.

"It's day three. You said three days on the first night. And you left the Procyon run on for me this morning and I did it, and I carried a reader eight light years before breakfast, which means I've already worked today with your say-so."

Dunnel made a very small noise and turned it into a cough.

Calloway looked at her for a moment, and then did the thing that makes her worth working for, which is concede the point immediately and completely.

"That's correct. You're on the rota as of this morning and the Procyon run was mine to give, and I gave it. Fair."

Then, without a pause:

"**And you're still not going near that ring**, and I want you to notice that I've just changed my reason rather than pretending I hadn't.

"It's not the grounding. It's that you pushed after a set four days ago, that housing is doing something with no precedent in eight hundred years, and you are the single most useful person in this hall precisely because you keep walking into the thing in front of you."

She put the folded list in her breast pocket.

"So. You're on the rota. Where I put you."

"I go where you tell me to go. Send me."

Calloway's face did something very slightly.

"...Right. Good." She didn't make anything of it, which is how you know she noticed. "Then here's where I'm sending you, and it isn't a consolation job.

"Four hundred and six first-throughs on this station. Every one of them has been standing on plates all week. Seven's been sticky five days and *nobody told me*, and the reason isn't that they're stupid, it's that there was nowhere to put it.

"You've been here four days and you've got two things out of a stairwell and one off a tallyman's private sheet that I didn't have. **You ask people things and they tell you**, and nobody on my staff has done that all week because we were all busy being busy."

She took a step closer so the hall didn't get this bit.

"So go and collect it. All of it. Which doors have been sticky, when, how long, how bad. Not just seven — *all nine*. I want to know whether this is one housing or whether I've got a station-wide problem and have been reading nine separate inconveniences."

"...Nine."

"Nine." She held her eye. "Because if four's been sticky too and nobody's mentioned that either, then everything Sirius has just told this hall about one refinery's orbit is the wrong shape, and I'd very much rather find that out from you in an hour than from a committee in a month."

And then, loudly enough for the gallery, deliberately: "And you've got **standing** for it, as of now. Anybody asks who sent you, it's me. Anybody declines to answer a courier off the rota, they can decline to answer the Director instead. Dunnel — note that."

"Noted."

"Take whoever you need. Not Dray."

---

And then the Old Man said it on her pin, in the flat voice, while she was still three steps from the cordon.

*The fathom in the gallery void does not see. It feels what the air in a room is doing — that is how it knew about the column. It is the only creature on this station that reads a room by its field rather than by looking at it.*

*Every knack you have been told about this morning reads the far side. That one reads the near.*

So she said it out loud, because somebody had to and she was the one standing there.

"Call. Vantage. The fathom in the void — it reads fields. Near side. Not the far side. The *near* side, where we're all standing."

Calloway went very still.

"Say that again."

Lint said it again.

Dunnel's head came up off her pad. "Director — the fathoms have been in that void since the Cold Doors opened."

"I know how long they've been in the void, Dunnel." Flat. Not at Dunnel. "Right. **Get it.** Ask it properly, it's a citizen, and if it says yes it comes to me and not to that ring."

She was already turning to the queue.

"Then the nine doors. Go."

---

Vantage was where it always is, and it came down before she had got to the rail, which it had not done before.

::: fathom
Something has happened in the hall. Four levels have been quiet for an hour and I can feel the queue not moving from up here.
:::

So she told it. Dray at the ring and what he said and the blood. Pell's board, five days, forty-one for twenty-three. Seven hundred and ninety-four shallow dips and thirty deep ones and twenty-six and a half years, and that this is the thirty-first and it is at two point six on day five against a previous record of one point one.

And then the ask — properly, because it is a citizen, and she said so out loud in that order.

Vantage did not answer for a long moment.

::: fathom
Say the last part again. The part about what I would be for.
:::

"Reading the near side. The housing. Not through it — *it*. What the field in that ring is actually doing, now, while it's lit. Every human knack reads the far side. Dray put his hand near a door that's got two answers and it knocked him down. You read a room by what the air in it's doing. That's not the same sense and it might not be the same risk."

::: fathom
It is certainly not the same sense.
:::

A pause.

::: fathom
It might very well be the same risk, and I would be guessing, and I am not going to tell your director that I know it is safe.
:::

It drifted lower.

::: fathom
But I will tell you what I think it is, and then I will come, and she can decide.
:::

::: fathom
Dray reached **through**. That is what the Listen is — a sense that goes to the far side, and it went to a far side that is currently two things. I would not be reaching through anything. A field is here. It is in the metal and the air of that ring whether anybody is crossing or not, and I have been reading fields in this void for six years without putting my hand in anything.
:::

A beat.

::: fathom
I think it is more like standing next to a fire than reaching into one. I think that, and I do not know it, and the difference matters.
:::

---

::: fathom
And Lin — understand what you are asking, because you are fifteen and polite and you have not said it.
:::

::: fathom
If I can read that housing, then every fathom in human space can read every housing in human space, and has been able to for nine hundred years. There are eleven of us at the Nine. There are thousands across the Causeway. We have been riding on children's collarbones through doors we could have been reading the whole time.
:::

::: fathom
That is not a small thing to hand to the Threshold. It makes my people useful in a way we have not been, which will be good, and it will also mean somebody will want us at every ring on every station, and nobody will ask us twice after the first time.
:::

It rose slightly.

::: fathom
I am going to say yes anyway. I want it on the record that I saw that coming.
:::

::: fathom
Take me to her. And I would like Hesk present, because he will have thought about the field in those housings for nineteen years and will be the only human in the room who can tell her whether I am talking nonsense.
:::

---

Finding Hesk took eleven minutes and involved Sen, who knew exactly which trunking run he was in and did not ask why she wanted him.

He came up out of a deck hatch on the third level with a headlamp on and a wrench in his teeth and the expression of a man whose morning was going fine.

"...You again."

"Calloway wants you."

He took the wrench out of his mouth.

"*Calloway* wants me."

"And a fathom asked for you by name."

He looked at her for a long moment, then at the hatch he had just climbed out of, then back at her.

"Right," he said. "Well. That's my afternoon gone."

---

The office. Calloway. Dunnel with her pad. Mina Haral in the good chair with her brace on a cushion, who had been in the room for twenty minutes and had evidently been told everything already and had formed opinions about all of it.

And then a fifteen-year-old, a deck engineer in a coverall, and a metre of eyeless weave which would not use the corridor and had come along the open side of the stairwell.

Dunnel got up and propped the door wide without being asked, which Vantage noticed.

::: fathom
That was thoughtful. Thank you.
:::

Vantage put it to Calloway itself, which was right, and it put it exactly as it had put it to Lint — including the parts against its own case. That it would not be reaching through. That a field is in the metal and air of that ring whether anybody is crossing or not. That it thought this was more like standing beside a fire than reaching into one, and that it *thought* that and did not know it, and the difference matters.

And then the part she had not said and it did.

::: fathom
If I can read that housing, Director, then every fathom in human space can read every housing in human space. There are eleven of us here and thousands across the Causeway, and we have been riding on children's collarbones through doors we could have been reading the entire time.
:::

::: fathom
Which will be good. And it will also mean somebody will want one of us at every ring on every station, and the asking will be a formality after the first time.
:::

::: fathom
I am saying yes. I would like it minuted that I saw that coming.
:::

Dunnel wrote it down.

---

Calloway turned to Hesk.

"Is it talking nonsense?"

Hesk had gone very quiet and was holding his wrench in both hands.

"...No." He cleared his throat. "No, Director. And I've got something and I don't know if it's anything."

"Go on."

"A housing draws. Big draw, short, every crossing — it's on my bus on the hall deck and it has been for nineteen years." He turned the wrench over. "And **seven dims the hall lamps**. Little flicker, half a blink, nobody sees it. Been doing it as long as I've been here."

"All nine do that."

"No, Director. Three does, because three's old. Seven does it *more*, and seven's done it more since Friday, and I've —" he stopped. "I've been meaning to put a note in. It's lamps. You don't write up lamps."

Mina Haral, from the chair, very dry: "No. You don't write up lamps."

Nobody said anything for a second.

::: fathom
I could tell you whether the draw and the dimming are the same event, from the rim, in about four seconds.
:::

Calloway looked round the room — a fathom, an engineer, a reader, her deputy, and a fifteen-year-old.

"Just need to know what's happening," said Lint. "This is weird and I'm a bit scared."

Calloway did not tell her it was fine.

"Good. It's weird and I'm scared too, and so is everyone on that gallery, and the ones who aren't haven't understood it yet."

She pulled a chair round with her foot so it was facing the rest of them.

"Sit down. Here's what we've got.

"Five facts. Seven's been setting five days. It's at two and a half seconds and climbing. It's done this seven hundred and ninety-four times before, thirty of them badly, and every one of those thirty stopped by day six.

"Today's day five.

"And nobody on the far side knows any of it, because the only way to tell them is to send a child through the thing we're frightened of."

She looked at Vantage in the doorway.

"So the question in this room is whether I let a citizen who isn't mine put his sense on that ring, having watched one of mine go down doing something adjacent two hours ago."

Mina, from the good chair, not looking up: "That's not the question."

Calloway said yes in about four words, and the room emptied.
