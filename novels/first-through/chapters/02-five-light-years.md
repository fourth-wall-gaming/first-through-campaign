# Chapter 2 — Five Light Years

*Day nought · first watch*

The dressing room. Everything went in the locker — boots, the lot, because nothing dead crosses and never has. Only the weave stayed, which is alive and grown to you and the single exception there has ever been. Hers was two years old and starting to cloud across the shoulders, which is thirty-four crossings showing.

And there was the half-second with nothing in it — BlingBling gone, not quiet but *absent*, the way a tooth is absent — and then she was back, blazing green all over Lint's shoulder, saying the thing she says every single time.

::: glint
Still here. Still here. Still here.
:::

Procyon smelled different. Colder air, and something citrus they put through the scrubbers. The boots on that side were stiff and square-toed and a size generous, and she would be giving them back in an hour.

Mina Haral was waiting on the bench, already dressed, with a bag on her knees and her hands folded on top of it. Eighty-one. Thin as paper. She looked up and smiled like Lint was late.

"You'll be the Garden girl. Good. Sit down a minute before we do this — not for me. For you."

She looked at Lint properly, which at her age involved turning her whole head, and her eyes went to the shoulders of the weave and stayed there.

"Thirty-four. Mm. You're quick, then. They work you."

Then she counted it off on fingers like dry sticks. "Right. Here's how we do this, and you'll forgive an old woman for saying it out loud, because the ones who don't say it out loud are the ones who get careless. It takes minutes, not seconds. You hold it and you don't hurry it. I'll be heavier than you expect and I will not help you. And if it starts going wrong at any point, you stop, and you put me back down on this bench, and we try again tomorrow."

A beat. "Say it back to me."

Lint said it back. All four.

"Good. Most of them get the first three." Mina patted the warm bag on her knees. "The fourth is the one they argue with, and I have had two argue with me, and I did not travel with either of them again."

She got up — slowly, one hand on the bench, waving off the one Lint offered — and went to stand in front of the housing.

"I'll tell you what I tell all of them. You're not carrying a passenger. You're carrying the door. I'm just what's in your arms while you do it."

BlingBling had gone absolutely still on Lint's collarbone. Not dark. *Still*, which is a different thing and much rarer.

---

She took Lint's arm. She was, exactly as promised, heavier than she looked.

And it did not take.

She knew it before she was two steps in. A crossing for one is nothing — a step and a change of air. This was like pushing a door against a wind on the other side of it: the field was there, she had hold of it, and it would not *open* for two. It gave, and set, and gave again.

Mina Haral was a weight on her arms and she did not help, exactly as advertised, because helping is the thing that gets people hurt. Her eyes were shut. Her hands were folded.

And BlingBling, who had not shown a single colour since the bench, flared once — hard, white, right at the edge of vision, which is not a word in any vocabulary the two of them had ever built. It was just an alarm.

The bench was four steps back. Mina had told her what to do. Mina had also told her she'd watched two people argue with her about it.

**Since when do we give up half way.**

She pushed, and the door did the thing nobody at the Yards can teach you, because you cannot practise it: it stopped being a door she was holding open and became a thing that was holding *her*. The set went out of it all at once. Her arms were still full of Mina and the floor was still under her and none of that was any help at all.

Then she let go of the door.

That was the only decision she actually made, and she made it in about a fifth of a second, and it was the right one — and letting go of a door you are halfway through is like stepping off a stair that isn't there.

The floor came up and hit both of them.

She was on her back on the Procyon side with Mina Haral half across her chest and the issue tunic torn at the shoulder, and the tallyman was already shouting for a hall doctor and three people were running. Mina was breathing. Her eyes were open. There was blood from her nose, a thin bright line of it, and one of her wrists was wrong.

Lint's arms had no strength in them at all. Everything from the elbows down was somebody else's. The clouding went all the way up her forearms now and she could see it from there, and she had never been able to see it before.

Mina Haral turned her head — slowly, the whole head, the way she does — and looked at her.

"Good girl," she said. "You let go."

---

Two doors down was a four-bed infirmary with three beds empty.

"There she is," said Mina, and sounded pleased. "The girl who dropped me."

"I'm sorry." It came out flatter than Lint meant. "I should have put you down. You told me and I didn't and I'm sorry."

Mina considered this with her head on one side. "Yes. You should. And you did, in the end, about a second later than was ideal — which is roughly where I'd put most of them, so don't go making a career out of it."

She shifted the bag an inch, which seemed to be how she thinks.

"I was turned back at a hall when I was twenty-two. In Alpha Centauri, by a doctor younger than me, who was very kind about it." A pause. "I have been paying children to carry me ever since, because I would not stop working, and I have never once pretended that is a clean thing to do."

She looked up — the whole head.

"Forty-one times. I know every name. Today's was free, and it went wrong, and you are the only one of them who has come down the corridor to say so."

Her good hand found Lint's and tapped it twice, briskly, the way you'd settle an account.

"Go home. Tell Calloway it was my fault and my figures, because it was. And come back for me in three days, when I'm stronger and you are too — I don't want anybody else."

---

Then Lint asked BlingBling whether there was anything *she* wanted to do while they were here, which was a question nobody had put to her on a working day in her life.

BlingBling took a moment. Then — gold, a quick showy spiral of it down the sleeve and back, far too big for the question.

::: glint
Nothing. I am fine. I have no needs. I am a serious professional.
:::

A pause. Then, smaller, in the green she uses when she isn't performing:

::: glint
There's a gallery on the third level. Unfiltered. Procyon's a white star and the dwarf makes it strange, and the light comes in raw and it is the best light anywhere on the Causeway and every glint within four doors knows it.

I have never seen it. We are always turning round at the hall.

Also I would look *incredible* in it.
:::

The gallery was a long room with one wall missing. Not missing — glazed, floor to roof, unfiltered, and nobody had put a shutter anywhere near it. Procyon came in white and hard and made every edge in the room too sharp, and off to one side the dwarf sat in it like a chip of something much brighter than it had any business being.

It was the cleanest light Lint had ever stood in.

And there were eleven glints in there. On the rail, on the sills, two of them hanging upside down off a light fitting for reasons of their own, and the whole room was talking — colour going back and forth across it, fast, overlapping, a conversation at a speed no mouth could manage.

She could not read a word of it. Not one.

Then BlingBling went out into the middle of it, into the raw white, and *lit up* — the greens and the gold and that hot pink, all of it at once, a creature from a system where the sun tries to kill you several times a day standing in the best light in human space being absolutely unbearable about it.

The room responded. Loudly. Somebody flashed something that was obviously rude and she gave it straight back.

And Lint found she had sat down on the bench along the back wall with her useless arms in her lap, in the white light, watching her friend show off to strangers — and that the day had stopped being the worst one she'd had.

Twenty minutes on a bench in the best light in human space. Her arms came back by degrees. The clouding on her forearms did not, and won't.

At the end of it she lifted two fingers and flicked them — *let's go* — the same two fingers she'd used for that since she was thirteen.

BlingBling came off the sill mid-sentence, abandoning what was clearly a winning position, and was on her collarbone before Lint was off the bench. One short gold pulse.

::: glint
Yes. Home. Thank you.
:::

She didn't say it again or make anything of it, which from her is enormous.

---

The return was nothing at all. A step, a change of air, BlingBling out and back, and the Nine came up around her like noise she'd forgotten was loud.

She came up the gallery stair with nothing in her hands and no reader behind her, which is not how anybody leaves this station and comes back, and two people in the hall watched her do it.

Calloway was at the window with her back to the room, and she had been there long enough to have seen it.

"Tell me."

"I failed. Then I pushed and broke her wrist."

Calloway turned round. She looked at Lint's face for about a second and at her forearms for longer.

"Is she alive?"

"Yes. Broken wrist. They're keeping her overnight because she's eighty-one. She said to come back for her in three days."

Calloway let out a breath she had clearly been holding since Lint came up the stair empty-handed. Then she pulled out the chair opposite and sat down in it, which she almost never does, and pushed the indefensible tea across the table without asking.

"Right. You failed the carry. That happens, it happens to everybody, and it is not what I want to talk about." She waited until Lint looked up. "You pushed after it set."

Not a question.

"Lin, I have done thirty-one years of this and I have lost eleven people, and not one of them was lost because they weren't good enough. Every single one was lost because they were having a bad day and decided they could have it both ways."

"I messed up. It's all on me. She even told me to repeat what to do and I didn't do it even then. And I swore."

Calloway took that without blinking, and let it sit for a moment so Lint could hear herself having said it.

"Good. That's the right answer and I'm glad you got there without help." Then: "And you swore."

"...Yes."

"In front of Mina Haral."

"She said *language*."

"I should think she did." Calloway's mouth did something she didn't quite get under control in time. "That woman taught half the readers in human space and she has a tongue on her like a dock crane, and she will be *delighted*. She'll bring it up every time she sees you for the next forty years."

She let Lint have about three seconds of that, which was exactly as much as she needed.

Then she leaned in on her elbows.

"Here's what I actually care about, and then we're done with it. You stopped. Late — a second late, and a second is the whole thing — but you stopped, and you put her down, and she's alive and complaining about it in a Procyon infirmary tonight." She tapped the table once. "That's the skill. Not the carry. *Stopping* is the skill, and almost nobody your age has it at all, and you've got it about a second slower than you need it."

She stood, took the mug back untouched, and tipped it down the sink.

"Three days. You go back for her, you do it properly, and you do not get on the rota before then — I'll have your hide. Go and feed your weave, go and sleep, and tell BlingBling she can stop pretending she isn't listening from the kit rail."

A small, deeply unconvincing pulse of innocent blue from above the suits.
