# Chapter 7 — Supper for Four

*Day one · dusk, the low gallery*

She went to the Wolf Gate queue, because she'd been told four separate times that day that it has the best food on the station and she'd decided to find out. They were right and they will never stop telling you. She brought back enough for four, on the theory that it wouldn't be wasted.

They were on the low gallery, which is where everybody under about twenty ends up at dusk.

Ferren saw the tray first and his whole face changed. "Oh, that's Wolf Gate. That's *Wolf Gate.* Rill. Rill, she's been to the Wolf Gate."

"I can see."

"On her *first day off.* Unprompted."

"I was there," said Sen, off shift, ink washed to the wrist, sitting on the rail with her heels hooked under it. "She's been everywhere. Three times I saw her."

Rill took a bowl without being asked, which from her is a compliment, and knocked her shoulder into Lint's once as she sat down.

So: Ferren, who lost at sling that morning and had told nine people about it in a tone suggesting he had been present at something historic. Rill, twenty-one, sleeves down. Sen, eleven, who keeps the most important object in the Causeway. And BlingBling, established on the rail at precisely the height where the gallery lamp hit her best, holding court for an audience that did not technically exist.

It was, Lint realised, a crew. Assembled in a day and a half, out of nobody she was given.

---

She was most of the way through when a man came along the gallery and stopped at the end of the bench. Good coat. Fifties. Entirely at ease.

"Ferren. How's the shoulder?"

"Fine — yeah, fine, it was nothing—"

"Four weeks ago you couldn't lift a crate and you were *extremely* brave about it." He was smiling. He looked at Sen. "Sennet. Is the Bell line still wrong?"

"It's right now."

"Because of you, I heard." He said it like it mattered, and Sen sat up about an inch.

Then he turned, and he had Lint's name before she'd said it.

"And you're Lint. Out of the Garden, thirty-four at fifteen, came in on the eleventh." He put a hand out. "Benno Raske. I've been hearing about a flat throw all afternoon."

Rill went very slightly still beside her.

Lint turned and looked at Rill. She looked for a beat too long. Then another one. She was still looking when Rill turned her head and found her doing it — and she got nothing at all out of her face, because there was nothing on it, because she's twenty-one and has had four years of people looking at her sideways and has got extremely good at being a wall.

What she did get was Rill seeing her check.

Her sleeves were already down. She pulled one cuff a half-inch further over her wrist anyway, which was not a thing she needed to do, and went back to her bowl.

Benno saw it too. Benno sees everything. He didn't let it sit for even a second.

"What do I do," he said, taking the question up warmly, as though it were the most natural thing anyone had asked him all week. "I arrange carries."

He said it plainly. No softening at all.

"Somebody needs to be three doors away and won't wait a fortnight for the manifest. A pressure fitter wanted at Coldwater by the second watch. An old woman who wants to see Procyon one more time." A small open gesture. "The Threshold runs the traffic that's scheduled. I find couriers for the traffic that isn't, and I agree a price, and I pay it on the day. I've never underpaid anybody in my life and you can ask anyone on this gallery."

"He hasn't," said Ferren, with his mouth full. It was not a defence. It was just true and Ferren knew it was true.

"I was landlocked at twenty-three," Benno added, easily, without a trace of self-pity. "So I'm no use to anybody through a door. This is what's left."

He looked at the tray — the four bowls, the Wolf Gate food, the empty one.

"You've bought supper for three people on your day off, which tells me more about you than the throw did." He straightened up. "I'm not asking you for anything tonight, Lint. I'd just rather you knew my name from me than from somebody on the gallery."

A beat. "Can I get another round of that in? Sennet eats like a docker and I enjoy watching it."

"Sorry," said Lint, a bit flustered. "I only got enough for us."

"*Us*," Benno repeated.

Not sharply. He just turned the word over once, the way you'd check the weight of something, and something went across his face very fast and was gone before you could name it.

Then he smiled and it reached his eyes.

"Fair enough." And he sat — on the end of the bench, leaving a clear space, in the manner of a man who has sat on a lot of benches where he wasn't quite invited. "I've eaten. I'll just be here a minute."

---

He was a minute and it was a good minute.

He asked Sen whether the Standing had a line for the new door at LHS 1140 yet, and Sen said *no, because it isn't a door yet*, with enormous scorn, and Benno said "quite right, I'm being sloppy," and meant it. He told a story about a Coldwater grain crate that came through upside down in 'ninety-one and what was inside it and what the tallyman said, and Ferren laughed so hard he had to put the bowl down, and Sen had heard it before and was mouthing along with the punchline.

He didn't ask Lint a single thing about herself.

He didn't look at Rill once, either, and Rill didn't look at him, and the two of them managed this so smoothly that you'd have to be watching for it.

Then he stood, tapped the rail twice, and went. Three separate people said hello to him before he was out of sight.

"He's *brilliant*," said Ferren, wiping his eyes.

"Mm," said Rill.

Sen, from the rail, flatly: "Mum says don't take his money."

"Your mum says don't do loads of things."

"She says it about *him* the most."

---

Lint put her thumb out towards the end of the gallery where he'd gone — then turned her hand over, palm down, fingers shut. *I don't like him.*

BlingBling came off the lamp bracket and down onto the bench beside her knee, out of the good light, which she does not do for nothing.

::: glint
No. Nor do I.

He talked to everyone on this bench. He asked the child about her board and the loud one about his shoulder and he knew your number before you said it.

He did not look at me once. Not once. Not when I moved, not when I lit, not when I was six inches from his hand.

Everyone looks at me. I make certain of it. He is the first person on this station who did not bother.

I do not think he is pretending to be kind. I think that is the worst part.
:::

And glints cannot be bought and cannot carry cargo and cannot be hired, so there is nothing a man who arranges carries would ever need from one.

Sen was still on the rail with her heels hooked under it, and she'd watched that whole exchange without understanding a single flash of it, and she didn't ask.

What she said instead, eventually, was: "Rill's going to be at the void tomorrow. She said she would, so she will." A beat, eleven years old and entirely matter-of-fact. "She doesn't come down much any more."
