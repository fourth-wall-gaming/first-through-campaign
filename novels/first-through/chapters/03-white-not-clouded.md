# Chapter 3 — White, Not Clouded

*Day nought · the dead watch*

She didn't sleep. She got maybe an hour of the kind that isn't, the kind where you're aware of the blanket, and then she was just lying in the dark doing the carry again with a different ending. Four or five different endings. In two of them she put Mina down.

She got as far as the water point on the second level. Then the hall below did the thing it does.

It isn't a sound. It's about nine people all stopping being loud at the same moment, which is a sound of its own.

Door four. A night transit, which nobody schedules.

**Rill** came up out of the housing already peeling the membrane off her forearms, and she was very good at it — no stagger, no hand on the frame, the way you do it when you've done it two thousand times. Twenty-one. There is a record scratched on a stanchion in the gallery void with her name against it and nobody has got close in four years.

Two hall kids who can't cross and never will were *already* at the bottom of the stair with a towel and a flask.

And behind her, up out of the same door about forty seconds later and much more slowly, came a man in a good coat with a soft bag, who was not Threshold, who was not fifteen, and who was being very carefully not looked at by everybody in the hall.

Rill handed him off to somebody waiting at the far arch without a word. Transaction. Done.

Then she sat down on the bottom step, took the flask off the hall kid, said something that made her laugh, and tipped her head back against the rail with her eyes shut.

From the second-level rail you could see her forearms. They weren't clouded. They were *white*.

---

She opened one eye, because people always know, and found Lint on the second-level rail at four in the morning in a blanket.

"...You're the new one. You're up late."

"You're Rill. Yeah. Messed up a transit today."

"Rill. Yeah." She said it the way you'd admit to a surname. Then she shifted over on the step and tapped the deck beside her twice with two fingers, which is the same signal Lint uses on BlingBling, and apparently everybody uses it, and she had not known that.

Lint came down the stair in a blanket and told her. Not all of it. The carry, the set, the push.

Rill did none of the things she'd been braced for all evening. No wince. No *oh no*. She listened with her eyes shut like somebody checking a figure.

"Is she alive?"

"Yes. Broken wrist."

"Hm." A pull from the flask. "Then you didn't mess it up."

And she said it so *easily*.

"That's the job, new one. It sets, you push, you find out. Everybody who's any good at this has pushed." She held a hand out flat in the low light, and it was steady, and it was the white of a thing left too long in water. "They'll all tell you the number of people who died pushing. Nobody ever counts the ones who got carried out because somebody did."

"Who was it, anyway. Procyon run?"

"Mina Haral. Yep."

Rill laughed. Actually laughed — once, short, surprised out of her. "*Mina.*"

"You know her?"

"New one, everybody knows her. She taught my reading. Not me specifically — she taught the people who taught us, there's about four hundred of us who think she taught them personally and she lets us all think it." Then, flatter: "I've carried her twice."

The hall kid with the towel went very still.

"Alpha Centauri, both times. First one I was seventeen and she talked the entire way through the housing about the ceiling, which is a thing she does so you don't have time to be frightened, and I didn't work that out until years later." A beat. "She tips. You'd think she wouldn't, because she's got nothing, and she does, every time, and you can't give it back because she just puts it in your boot."

She turned the flask round in her hands. "Eighty-one. Still going. Honestly it's —"

And she stopped. Just stopped, mid-sentence, like a door closing softly in another room. Lint watched her decide not to finish it.

---

"Bet you've carried her before," Lint said, when Rill was already turning for the arch.

She stopped. "Twice. I just told you twice." Not sharp. She looked at Lint for a second longer, working out whether she hadn't been listening or whether she was asking something else, and then deciding it was something else.

She came back two steps. "You mean the man in the coat."

Lint didn't say anything, which is the same as saying yes.

"Right." Rill set the flask down on the step. "Ask it properly, then. You've earned one straight answer tonight and I'd rather it came from me than off somebody on the gallery."

So Lint asked her why she takes them. And she gave it in one sentence, the way you'd read out a number:

> "Because the money is real, and it's the only money anybody has ever offered me for *afterwards*, and nobody — not Calloway, not the Threshold, not one person in six hundred years — has ever worked out what a frayed-out first-through is supposed to do with the rest of her life."

The hall was very quiet. Even the kid with the towel.

"That's it. That's the whole thing. It isn't brave and it isn't tragic, it's a job." She picked the flask back up. "And Mina Haral pays children to carry her because the alternative is she never sees Procyon again, and the hall at twenty-two told her no, and she was *twenty-two*."

Then she was done. You could see her be done.

"Right. The void. Tomorrow, if you're not asleep by then." She was going. "You still haven't told me your name."

"Lint. Call me Lin."

"Lin." She tried it once, nodded like it had been filed. "Rill. Which you knew."

She was three steps into the arch when she stopped and turned round, and this time she took a moment over it.

"Hey. Lin. You asked me that without your face doing anything."

"...Was I supposed to?"

"Everybody does. Not on purpose." She shrugged, and it was a tired, genuine, slightly embarrassed shrug, and for a second she looked about nineteen. "Anyway. It was nice. Go to bed."
