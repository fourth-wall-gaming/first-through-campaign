# Chapter 8 — Sticky

*Day two · dawn, grounded*

She woke up on her own. BlingBling was a grey smear on the pillow, came up to a slow unwilling bronze when Lint's weight left the bunk, registered that nothing was happening, and went straight back to grey.

On the stair, two Wolf Gate loaders went past her with crates, mid-grumble.

"— and it's *sticky* again, I stood there like an idiot—"

"It's sticky every spring. Give it a second and it has you."

"I gave it a *second*."

And they were gone round the turn of the stair.

She caught them two flights down. They were happy to talk. Of course they were — loaders with crates and a grievance, and she was fifteen and Threshold and actually asking.

"**Sticky.**" The older one set his crate on the step. "You've not had it yet, then. So — you step up, you give it your weight, and nothing. Not a push. It just goes solid, like the ring's a wall. Then you come off it, you give it a second, you go again and it has you. That's sticky."

"That's a *set*," said the younger one. "They teach you set at the Yards."

"Set's what happens once. Sticky's when it's doing it all week."

"It's the same thing—"

"It is *not* the same thing, because set's your fault and sticky's the door's."

And then, in passing, as though it were the dullest thing he knew:

"Seven's sticky every spring. Has been since I started. You give it a second and it has you, and come summer it stops, and nobody thinks about it."

And it went past her.

Not because she wasn't listening. Because it was the single most reassuring sentence anybody had said to her since she arrived, and it arrived in a stairwell from a man with a crate on his knee who had been doing this job since before she was born.

"So it's — that's just a thing it does?"

"It's just a thing it does." He got the crate back up on his shoulder. "Everything here's got a thing it does. Four's loud, the Old Line smells of fish twice a year and nobody's ever found out why, and seven's sticky in the spring." He set off down the stair. "You'll have a door you hate inside a year and it'll be a different one to mine."

The whole thing took ninety seconds, and she came away from it feeling considerably better about a door she had not been worried about in the first place.
