# Chapter 24 — Sixty of Sixty-One

*Day four · night, into day five*

She sat down on the deck plate with her back against a stanchion, which was the first time she had sat down since dawn, and she watched other people do it.

There was nothing left in her arms and she had known it on the plate with Teo. Six crossings. Nobody has ever done six, and the reason nobody has is that the seventh is the one that takes something back off you.

Mina Haral came off the bench and got both thumbs into the back of her neck and did not let her get up, and she did not try very hard.

It took most of the night and she watched all of it.

---

It stopped working. Not all at once.

The first carry took four sets instead of one. The second took nine. By the fourth, the first-through was coming back out of the ring empty-handed with her jaw set, and the number on Dunnel's sheet had gone three point six, four point one, four point four, and a carry that had taken forty seconds at dawn was taking eleven minutes.

Vantage read it off the rim every half hour and said the number out loud to the whole hall and did not soften it once.

Two things happened in that hour.

A first-through called Oris — one of the three who had been stuck on the Ladder side, who had come home and would not stay home — forced it. Didn't wait for a carrier, didn't wait for the count, just walked at it because he was fifteen and he had spent a day on a rock with people who could not leave it. It took him and let go of him halfway. He came down on his back on the Ladder apron with a collarbone you could hear from four metres away, and he was on the wrong side of it.

And the Wolf Gate girl, the one who had argued about leaving her — she got home. Walked out of the ring at the Nine on her own two feet at forty-four seconds, sat down, and was sick on the deck plate. The third of them came through about an hour behind her and went straight to bed and nobody saw him for a day.

Six of the seven went. Then the number was five point two and the mouth was not hesitating any more. It was *letting go*.

Vantage, flat, to the whole hall: "The potential at the far mouth is resolving to a second value. I give it minutes, not hours."

Still on Ladder: Tace Orun. One of her crew. And a fifteen-year-old with a broken collarbone who could not be carried by anybody who was not very good indeed.

---

Rill went back in for him.

Twenty-one years old, with however few crossings she had left and nobody's estimate agreeing on the number. It took her five sets, and she came out of that ring forty minutes later with a fifteen-year-old over her shoulder, put him down on the plate, and sat on the deck beside him and did not get up for an hour.

Four hundred for the night, and the last one was hers, and she was paid for the one before it.

Nobody said anything about either, which is how that hall works.

Mina called *put them down* from the bench three hundred-odd times and lost her voice doing it, and at some point near the end she stopped being able to say it and started just raising her hand, and four hundred children who had had two hours of her watched for the hand.

---

Then the last of Tace's crew came through, and that was sixty.

Nobody had to be carried off that rock after that, because there was nobody left on it who wanted to be.

Tace Orun stayed.

She crossed the sixty-first name off her own board — hers — handed the board to Oris to take through, and then stood back off the apron with her hands behind her and said she had a rock to run and a drain to fix and she would see them when the door was sure of itself.

Calloway argued with her through a mouth at five and a half seconds and lost.

---

It went three hours later, in the dead watch, and it made no sound at all.

---

Morning.

Door seven at the Nine was warm, steady, and read zero point zero. It had not read zero in four days.

And it opened onto a concrete apron under a welded roof, where a foreman was sweeping grit out of a ring that had worked exactly once in a hundred and ninety years and now worked every time.

The Standing had been corrected. Sennet did it at some point in the night and nobody saw her do it. Where the refinery's line used to run there was now a line that said **LADDER**, and against the refinery there was nothing at all, for the first time in seven hundred and ninety-eight years. Twelve thousand people on the far side of nothing, entirely safe, and completely alone, and the Tally did not know yet.

Sixty people were asleep on the Threshold floor. Four hundred first-throughs had given up their bunks and were sprawled in the galleries and on the stairs, and three of them were asleep in the kettle alcove. Somebody was already complaining, loudly, that the Wolf Gate food run was gone and what were they supposed to eat now.

There was nothing left in her at all, and her arm would not go above her shoulder.

And she was the only person on that station who had stood in that shed.
