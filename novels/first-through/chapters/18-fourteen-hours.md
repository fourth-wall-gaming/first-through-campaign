# Chapter 18 — Fourteen Hours

*Day three · the ring, and the far side of it*

The hall. Cordon open on one side. Vantage settled at the lip of the housing, close enough that the light off him went strange, and stopped moving.

Nobody counted out loud. Everybody counted.

Eleven seconds. Then he came off the lip, fast, and went up two metres before he spoke.

::: fathom
There is a slope across that ring and it runs from the port side to the starboard side and it is **steep**. Not the field of a working housing. I have felt three others this week to compare and they are flat.
:::

::: fathom
And it is not steady. It moves — a hand's width, side to side, about twice a second, the whole time. It is hunting.
:::

He stopped.

::: fathom
I can also tell you which way it is leaning, and I would like somebody to write it down before I say it, because I will only be sure once.
:::

Dunnel already had the pen.

"Wait. Listen."

Lint put a hand up. He stopped.

Nine queues. The Procyon Stair running. Somebody's crate going over two bays down. A tallyman reading out a manifest.

And the housing, which makes no sound at all and never has.

Vantage waited. So did the hall.

"...Nothing. Sorry. Go on."

::: fathom
Port side. The low side is port, and the slope is dragging that way, and it has been the whole eleven seconds.
:::

Hesk, very quietly, from behind the cordon rope: "Port's the awkward approach. That's the side everybody stands on."

---

The rest took four minutes and came out as a number.

Vantage's slope, plus Hesk's lamp flicker, plus seven hundred and ninety-four dips, and the answer was that door seven was going to move, port side, and not on day six.

*Fourteen hours,* said Sirius. *Plus or minus four. I would not bet the far side of it on the minus.*

Calloway did not sit down.

"Right. Twelve thousand people, a hundred and forty on the branch, and nobody over there knows. Mert — get me every courier on this deck who'll volunteer, in the hall, now."

Then, to Lint, flat:

"You're the one who goes. You said so twice today. Through, tell Vey, come straight back."

Dunnel: "Director, she's four crossings in today and the last one was carrying."

"She slept, and she's the only one of mine who's done a hard carry clean this month."

Calloway looked at her.

"Yes or no."

"Of course I'll go."

---

Ossuary had her sealed in ninety seconds. Mina said one thing on the way past and it wasn't *good luck*.

The plate. BlingBling on her collar.

It set, and kept setting. She came off, eased, went again. Nothing. Again. Nothing.

Ninety seconds of a lit ring refusing her in front of the whole hall.

And then she stopped fighting it for a quarter of a beat — and it had her *instantly*. No wait at all. Less than any of the twenty-three that morning.

---

Hot metal. Ozone. The hardened corridor, shift workers eight deep, and a tallyman already turning round.

Vey was twenty metres away at the corridor mouth and saw her come up out of the housing with nothing in her hands.

"Seven's shut at your end?"

"No. It's moving. Fourteen hours, port side."

She didn't ask who said so.

"Rolf — shelter board, now." Then back to her, already walking. "Tell me the rest while I wake twelve thousand people."

So she gave it to her walking, in order, and Vey didn't interrupt once. Seven hundred and ninety-four of these in the record. Thirty bad ones. A twenty-six-year cycle and this is the thirty-first, and it is day five at two and a half seconds against a previous worst of one point one. A slope across the ring, steep, port side, hunting twice a second. Fourteen hours, plus or minus four.

And that it is not going to shut. It is going to **move**.

Vey stopped dead at the shelter board.

"Move where?"

"Nobody at the Nine knows. Same potential, same system, that's all the physics gives us."

And she went very still, and then she said it.

"**Ladder.**"

She was already pulling a chart off the wall.

"Sixty-one people. Survey post, hundred and ninety million kilometres out. They've got a ring in a shed that's never opened in two hundred years and it sits at our depth, because the first crew put it there *on purpose* in case this ever happened."

She looked up.

"And they've got no shelter. And the twins are closing."

"Ok. What does that mean?"

---

Vey turned the chart round and explained it slowly, without softening.

"A door isn't tied to a *place*. It's tied to a **depth** — how deep it sits in all the gravity round here. Like a water level. It doesn't care which rock it's on. It cares what level it's at."

A finger on the chart. "We're here, this corridor, at a certain depth." The finger moved. "And a hundred and ninety million kilometres out is Ladder. Sixty-one people on a rock. And by accident of where that rock sits — **Ladder's at the same depth as us.**"

"So when it jumps—"

"It goes *there*. Not away. Not shut. There."

She tapped it twice.

"We lose it. Twelve thousand of us, cut off, four weeks by ship to the only working door in the system. We'll live — we've got the shelter and we grow more than we eat. We'll just be gone until somebody cares.

"And Ladder gets a door they've never had. Sixty-one people who could never leave, suddenly able to."

She rolled the chart up.

"Welded roof. No shelter. And the twins are closing."

---

"Ok. Can you signal them directly what's going to happen? What's your plan here?"

"Inside a system? Course we can."

Vey was already at the console.

"Light works. Ladder's ten and a half minutes away by radio and we talk to them four times a day about supply runs. It's only *between stars* that nothing crosses but a person — everyone forgets that because everyone only ever thinks about doors."

She started typing.

"So here's the plan and it's three things.

"**One.** I tell Ladder in the next minute. They've got fourteen hours and a ring in a shed, and the first thing those sixty-one people are going to do is dig that shed out and find out whether the apron's still flat.

"**Two.** I put twelve thousand people in the shelter and I keep them there, because the twins are closing anyway and that part was always coming.

"**Three** — and this one's yours, and it's the only one I can't do."

She looked up.

"**Nobody at the Nine knows where that door is going.** And in fourteen hours it'll be standing in a shed next to sixty-one people with nowhere to shelter, opening onto your hall — and if your lot aren't *expecting* it, they'll waste a day finding out."

She went back to typing.

"So go. And go soon, because when it moves, whoever's standing on this side of it stays here for about a month."

"Yep. I might be back. Depends on what Calloway says." A beat. "Good luck."

Vey didn't look up from the console.

"Don't wish me luck, I've got a shelter. Wish it at the shed."

Then, as Lint turned for the corridor:

"And if your director sends you back — come *before* the fourteen hours, not after. I'd rather tell you I'm fine than have you find out."

---

A short set, one ease, and it had her.

The Nine. Nine queues, a cordon, and about forty people who had been staring at an empty lit ring for eleven minutes.

Calloway was two metres from the rope.

"Where's it going?"

"**Ladder.** Sixty-one people, hundred and ninety million kilometres out, survey post. There's a ring in a shed there that's never opened — same depth as the refinery corridor, so when it jumps it lands there. Fourteen hours. Vey's telling them now by radio and putting her twelve thousand in the shelter."

Dunnel stopped writing.

"Ladder's got no shelter," said Lint. "And the twins are closing."

Calloway turned round to the hall and the hall was already quiet.

"Right," she said. "Then in fourteen hours that door opens into a shed with sixty-one people standing round it, and we are the only way out they have ever had."

She raised her voice.

"**I want every first-through on this station in this hall.**"
