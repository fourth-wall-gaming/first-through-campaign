# Chapter 1 — The Kettle

*Day nought · dawn*

The ready room smelled of hot metal off the hall below, and of tea that had been wrong for six hundred years.

Nine doors going at once under the window, nine queues, the noise coming up through the floor like weather. A long table. A kit rail. The kettle.

Calloway had her back to the room and four people were telling her she was wrong about it. One of them was standing on the table. One was a cairn, low and plated and immovable near the hotplate, who had been there some hours.

"—because you *scald* it. Every morning. We have told you every morning for a year."

"I was doing this before your mother qualified."

BlingBling was on the kit rail above the suits, going bright-dark-bright at the speed that meant she was laughing, and the boy on the table kept losing his thread because of it.

Then Calloway turned round and saw Lint in the doorway, and her whole face changed.

"Lin." She put the pot down. "There she is. Thirty-four and still upright." To the room, not looking away: "This one swims. In water. Outdoors."

The one on the table said, "In *what*?"

"Sit down, don't let them give you the tea, and tell me how the elbow is — because the log says you went through Procyon twice on Tuesday and I know exactly what that housing does to people."

"Fine," said Lint. "The elbow's fine."

Calloway gave her a look that filed that under *answers I will come back to*, and went back to the pot.

---

Lint tipped her chin a fraction at the kit rail and flicked two fingers — *what's going on* — and BlingBling answered before her hand was down, because she had been waiting to be asked.

::: glint
He is trying to ask her something. Twenty minutes. Four run-ups. The tea is not the subject.
:::

The cairn by the hotplate said, in that unhurried way, "He has been building to it since the night watch. We are all very invested."

"*Thank* you, Ossuary," said the boy on the table.

And Calloway, pouring, not turning round: "Ask me properly or get down, Pell, the pair of you are making the room strange."

So Lint asked him directly what he was talking about, which is not the same as taking the hint, and Pell went a deeper shade of pink and said it fast, at the table rather than at anybody.

"I want to go on the Wolf Gate rota. Permanent. Not the rotation — the standing run."

Somebody by the kit rail whistled, because the Wolf Gate is a dull door and nobody asks for it.

"My mother's at Ladder," he said. "And my brother. He's six. I get out there twice a year on the rotation and he's a different kid every time, he doesn't—" He stopped, and shrugged, and went back to the middle distance. "That's it. That's the whole speech. It was better at four in the morning."

Calloway had not turned round. She had been pouring the same pot for about fifteen seconds longer than pouring takes.

"Yes," she said.

"I had — there's more of it—"

"I'm sure there is. The answer's yes, Pell, it was yes before you got on the table. You're on the standing run from the next rota. Go and tell your mother."

---

Then she set a mug down in front of Lint. It was, demonstrably, scalded.

"Drink that and stop encouraging them." A pad beside it. "Procyon again, I'm afraid. Mina Haral's coming back and somebody has to go and get her — she's a reader, she's eighty-one, and she is not crossing without a first-through who'll be patient with her. Five light years, shortest hop on the Causeway, you'll be back before the watch turns."
