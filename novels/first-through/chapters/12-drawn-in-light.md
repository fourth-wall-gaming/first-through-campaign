# Chapter 12 — Drawn in Light

*Day two · second watch, the void*

Vantage was there, obviously. It had been there since first watch and it was going to pretend otherwise for at least a minute.

::: fathom
You are punctual. I had not formed a view either way.
:::

Ferren was at the plate with a bag, having come off an early, with the specific smugness of a man who turned up uninvited and got away with it.

"Told you I was coming."

"You said you were coming."

"And here I am. Observe." He tossed the bag up and caught it. "Learning."

Up on the handrail, eleven glints were already drilling intervals, and BlingBling went up to join them and was immediately and loudly insufferable about having been elsewhere.

---

Vantage came down to about head height, which for it is close.

::: fathom
Two days ago I told you that you were all throwing into a dead charge and that there is a standing field across this gap and not one of you has felt it once. That was unkind and it was also imprecise, and I have had two days to think about how to say it properly, so here it is.
:::

::: fathom
You cannot feel a field. You have eyes. **But you can see what it does.**
:::

::: fathom
The exchangers at the waist push a column of air up this void — and it is not still, and it is not uniform. It is strongest at the waist, it weakens going up, and it **turns** about a third of the way across because of the deck geometry above you. Which means a bag thrown flat and hard fights it for the first third and then gets shoved off-true for the rest.
:::

::: fathom
That is why Ferren is good. He corrects for it without knowing he does.
:::

"I keep *telling* you I don't—"

::: fathom
You have been doing it for four years and that is precisely why you cannot teach anybody.
:::

::: fathom
So. You will not learn to feel it. You will learn to **read** it, and I am going to show you how, because it is extremely simple and nobody has ever looked. Dust. Lamp flicker. The way the hall noise arrives louder for half a second. There are about nine tells in this void and they are all visible to a person with eyes, and I have been watching you lot miss them for six years.
:::

::: fathom
Stand at the plate. Don't throw. Watch the gap for a full minute.
:::

A full minute. Then another one, because she wasn't having it.

And she got **nothing**.

The void was forty metres of nothing with handrails round it. There was dust, because there is always dust. The lamps did what lamps do. The hall noise came up and went away and came up again and she could not tell whether that meant anything or whether she was a fifteen-year-old staring at a gap.

"...I can't see it."

::: fathom
No.
:::

Vantage said it without any disappointment at all, which was somehow worse.

::: fathom
You are looking for **air**, and air is not visible, so you are looking for a thing that is not there. I have explained it badly. That is mine, not yours.
:::

"You said there were nine tells—"

::: fathom
There are nine tells and they are all second-order. Dust that is going the wrong way, and you do not know which way is wrong. A lamp flicker, and you do not know the lamp's normal. I have six years of this void to compare against and you have three days, and I have just asked you to read a language by staring at it.
:::

It went quiet for a second. Then:

::: fathom
So let us do this properly, and I am going to say something that costs me considerably.
:::

::: fathom
I am the wrong teacher. I can feel the field and therefore I cannot show you how to see it, which is **exactly** the thing I said about Ferren eleven minutes ago, and I did not notice I was doing it.
:::

Ferren, with enormous joy: "*HA.*"

::: fathom
Be quiet.
:::

::: fathom
There are twelve creatures in this void who do not feel the field and do not need to see it, because they are **in** it. All day. Every day. They fly through that column and they correct for it and they have done since they hatched.
:::

Up on the handrail, eleven glints and her own had stopped drilling.

::: fathom
Ask them.
:::

And BlingBling came down off the rail at speed and landed on her collarbone, and you could tell from two metres away exactly what was coming, because she had said it to Lint three days earlier on that exact deck and neither of them had thought anything of it at the time.

::: glint
There is a standing field in the gallery void, and I have always known about it, and I thought everybody did.
:::

---

"How do I see it?"

::: glint
You don't. You can't see air, Lin, air isn't a colour. Honestly.

You see *me*.
:::

And then she was off the collarbone and out into the void. Lit. Full gold, deliberately, the brightest she had ever gone in a working space — and she flew the gap in a straight line at the waist, and about a third of the way across Lint watched her *get pushed*.

Not drift. *Pushed*, a clean metre and a half sideways, and she didn't fight it, she rode it out and came round and landed on the far rail.

Then she came back the other way, lower, and it hardly touched her. Then higher, and it shoved her almost into the underside of the deck.

"— oh."

::: glint
Oh.
:::

She agreed, insufferably.

And then the other eleven came off the rail, because of course they did, because showing off is how they learn anything.

Eleven glints and hers, all lit, flying the column at different heights in a loose shambolic formation that was being argued about the entire time — and forty metres of void turned into a **diagram**.

She could see the whole thing. The column standing at the waist. Where it weakens going up. The turn, a third of the way across, exactly where Vantage said it was and absolutely nothing like where she had imagined it.

It was in the air, drawn in light, by twelve people who found it so obvious they never mentioned it.

Four levels of gallery stopped to watch and nobody pretended they hadn't.

Very quietly, from somewhere above the deck:

::: fathom
I did say that I was the wrong teacher.
:::

Ferren sat down on the plate. "Four years," he said, to nobody. "Four years I've been doing this."

---

And here is the thing nobody warns you about.

Twelve glints were holding a formation in mid-air to show her where the column was. Four levels of gallery had stopped. A fathom who waited six years for somebody to ask it a question was at her shoulder. Everything she needed to know was drawn in light, in front of her, forty metres wide.

And she overthought it so comprehensively that she forgot to throw properly. Half a hand higher than feels correct — she went about *three* hands higher, because she was concentrating, and she released late, and the bag went up out of the column entirely, missed the turn, missed the whole phenomenon she had just had explained to her by a committee, clipped the handrail on the far side at an angle of pure insult, and dropped forty metres into the netting.

Dead silence from four levels. Then a single voice from the second gallery: "*OHHHH.*"

"That," said Ferren, with immense care, "was worse than your normal throw."

"I know."

"That was worse than any throw I have ever seen. And I've seen a hall child throw with the wrong hand on purpose."

"*I know.*"

::: fathom
A control, of sorts. We now have one data point establishing that the information is not sufficient on its own.
:::

"Thanks."

::: fathom
You are welcome.
:::

BlingBling was extremely gold and absolutely silent on her collarbone, which is the single most devastating thing she could possibly have been doing.

"*Grash* it. Try again."

Nobody says *language* out there. That's the Yards. The bag came back up on a line. She stopped concentrating. That was the only thing she did differently.

---

And then, because the day had gone well and she was fifteen:

"Do a flip. Try not to die. Wooo hooo."

Two of them, actually.

She got one foot on the cable trunk by the plate and went up, and in ninety-second spin at the waist of a two-kilometre cylinder you go up *far* — and the first rotation was clean and she had time, so much time, absurd amounts of time, and somewhere in the middle of it she decided to have another one.

She came down on the plate on both feet with her knees absorbing it and one hand out for balance and she did not even stagger.

And then, because the Garden is still in her somewhere, she finished it with a bow.

The void went **off**.

Four levels. The Wolf Gate kids hammering on the handrail. Somebody at the second gallery doing a sustained note that was going to get them told off. The hall child with the flask standing on the top rail, which is explicitly banned four times over.

"**TWO?**" Ferren had both hands on his head and was walking in circles. "She did *two*. On the plate. After that throw. Rill — **Rill** —"

And Rill was on the stair, because she had come down again, and she was leaning on the rail with her arms folded and she was laughing — properly, openly, the whole way, which nobody at the Nine had seen her do.

"Yeah," she said. "All right. That was good."

Eleven glints went up all at once off the handrail in one enormous synchronised flash and immediately broke into an argument about who was late. BlingBling was in the middle of them, lit like a signal lamp, and was not arguing, because she had nothing to prove to anybody in that void.

::: glint
Vantage teaches extremely badly and was right about everything, and I have decided he is one of ours.
:::

A pause from somewhere above the deck.

::: fathom
...I shall take that in the spirit in which it was intended.
:::

---

She didn't go far afterwards. Fourth level, port side, past where the Coldwater offices stop being offices. The maintenance gallery with the ports nobody bothered to shutter.

Her spot.

Somebody had been in there since that morning. There was a cushion on the cable trunk that had not been there before — an old one, Threshold grey, with a repair on the corner in somebody's bad stitching — and it had very obviously been put there on purpose by a person who knew she came there and did not want to be caught doing it.

She read the speeches until the warm-air return and the low orange lamps did for her, and fell asleep on it, and that is where day two ended.

**Best day ever.**
