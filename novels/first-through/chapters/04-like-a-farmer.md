# Chapter 4 — Like a Farmer

*Day one · grounded, first of three*

Rill put the bag in her hands. Wet sand in canvas, worn shiny at the strap.

"Feet behind the plate. Nobody catches for you on the first one, so just get it there."

Forty metres of open void. Six levels of the Nine stacked above it and the hall noise coming up from below.

She didn't think about it, which is the whole trick, and the Quick did the rest. The bag went out flat and low and landed in the hands of a boy on the far rail who absolutely was not expecting to have to do anything, and who caught it anyway, mostly with his chest.

Rill said nothing for a second. "...Hm."

"Hm?"

"You've got a horrible flat throw and I hate it." She took her sleeves down and then shoved them back up again. "Right, that's not fair, you should know that's actually good. Garden thing? You lot throw like you've had weather."

"*Garden thing?* What am I, a lawn ornament?"

Rill actually laughed, which from her is a loud noise. "She's from the Garden, Ferren. She's stood in weather nobody made."

"I've stood in weather!"

"You've stood in a *leak*."

---

Best of five took nine minutes and turned into the thing everybody on four levels stopped to watch.

Ferren was better than her. Nineteen, two intakes up, and a throw that went out so flat it looked like it was on a wire. He took the second and the fourth without appearing to try, and he was *nice* about it, which was unbearable. She took the third because his catcher sneezed.

And she took the fifth because he went for the spectacular one and she didn't. Her bag went out flat and low and boring and landed in a hall child's lap, who had not moved off the top rail the entire time, and who caught it one-handed without putting the flask down.

Dead silence for about a second and a half. Then forty metres of gallery made a noise you could feel in the deck plate.

"That's — no. *No.* That's a boring throw. That's an *agricultural* throw—"

"It counted," said the hall child.

---

Which is roughly when something a metre across and the colour of nothing in particular detached itself from the upper rail, where it had clearly been all along, and drifted down through the middle of the void without any obvious means of doing so.

A **fathom**. Eyeless. Not interested in being looked at. It stopped about two metres off, and a voice arrived in the middle of her head without passing her ears.

::: fathom
*You are all throwing into a dead charge. There is a standing field across this gap and not one of you has felt it once.*

*I could put that bag in the child's hands from here, blind, at twice the distance. I have been offering to demonstrate this for six years.*
:::

"Nobody asked, Vantage," said Ferren, through his teeth, not looking at it.

::: fathom
Nobody ever does.
:::

"OK," said Lint. "I'll bite. Show me."

Six years, and the person who finally said yes had been there a day.

::: fathom
...Oh.
:::

For one unguarded instant the dryness dropped clean out of its voice, and that was somehow the most embarrassing thing that happened to anyone in the gallery that day. Then it recovered, hard.

::: fathom
Very well. Since someone has finally shown some rigour.
:::

---

It made the boy on the far rail hold the bag, four metres out from the plate, and then it went up — twelve, fifteen metres, until it was a pale smudge against the underside of the next deck, and it stopped in a place where there was visibly nothing to stop against.

::: fathom
There is a conduit run behind that panelling.
:::

A pause.

::: fathom
Of course you cannot feel it. You have eyes.
:::

"Get on with it," said Ferren.

::: fathom
Let the bag go.
:::

And it didn't fall straight. It fell *wrong* — drifted about a metre and a half sideways on the way down, which should be impossible in a cylinder with its own gravity and absolutely no wind, and landed in the hall child's lap for the second time in five minutes.

The hall child looked down at it. Then up. Then said, with enormous satisfaction, "It counted."

Dead quiet across four levels.

"...What was that," said Rill, slowly.

::: fathom
Air.
:::

Vantage was already drifting back down, insufferable again and visibly pleased about it.

::: fathom
The gallery exchangers push a column up the void and nothing in it is still. You have all been throwing through a river for two hundred years and calling it skill.
:::

Ferren sat down on the walkway.

Lint gave the gallery the full face — eyes wide, mouth round, both hands up, the whole *oooooooo* — and four levels of the Nine gave it straight back, which was the loudest thing that had happened in the void all week.

"I'm being bullied," said Ferren, to nobody.

Then she turned back to Vantage and told it properly that it was actually, genuinely cool, and that she'd never have known it was there.

And the fathom — eyeless, a metre of membrane, a hundred and ninety centimetres off the deck — went completely still.

::: fathom
...Yes. Well.
:::

It did not know what to do. You could tell it did not know what to do, from a creature with no face whatsoever.

"And," she added, "I might actually be able to *use* that. If I didn't throw LIKE A FARMER."

"OH, that's mine," said Ferren, both arms out. "That's my line. You can't have my line."

---

It settled, more or less, at her eye level, and when it spoke again it had dropped most of the performance and kept all of the snobbery.

::: fathom
It is not complicated. The column is strongest at the waist and it turns about a third of the way across. A flat throw fights it. A throw you release half a hand higher and slightly off-true lets it carry.
:::

A pause.

::: fathom
Ferren has been correcting for it unconsciously for four years and has no idea he does it. That is why he is good and cannot teach anybody.
:::

"I'm RIGHT HERE."

::: fathom
I am aware.
:::

Then: *I will show you. Not today — the light is wrong and the exchangers run hard until the second watch, and you have been awake for four hours after breaking an old woman's wrist, which the entire station has discussed.*

The gallery noise dropped about half a step.

::: fathom
Tomorrow, then. If you are still curious.
:::

"Oh yeah. Tomorrow. I'll be here — got nowhere else to go."

::: fathom
Tomorrow.
:::

A pause, and then, with the air of someone conceding a great deal:

::: fathom
Vantage. Since you did not ask, which was correct of you.
:::

And it went straight up and out through the gap under the next deck, where there is presumably a conduit run and a column of moving air and something that passes, in here, for a sky.
