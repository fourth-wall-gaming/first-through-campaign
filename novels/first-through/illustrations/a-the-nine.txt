SCENE. The anchor hall at the Nine: the waist of a rotating cylinder two
kilometres long, Threshold home station at Sirius. A single wide documentary
photograph from a gallery one level up, looking down and along.

THE FLOOR. Nine metal rings set flush into worn deck plate in a shallow arc,
each four metres across, each with its own queue of people waiting with
crates, bags and children. Eight of the queues are moving. The deck is scuffed
to a shine along the walking lines and filthy in the corners.

THE SHAPE OF THE PLACE. It is a cylinder and the picture must say so: the
floor curves UPWARD away from the camera on both sides, carrying its lamps,
gantries and galleries up and over until the far side is overhead. Six levels
of walkway and handrail stacked up each flank. No horizon.

THE PEOPLE. Mostly teenagers in close-fitting matte cloth suits, long-sleeved
and long-legged, like plain soft working clothes — and every one a different
colour, dyed to taste, deep and saturated, with one small plain Threshold
flash at the left shoulder. The hall is full of colour at child height and
grey at adult height. They move with purpose, carrying nothing much. A tallyman at the head of each
queue with a board against his chest. Two children at the bottom of a stair
with a towel and a flask, waiting for somebody. Adults in ordinary clothes,
queuing, out of place.

DETAIL. High on the gallery wall above the hall, a slate board four metres
wide, ruled in chalk with a line for every door in human space, and an
eleven-year-old on a stool beside it with a rag. Ramps where there should be
stairs. Duct grilles cut to a very fine mesh.

LIGHT. Hard blue-white raking in from high ports — Sirius, which no filter
quite tames — fighting warm sodium from the working lamps on every gantry.
Dust in the beams.

STYLE. Reportage. The photographer is standing on a walkway in a working
building. Available light, visible grain, nothing staged.

AVOID. Chrome. Glowing holographic displays. A viewport with a planet in it.
Sleek curves. Anybody posing. Lens flare. Making it look like a spaceport
concourse from a film.
