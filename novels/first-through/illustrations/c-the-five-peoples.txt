SCENE. The low gallery at the Nine at dusk, which is where everybody under
about twenty ends up when their shift is done. A walkway along the open edge
of the gallery void: handrail on one side, a long bench against the wall on
the other, a hotplate at the end of it. Past the rail, forty metres of open
air and the lit galleries of the far side.

One wide documentary photograph, eye level, mid-distance. All five of the
peoples are in it, at their true sizes against the people they work with, and
not one of them is being looked after.

THE PEOPLE. Five or six first-throughs, twelve to twenty, sprawled over the
bench and the deck with food. Close-fitting matte cloth suits, long-sleeved
and long-legged, each dyed a different deep colour, with one small plain
Threshold flash at the left shoulder — and on the older ones the dye visibly
bleached pale across the shoulders and up both forearms. Mismatched boots.
They are eating and arguing and nobody is posed.

THE GLINT, foreground. Small enough to close a hand around — eight
centimetres, faceted, hard-shelled — settled on a girl's collarbone where her
neck meets her shoulder, obviously comfortable and obviously there by its own
choice. It is MID-FLASH: lit from inside, throwing real coloured light up onto
her jaw and the underside of her chin, and that is the only strong colour in
the frame. She is not looking at it. She is looking at whoever she is arguing
with. Four more glints are strung along the handrail behind her at different
colours, plainly talking to each other over everybody's heads.

THE FATHOM, mid-ground, out past the rail. A metre across, hanging unsupported
in open air with nothing holding it up: a thin translucent rippling membrane,
a jellyfish bell crossed with a sheet of oiled paper, almost weightless, its
edge curling slowly as it reads the air. COMPLETELY EYELESS — no eyes, no eye
spots, no sensory cluster that reads as a face, nothing on it turned towards
the camera. It is out there because the void is the only part of this station
with moving air in it. Nobody on the bench is paying it any attention and it
is plainly listening to all of them.

THE CAIRN, left, on the deck beside the hotplate. Low, dense and unhurried,
half a metre across and about the mass of a full toolbag — the silhouette of a
footstool built out of dressed stone. No visible limbs in use, no face, no
eyes. Somebody has put a cup down on it without thinking about it and somebody
else is sitting leaned against it. It has been in that exact position for six
hours and will be for six more.

THE SIXLIMB, right, up on a crate at head height so it can be heard.
Cat-sized, half a metre long, SIX LIMBS and FOUR OF THEM ARE HANDS — two
carrying its weight, two holding the thing it is eating, two in the middle of
a gesture. Nothing feline: no muzzle, no whiskers, no tail, no face that reads
as human or as a pet. A boy is turned fully towards it, mid-sentence,
answering it. Everything in a sixlimb's culture is conversation and this one
is winning.

THE SCATTER, background, leaving. About forty small hard-shelled units, each
the size of a plum, moving as one loose shoal along the top of the handrail —
a clear centre of mass and NO LEADER anywhere in it. A third of the group has
already broken off and is streaming into a duct grille in the wall, cut to a
fine mesh exactly wide enough to pass them and nothing larger. That grille is
why the architecture is the shape it is, and every deck on this station has
one.

SCALE, which is the point of the picture. Against a standing fifteen-year-old:
the glint is the size of her fist, the sixlimb would sit in her lap, the cairn
comes to her knee, the fathom is as wide as her outstretched arms, and the
scatter is forty things the size of plums.

DETAIL. A ramp instead of a stair at the end of the walkway. One corner of the
gallery left deliberately unlit. Food wrappers. A towel over the rail.

LIGHT. Warm low gallery lamps doing most of the work, cold spill coming up out
of the void from below, and the glint's own light on the girl's face. Two
colour temperatures disagreeing. Dust.

STYLE. Reportage — a photographer who sat down with these people for an hour
and took this near the end of it. Available light, visible grain, nobody
posing, two or three people cut off by the frame.

AVOID. Anybody holding, carrying, cradling, stroking or feeding one of them.
Leashes, harnesses, cages, perches, carriers. Shoulder-parrot poses. Anybody
gazing fondly down at one. Bug eyes, mandibles, tentacles, humanoid posture,
or a face on anything that has no face. Making the scatter read as a swarm or
a hazard — it is a committee. Making the sixlimb read as a cat. Anything cute,
anything mascot-like, anything that looks like a pet. Glowing eyes. Anybody
looking at the camera.
