SCENE. A field-guide plate: the five peoples of the Causeway, all five on one
sheet, each at a consistent scale against a plain warm-grey ground with a fine
scale bar beside it. Muted gouache technical illustration of the kind a
natural-history field guide uses — observed, labelled, unglamorous. Not a
group portrait and not a lineup of characters: five specimens, drawn by
somebody who has watched each of them for a long time.

1. A SIXLIMB. Cat-sized. Six limbs, FOUR OF THEM HANDS and the other two
carrying the weight. Built low and quick. Draw it mid-conversation, two hands
occupied with something and two gesturing, because everything in a sixlimb's
culture is talking and one that has gone quiet is one in trouble. No face that
reads as human. No whiskers, no muzzle, nothing feline despite the size.

2. A FATHOM. Roughly a metre across, hanging unsupported in mid-air with no
visible means of doing so: a thin translucent rippling membrane, a jellyfish
bell crossed with a sheet of oiled paper, almost weightless. COMPLETELY
EYELESS — no eyes, no eye spots, no sensory cluster that reads as a face. It
has never been interested in seeing. Show the membrane edge curling as it
reads the air.

3. A CAIRN. Low, dense and unhurried, about the mass of a full toolbag. The
silhouette of a footstool built out of dressed stone. No visible limbs in use, no
face, no eyes. Draw it beside a hotplate, settled, as if it has been in that
exact position for six hours and will be for six more. It should look like a
piece of masonry somebody has grown fond of.

4. A GLINT. Small enough to close a hand around. Faceted, hard, iridescent,
built to shed radiation from a sun that tries to kill it twice a day. Draw
THREE of them at once in a short sequence to show the only thing that matters
about them: mirror-bright, mid-colour, and absolute black. Their language is
light and it is theatrical by grammar. One of them is upside down.

5. A SCATTER. Not one animal — about forty small hard-shelled units moving as
one thing, drawn as a loose shoal with a clear centre of mass, and a third of
the group already breaking away toward the edge of the plate on its own
business. Hard-vacuum tolerant. Nothing in the shoal is in charge.

LETTERING. A small caption block under each specimen with its name, its home
system and a scale reference. Engraved small capitals, horizontal.

STYLE. Hard-SF xenobiology, plausible and unglamorous. Muted palette, matte,
no rendering gloss, no background scenery. Lit evenly and flatly except the
glints, which are lit by themselves.

AVOID. Bug eyes. Tentacles. Mandibles. Faces on anything that has no face.
Humanoid posture. Anything cute, anything mascot-like, anything that looks
like a pet. Glowing eyes. Making the scatter look like insects, or a swarm
with menace in it — they are a committee, not a hazard.
