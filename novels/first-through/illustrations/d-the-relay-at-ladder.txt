SCENE. A sealed shed on Ladder, a survey post on a rock a hundred and ninety
million kilometres out. Concrete apron, red rock dust over everything, a
welded roof with no sky. The relay is running and has been for hours: this is
how sixty-one people are coming off that rock, one at a time, in a child's
arms.

THE RING. Set into the concrete, a metal ring four metres across, lit. Two
metres clear of it, a line drawn on the slab in pen, and beyond that a raw
fresh cut edge where the slab was sawn through that morning. Four metres off,
a fuel bowser nose-down in a pit the size of a bunkroom, exactly where they
dropped it to get it off the ring. A sheet of cut deck plate leaning to one
side, propped, where it was used as a dust shield.

THE ACTION, CENTRE. A first-through has just come up out of the ring with an
adult in her arms and is setting them down — the carrier young and visibly
spent, the passenger old and cooperating, both of them talking. Behind them,
a line of adults waiting along the wall under the lamp hall doors, not
arguing, in an order somebody decided.

LINT, foreground left, a metre from the ring, where she can see a carrier's
face and reach their shoulder. Fifteen, membrane clouded to the elbow, filthy
to the knees, hoarse. She is not carrying anybody. Her hand is half-raised and
she is saying the only thing her job consists of, loudly, to the carrier and
not to the passenger: put them down.

RILL, mid-ground right, twenty-one, broader, sleeves pushed up and her
forearms WHITE rather than clouded — that is the whole of her character and
it must read. She is carrying a man and she is walking past the front of the
line to do it. She is not looking at Lint. Lint is looking at her.

THE REST. Two people with brooms keeping grit out of the ring. A foreman in
her forties with a cutting torch set down beside her and a board in her hand,
watching the ring rather than the people. A row of children against the far
wall, already waiting their turn, with their names written on their hands.

LIGHT. One bad orange strip overhead doing all the work, and the cold spill
off the lit ring underneath it. Dust in both. Deep shadow everywhere else.

STYLE. Disaster-relief photojournalism. Mid-distance, available light, high
grain. Everybody is tired and nobody is posing. The picture is about a queue
working, not about a rescue being heroic.

AVOID. Dramatic rays of light from the ring. Anybody carrying somebody in a
heroic bridal pose. Tears. A crowd reaching out. Rubble and fire. Making it
look like an evacuation poster. Clean clothes.
