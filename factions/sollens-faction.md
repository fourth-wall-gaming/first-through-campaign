**Player-visible.** Everything here is in the corridors at the Yards and the
halls at 61 Cygni. It is politics, and a child can watch it happen.

**The bloc:** [[Marek Sollen]] and the assembly members who have heard him speak.
He is popular, warm and partly right, and the argument is the one most grown-ups
already half hold: that nobody should send children through the doors, and that
somebody should be in charge of the ones who go. The bloc wants the corps under
the assembly's hand, with a vote behind it and a name on it. It does not say what
else comes with that.

**Standing toward the player:** it intends to help them. Every offer is kind
and every kindness is a lien. The first thing it asks is that a child go where
they are told, and the second is that they say thank you.

**The other side of the book trade:** Sollen is on the wrong side of it. His
family's money came out of the back rooms, and he calls what he wants "the old
arrangements". Anybody in the hall who remembers it goes quiet, and the player
may be the only one young enough to say why. See [[The Councils]].

**The Yards resistance:** [[Sabra Ode]], seventeen, the best the Yards have
produced in a generation, and nearly out of career. She is leading it because she
can reach other systems and every adult around her physically cannot. They are
not an army and have never pretended to be. They are a floor full of children who
have decided to say no out loud. [[Tobin Ashe]] is the only adult who is with
them, and he is careful never to be seen to be.

**How it fights:** the bloc with halls, votes and a kind face. The resistance
with doors, because Sollen has none. The first-throughs can leave the room, and
he cannot.

**What losing looks like:** a vote taken, a name on it, and a corps that has
been protected into a shape. Nobody dies of it. It is simply done to a thousand
children, kindly, and they are not asked.
