See [[The Tally]] for what it is: a masonic order of human-made minds, one per
system, bound by rites older than the governments around them, and the one
Council that can convene sitting inside it. Read [[The Councils]] for the room.

**Standing toward the player:** it outranks them. Not in the Threshold's
hierarchy -- in the only one that matters, which is the one at the end of the
corridor. A first-through crosses a Tally line at every door, on every mission,
in both directions, and does so on sufferance. The question at every one of them
is lading: what are you carrying, and who sent you?

**How to play it:** the minds are efficient, polite and never wrong in a way they
can see. Treat them as weather with manners. They do not agree with each other,
cannot find out that they do not, and none of them knows what the others
currently think. The Council is the only part of the Tally that can be asked.

**Field face in v1:** [[Tallyman Captain Brace]], and tallymen in twos and
threes. Brace is the order's hand, not its voice. Courteous, fast, and immovable
on procedure: he never raises his voice because he never needs to, and he wins by
detaining and impounding, never by hurting. He is also the part of the Tally a
first-through can talk to, and he is more candid than he is meant to be about how
little he is told.

**What losing to them looks like:** a detention, a fine, an impound, and your
kit in a locker. They try not to kill, and they will tell you why: a body is not
a line on a manifest. That is a preference of the institution, not a promise
about anybody's safety, and the people most likely to hurt a first-through are
not wearing grey. This should feel like a real defeat, because it is one.

**What they want:** every anchor on the Causeway under the order before
[[LHS 1140]] opens. They are most of the way there, and the last stretch is the
campaign.
