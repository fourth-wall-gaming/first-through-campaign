# Afterwards

## What the player should come away with

That they won. Say it plainly, because a young player who has spent a night
being frightened needs to be told. Everybody who could be got off was got off,
and the ones who stayed chose to.

That it cost, and that the cost was paid by people who said yes.

And that **they did it by not being the one who carried them all** — which is
the answer this scenario is built around and which most tables never find.

## What is still sitting there

Act One is routinely won with two of its best things unclaimed. Both are still
available afterwards and both should be allowed to be found late.

**The foreman's order.** Tace Orun had a list and it was not youngest first. It
was the ones whose parents are already dead, then siblings together, then
alphabetically, because she could not make herself choose between them and said
so to a hall. Nobody has ever asked her why her list was in that order. She will
tell anybody who asks her properly.

**The two lines.** Ladder has kept the only long-baseline record of the flare
twins in human space — grown in a basin in a shed, keeper by keeper, for two
hundred years. The flare entry and the **door** entry grow from the same
testimony.

The potential reshapes with the orbit. So the record that predicts the flare
predicts the stray. That breaks the campaign's central established lie, in Act
One, found by a fifteen-year-old, and it is far better if they notice it
themselves, with a hand on the lip of the basin, than if anybody points at it.

## Where it ends

Act One closes on the watch at van Maanen's Star reporting something for the
first time in three and a half centuries.

Act Two is Kapteyn's.
