# The Physics, Exactly

You will be asked about this by a player who is paying attention, and the
answers need to be the same every time, because the plot of the whole campaign
is a consequence of them.

## Transit is zero

Instantaneous. Not fast — **zero**. Step onto the plate and you are at the
other end, and the only reason you know is that the air is different.

What can take time is the door **agreeing to open**. That is a separate thing,
it is normally too small to notice, and when it becomes measurable it is the
single most frightening thing in this setting. See *The Hesitation*.

## Only matter crosses, and only a person carries it

No light, no signal, no radio, no data, no cargo that nobody is holding. A
person, and what that person has on them.

Inside a system, radio works perfectly normally. Two stations round the same
star talk four times a day. Players and Games Masters alike forget this, and
it is load-bearing: at Gliese 65 the refinery can warn Ladder by radio in ten
and a half minutes, and cannot warn Sirius at all.

**And therefore nobody can reach you.** A handler briefs on this side and
cannot follow, cannot call, cannot send help, cannot change the order. That is
why a fifteen-year-old's judgement is the whole safety system, and it is why
this campaign gives the player real decisions instead of instructions.

## A mouth is pinned to a potential, not to a place

The load-bearing sentence of the entire setting.

A door's far mouth sits wherever a particular value of gravitational potential
currently is. Mostly the matching surface drifts a few metres in a century.
A **stray** happens when the sum has more than one answer and the mouth jumps
to a different one.

Two consequences the players will meet:

**Straying is predictable**, because potentials reshape with orbits. Nobody
knows this. See the previous chapter.

**A door can move somewhere useful.** In Act One it does. The disaster and the
rescue are the same event, and that is the shape of the act.

## What a crossing costs

Fraying: small, cumulative, irreversible, and visible on the membrane.
Finished at about twenty-two. Carrying an adult costs the carrier a fortnight
off the end of their life, counted; a child costs minutes. Three or four adult
carries in a row finishes anybody.

Never round these down at the table and never let a Non-Player Character be
vague about them. The cost being exact and spoken aloud is what makes Act One's
turn land.

## Twenty years to the light year

The seedships fly at a twentieth of lightspeed, so a door opens twenty years
per light year after launch. Launch was nine hundred and seventy-five years
ago. That one sum fixes the entire map and the entire calendar — change it and
every date in the campaign is wrong.

The frontier is therefore a sphere a little under forty-nine light years out,
advancing one light year every twenty years.
