# Nine Hundred and Seventy-Five Years

The history matters at the table for one reason: it is the strongest piece of
wonder you have, and you can hand it to a player in ninety seconds without
stopping play.

The short version. A civilisation with one star spent everything it had on
ships that could not pay out for nine centuries, and argued about it in public
for two generations, and wrote the argument down — including the losing side,
including the forty years when the losing side was winning. Then they did it,
and then they ran out, and nothing dramatic happened at all. The launches
thinned and stopped because there was nothing left.

That absence is the honest shape of it. Historians hate absences; the good ones
say so and the bad ones invent a catastrophe. Be suspicious of anything that
gives a player a villain here.

Eighty-five years after the launch the first door opened. Then a lifetime of
nothing, then another door. Each one arriving to people who had only heard
about the last one from their grandparents.

**It is the longest-running promise anybody has ever kept, and every single
person who made it was nine centuries dead before it finished being kept.**

That sentence is worth saying once, out loud, at the right moment, and then
never again.
