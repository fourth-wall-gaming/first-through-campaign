# The People

Twenty-two named people and two species entries. Read the card before anybody
speaks — not once at the start of the campaign, **before the scene**. Every
voice problem this campaign has ever had came from running a character off a
memory of a card read three hours earlier.

A card describes **behaviour**. Play the behaviour; never let the character
announce it. If a card says somebody tells you the thing you will like least
first, they do that — they never say *"now the part you won't like."*

## Who owns what

**One character owns precision**, and in this campaign it is the Old Man.
Enumerating, insisting on provenance, asking for things on the record — that is
his characterisation, born of an inquest, and it is forbidden to everybody
else. The fastest way to turn six people into one person is to let them all
start asking for things in writing.

Short register notes, so each is recognisable with the attribution removed:

- **Calloway** concedes a point immediately and completely, then changes her
  reason rather than pretending she had not. Warm, funny, genuinely formidable.
- **Rill** answers in one sentence and changes the subject.
- **Sennet** is declarative and eleven, and treats her job the way an admiral
  treats a ship.
- **Benno Raske** agrees with every objection and does not change.
- **Vantage** holds being right above being comfortable, which is why he is
  arrogant *and* trustworthy: when he is wrong he concedes out loud, at no
  cost, because looking foolish is not a currency he trades in.
- **Mina Haral** pays for an error immediately and in full and then never
  refers to it again.
- **Pell Ivor** defers instinctively and corrects anyway, and you can see the
  effort each time. That gap is the character.
- **Tace Orun** has had one problem for two days and has thought about nothing
  else, and it shows in how fast she answers.
- **Ossuary** expresses himself by position, and washes the same cup to give
  people the privacy of his back.
