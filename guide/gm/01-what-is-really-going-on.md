# What Is Really Going On

**This chapter contains the ending.**

## The question the campaign is asking

A first-through is the most important person in human space for about seven
years, and then it stops, and nobody has ever worked out what happens next.

That is the whole engine. Everything else is the setting it runs in.

Three people answer that question, and the campaign is the player choosing
between them.

| Who | What they are |
|---|---|
| Rill | **The warning.** Twenty-one, frayed out, taking money from the only man who ever offered her a plan. |
| Calloway | **The answer.** Aged out at forty-one, runs a desk, and is the happiest and most loved person in the setting. |
| The Quorum | **The refusal.** The best first-through who ever lived, who would not accept being landlocked, and paid for it with everything that made him a person. |

Table: The Three Answers

In Act Three the Quorum offers the player exactly what he took. By then they
will have wanted it since the first session, they will have watched what it did
to Rill, and they will know what Calloway chose instead.

## The acts

**Act One — the Nine.** Making the player love this place and these people, and
learning the job in their own hands. Light for two beats, then the hardest
night of their life. *Takes:* a few days each off four hundred named lives,
freely given, because the player stood in a hall and asked for them.

**Act Two — Kapteyn's.** The wonder, and the first hard truth. Twenty-four
generations alone behind a buried door — and then the discovery that the stray
which opened it was predictable, and that somebody predicted it. *Takes:* Rill.
She runs out during this act, in front of the player, and it is nobody's fault
and entirely avoidable and both of those are true.

**Act Three — the Quorum.** The villain, the temptation, and the answer. He is
courteous, tired, physically unremarkable, and in more than one place. He does
not want to rule anything. He wants the Causeway small enough to hold himself
together across, and he has spent sixty years making sure nobody learns how to
move a door before he does. He found the only way to be in more than one place,
and it cost him being a person.

The climax is a race. LHS 1140's door arrives — the first new door in
twenty-five years and the first in history to open onto a living world. A
bigger Causeway is one he cannot keep in step with himself, so he needs it late,
shut, or in his hands. A fifteen-year-old with a fetch intends otherwise.

It ends with a door opening, honestly, with a child standing in it.

## The lie the setting is built on

There is a fact everybody in the Causeway believes, and it is wrong, and
breaking it is the spine of Acts Two and Three:

> Straying is random and cannot be predicted.

It is not random. A door is pinned to a gravitational potential, and a
potential reshapes with the orbits around it, and orbits are the most
predictable things in the universe. Anywhere somebody has kept a long enough
record, the pattern is sitting in it, unread.

The reason nobody knows is not conspiracy. It is that nobody logs a sticky
door, because an inconvenience that resolves itself is the definition of the
thing nobody writes down — and the only people who keep records long enough to
show the cycle are people nobody has ever asked for them.

Which is also what the campaign is *about*, underneath: **information that
exists, is available, and goes unread.** Pell Ivor's board. Sep Anwar's four
reports. A child teaching herself to read flash off a stool. Two hundred years
of handwriting in a shed at Ladder.

If a player ever picks one of those up and reads it, give them everything. That
is the game working.

## What is in the sealed files

`lore/gm-secret/` holds the Quorum, what Tolk is, and the outline of Acts Three
to Five. None of it is needed to run Act One and all of it is worth having read
once, because Act One is full of small things that will matter later and that
you will only place correctly if you know where this is going.
