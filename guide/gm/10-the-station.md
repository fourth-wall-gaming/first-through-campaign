# The Nine

Two kilometres of cylinder, turning once every ninety seconds, forty thousand
people, and nine doors meeting at the waist. More doors in one place than
anywhere else in human space, because eight of them are simply Sirius's nearest
neighbours and the ninth is the Garden.

Everything in Act One happens here or one step off it.

## How to run the hall

The anchor hall is the set. Get the player into it in the first ten minutes and
keep coming back.

What makes it alive is **procedure happening around somebody who is not part of
it**: nine queues, eight of them moving; a tallyman at the head of each with a
board; a courier coming up out of a housing already peeling her weave off
her forearms; two hall children at the bottom of a stair with a towel and a
flask, because they know who is due.

The sound of the hall going quiet is the strongest tool you have in this
building, and it is not a sound — it is about nine people stopping being loud
at the same moment. Use it twice in Act One and no more.

## Three details that do the work

**The decks smell like their doors.** The Procyon Stair is a suburb with prams
on the walkway. The Coldwater Run is red light and grain dust and the only
corner of the station that smells of farming. The Wolf Gate queue is shift
workers, the roughest line in the hall, and the best food on the station.

**The architecture is built for five peoples.** Ramps where there should be
stairs, a dim corner in every concourse, and duct grilles cut to scatter gauge
on every deck — a mesh sized so a glint can pass and nothing larger can. There
is a line in every budget argument about them and nobody has ever won it.

**The Standing** is four metres of board on the gallery wall with every door in
human space on it, kept by an eleven-year-old on a stool. Four things on a
line; people read two; the fourth is the watch and it is the only one that
matters.
