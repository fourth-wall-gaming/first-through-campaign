# Fetches, and Who Chooses

## Playing a fetch

<!-- illus: novels/first-through/illustrations/c-the-five-peoples.jpg | All five, off shift, and not one of them being looked after. -->

A fetch is a person, not a feature. The partnership happened because two people
talked constantly about nothing for months, and the talking *is* the bonding.

Three things keep them from collapsing into a pet:

**They have somewhere else to be.** Ask the player's fetch, occasionally, what
*they* want to do today. It is a question almost nobody puts to a fetch on a
working day, and the answer should be specific and slightly inconvenient.

**They are not equipment.** A fetch is not life support, does not fight, and
cannot fix a kit problem. A cairn setting itself around somebody in a hull
breach is a favour from a person, which is much better than a feature.

**They know things you do not.** Their people talk across every hall in the
Causeway, over human heads, constantly. That is a legitimate and bottomless
source of information the player can only get by having a friend.

## The player always chooses the special effect

When a roll earns a special effect, the player picks it. Not you, not a table,
not whichever one is most dramatic.

It is the single best teaching tool in *Mythras* for a new player, because it
turns every good roll into a small design decision and it teaches, in about
four combats, that the rules are a set of choices rather than a set of
outcomes.
