# How the Dice Work

*Mythras Imperative* is a **roll-under** game on percentile dice. Everything
below is the whole of what you need; there is no second layer.

## The basic roll

You have a number for each skill — Crossing 52, Perception 57, Athletics 61.
Roll 1d100. **Equal or under, you succeed. Over, you fail.** That is it.

Roll the tens die and the units die together and read them as a two-digit
number. A roll of 00 is 100, and 100 always fails.

**A critical** is a roll in the lowest tenth of your skill, rounded up —
Crossing 52 criticals on 01–06. Something goes better than it had any business
going.

**A fumble** is a 99 or 00 when you were going to fail anyway. It goes wrong in
a way that costs something.

## Difficulty

Your Games Master does not change the die. They change the number you are
rolling against, and they say which grade they are using out loud before you
roll.

| Grade | Your skill becomes |
|---|---|
| Very Easy | doubled |
| Easy | one and a half times |
| Standard | unchanged |
| Hard | two thirds |
| Formidable | half |
| Herculean | a fifth |

Table: Difficulty Grades

A crossing through a door that is behaving itself is Standard or Easy. A
crossing carrying a grown adult is Hard. A crossing through something that is
actively going wrong can be Formidable, and you will be told so beforehand.

## Passions, and arguing with the dice

Your three passions are not decoration. When one of them is genuinely driving
what you are doing, you can **augment** the roll with it: add a fifth of the
passion's value to the skill before the difficulty grade is applied.

A passion of *I am the one who gets there* at 70 is **+14** on anything that is
honestly about getting there. It is not +14 on picking a lock.

Say which passion and say why. Most Games Masters will say yes, and the saying
why is the good bit.

## Luck

You get three **Luck Points** and they come back each session. Once per action,
you may spend one to:

- **re-roll** the dice entirely, or
- **swap the digits** — a failed 71 becomes a 17.

Swapping is the quiet one and it is usually the better one, because it is
certain where a re-roll is not.

Three is not many. By the end of a bad night you will have none, and knowing
that you have none is part of how you decide what to attempt.

## Getting hurt, and getting tired

Damage lands on a specific part of you and most of what happens in this
campaign is wrenches, cracks and cold rather than wounds. You will know how
badly you are hurt because you will be told plainly.

Crossing repeatedly in a day makes you **tired**, and tired is mechanical: your
numbers come down. Nobody has ever made six crossings in a day and felt fine
afterwards.

## If you want the actual rulebook

You do not need it. Everything above is enough to play, and your Games Master
has the rest.

But if you are the sort of person who wants to see the whole machine,
*Mythras Imperative* is free — The Design Mechanism publish the PDF on their
[free downloads page](https://thedesignmechanism.com/free-downloads/). It is a
complete game in about sixty pages, and the combat chapter in particular is
worth reading once you have played a fight.

## The only piece of advice that matters

Ask questions before you act, out loud, as your character or as yourself. What
can I see? How far is it? Can this kill me?

You will get a straight answer every time. That is the deal at this table, and
using it is not timid — it is exactly what a first-through is trained to do,
and it is the entire difference between the ones who come back and the ones who
do not.
