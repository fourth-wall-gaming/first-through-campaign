# The Five Peoples, and Your Fetch

Four of the twenty-eight systems had somebody already in them. A fifth turned
up later. None of them are humanoid, none of them are hostile, and all five of
them cross doors free, because nobody has ever found a way to charge them and
the attempt has been made.

<!-- illus: novels/first-through/illustrations/c-the-five-peoples.jpg | The low gallery at dusk. All five, and not one of them being looked after. -->

**Sixlimbs**, behind the TRAPPIST-1 door. **Fathoms**, from the cloud decks of
Luhman 16 — eyeless, unsupported, and they read a room by what its fields are
doing rather than by looking at it. **Cairns**, out of the shelters of Tau
Ceti, who go mineral-rigid in four seconds and are immovable for hours.
**Glints**, from the flare twins at Gliese 65 — hand-sized, faceted, built to
shed energy, who talk in overlapping pulses of colour at a speed no mouth can
manage. **Scatters**, from the belts of Epsilon Eridani, who are hard-vacuum
tolerant and can send a third of themselves somewhere you cannot go.

## Your fetch

A **fetch** is one of them who has decided to work with you.

Not a pet, not equipment, not a familiar, and emphatically not a feature of
your character sheet that you spend points on. A person, with their own
culture, their own opinions about your decisions, and somewhere else they could
be. They cross with you because they want to. They can stop wanting to.

The partnership is not formal and nobody signs anything. What happens is that
you talk — constantly, for months, about nothing — and at some point it is a
partnership and neither of you can say which week it became one.

They will not fight for you, they are not your life support, and if you are
walking into vacuum in the wrong skin your fetch cannot fix that. What they
*can* do is be somewhere you are not, notice something you cannot notice, tell
you the truth when nobody else in the room will, and be extremely pleased to
see you.

A glint on your collarbone will also be insufferable in front of strangers, and
that is the single most reliable fact in this setting.

## And they are better connected than you are

Worth knowing, because it changes how you play.

Your fetch has their own people in every hall on the Causeway, and they talk to
each other constantly, over your head, in a language almost no human can read.
A glint knows what happened at Procyon this morning. You do not.

They also carry for free, and nobody searches them, and a species whose tissue
can hold data is a species that every government in human space has at some
point had a quiet think about.

Which makes you, specifically, the weak link in a great many plots. Nobody has
ever got anything out of a fetch by asking. Several people have tried getting
it out of the human.
