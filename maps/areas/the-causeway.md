A single astronomical chart sheet: the whole human-inhabited galaxy drawn as a
**three-dimensional graph of doors**, not as a map of space. Ink-on-linen
draughtsmanship, engraved line, the register of an admiralty chart or a mining
survey rather than a modern infographic. Warm paper, black and sepia linework,
one muted accent for the network itself.

**TWO PLATES ON ONE SHEET.** The scale demands it: twenty-two of the
twenty-eight systems lie within seventeen light years of Sol, and the remaining
six sit alone on a shell at forty to forty-nine. There is nothing in between.

**MAIN PLATE — the full sphere, forty-nine light years.** Occupies the left
three-quarters of the sheet.

The **galactic plane** is drawn as a ruled horizontal grid in perspective — a
flat lattice of faint squares receding to a vanishing point, seen from about
twenty degrees above. Concentric rings are scribed on that plane at ten, twenty,
thirty and forty light years, labelled at their right edge, with the outermost
ring at **48.75 light years** drawn heavier and lettered THE FRONTIER.

Every star is a small engraved point **held off the plane by a fine vertical
dropline** down to a tick on the grid, so its height above or below the plane is
read directly. This is the whole device of the drawing: the droplines are what
make it three-dimensional.

**SOL** sits at the exact centre of the grid, on the plane, a small sunburst.
The inner twenty-two systems crowd close around it — a dense knot roughly a
third of the way out, deliberately drawn tight and left mostly unlabelled here,
with a square bracket keyed to the inset.

The outer six hang far out and wide apart, each on a long dropline:

- **TRAPPIST-1** and **LHS 1140** well *below* the plane, LHS 1140 the most
  extreme object on the sheet — almost vertically beneath Sol at the very limit
  of the frontier ring, on the longest dropline in the drawing, its point marked
  with an open circle rather than a filled one and a small cartouche reading
  NOT YET OPEN.
- **GJ 1214** far out and slightly above the plane.
- **HD 40307** below the plane and on the far side.
- **55 CANCRI** above the plane.
- **HD 69830** close to the plane, far out.

The **doors** are the network: fine ruled lines joining star to star, each a
double hairline, each with a small open lozenge at its midpoint. Five long
straight runs go from Sol directly out to the frontier six — conspicuously the
longest lines on the sheet, drawn thinner and lighter, like old routes nobody
maintains. Everything else is short and tangled and lives in the inner knot.

**INSET PLATE — the Inner Reach, seventeen light years.** A framed rectangular
inset in the lower right quarter, its own smaller perspective grid of the
galactic plane, same drawing conventions, roughly three times the scale.

**SIRIUS is the hub of this plate and of the whole network.** Nine door-lines
converge on it — more than any other system, more even than Sol, which holds
seven. Letter it SIRIUS — THE NINE and draw a small nine-pointed rosette behind
its point. Its nine lines run to Sol, Procyon and WISE 0855 (all three very
short), Luhman 16, Epsilon Eridani, Wolf 359, Alpha Centauri, Gliese 65 and
40 Eridani.

Within the inset the vertical spread is the point: **Wolf 359, Lalande 21185 and
Ross 128 stand high above the plane** on long upward droplines, while **Gliese
65, Tau Ceti, YZ Ceti and Gliese 887 hang far below it**. Sirius, Sol, Alpha
Centauri and 61 Cygni sit close to the plane itself. Tau Ceti and YZ Ceti are so
close together that their two points nearly touch, joined by the shortest
door-line on the sheet.

**Lettering.** Engraved small capitals, horizontal, each name on a fine leader
line to its star so no label sits on the linework. A title cartouche across the
top. A scale bar in light years along the bottom of each plate. A compass rose
marked not with north but with GALACTIC CENTRE and an arrow for the direction of
galactic rotation.

**Do not draw:** nebulae, dust clouds, spiral arms, starfields, lens flare, any
glow or bloom, colour gradients, planets, orbits, ships, or any painted or
rendered sky. No photographic space art of any kind. This is a drafted chart of
a graph, on paper. Empty paper is correct; the void is not decorated.
