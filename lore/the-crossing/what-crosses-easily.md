A door does not move objects. It takes a living thing apart and lays it down
again, and that is the only trick it has ever had.

**Nothing manufactured crosses.** Not a crate, not a tool, not a coupling
assembly, not a coin, not a signal. People established this in the first month of
the first door and have been re-establishing it ever since, because every
generation produces somebody convinced they have found the exception.

The exception is a living thing, and living things come in more kinds than
people expect.

## You cross in your weave

What goes through with you is your **weave**: the close-fitting garment that was
grown rather than woven, and that is alive. It is the one part of your kit no
station ever issued you: it is grown on you, and it stays yours. You step onto the plate in it and you step
off the plate in it, and nothing about the crossing asks for anything else except your
**boots**, which are grown too. Each station grows you a pair in its own cut, you
keep them, and they cross on your feet, which is why a first-through can read a
stranger's last three postings off the way their boots are made.

That is also why a Threshold flash *grows into* the cloth instead of being sewn
onto it. The weave takes a mark the way skin does. A thing that was never alive
could not carry one through. See [[The Weave]].

It has been this way since the first pair. A courier does not arrive on the far
side of a door with nothing, because the far side of a door is sometimes vacuum,
and sometimes cold enough to stop a heart in a minute and a half, and nobody
has ever surveyed it first. That is the job.

## The kit is grown too

The **pressure-skin**, the plated **suit** and the **sensor hood** are grown from
the same stock as the weave. They are alive, so they cross. They go through the
door on you, and they come back through it on you, and they are the reason a
first-through steps into an unlit station and does not simply die in the first
ten metres. See [[A First-Through's Kit]].

They are also **Threshold property**. They are issued, and they are returned. When
a courier says *the Nine's suit, stays at the Nine*, that does not mean the suit
cannot leave the Nine. It has left it more often than most people have. It means
it was never yours, and that at the end of the run somebody signs for it and it
goes back in its tray with an inch of feed. The weave is yours. Your boots are issued
per station and kept. The skin, the suit and the hood are on loan, and the loan
is heavy, and the tallymen count it.

Nobody has ever carried a keepsake between stars that was not alive. In nine
centuries the courier songs have been mostly about that.

## Freight does not cross

Not a coupling assembly in a hold. Not bar stock. Not grain, not ore, not
anything by the tonne or by the crate. A door passes a living thing in its
grown kit, and every other kind of object is just a thing standing at the edge
of a plate.

Which means there is no army. You cannot move a force between
stars, so nobody has ever invaded anybody: a hall is only ever taken from the
inside, by people already standing in it.

Nor is there any trade. Not expensive trade. **None.** No convoy has ever
run between stars and none ever will. A world eats, breathes, wears, heats and
repairs itself out of what it has, or it does without, and every system in human
space is supplied from inside itself.

Coldwater cannot be sent a coupling assembly. Not refused one -- *sent* one. The
word on the requisition list is a courtesy.

## So the memory is a person

And yet everything humanity knows is in every system, and a child crossing
carries more of it than a library.

Because information does not have to be dead. A living thing holds it in its
cells and a living thing crosses. The living things are the **five peoples**, and
the one beside every first-through is their **fetch** -- see [[Your Fetch]].

A fetch observes. A fetch records, and recalls, and decides what to share and
with whom. The cells that hold a thing perfectly for a lifetime were doing it
long before anybody arrived to notice, on five worlds, for their own reasons.
It is a faculty, like a good ear. It is not a service anybody performs on them.

**Nobody writes to a fetch.** Not with a machine, not with a needle, not with
anything. When one arrives in a system, it reads the minds that run the place
with its own senses, directly, and when it leaves it gives out what it chose to
carry to whoever it chooses. Humans ask, and receive, and use. We are the
peripheral in that arrangement, and the sooner a new first-through works that out
the more pleasant the next few years are.

It was not always understood. For centuries the trade was in **books** -- fetch
people who had never been bonded to anyone, kept warm and fed, handed from
station to station and drawn on by strangers, and called animals by everybody
who profited. It was slavery, and it ended within living memory. The first thing
the five peoples did with their freedom was take the whole business of knowing
things off us. See [[The Councils]].

**And a fetch cannot cross alone either.** Something has to open the way, which
means every library in human space moves beside a child, and has for nine hundred
years.

Which is stranger than it sounds, because it is the same strangeness twice. A
first-through's body takes what a crossing does to it and builds around the
damage. A fetch's body takes what happens around it and keeps it. One carries,
one keeps, neither can do the other's half, and the two species evolved forty
light years apart with no ancestor in common.

And it is not one coincidence. There are **five** such species that anybody has
found, on five worlds with nothing in common, and every one of them fits us.

Nobody designed this. The xenobiologists call it **the correspondence** and have
had no theory for two hundred years. Most people never think about it. The ones
who do tend to stop sleeping properly.

There is a whole profession of asking, and another of checking what you were
told, and the two trades do not like each other. A fetch can be mistaken. A fetch
can be lied to by a mind that wanted it to carry something false. Something that
looks exactly like knowledge can be got into the wrong ears, and there have been
wars about it.

## The bottleneck was never capacity

A genome holds an absurd amount. At the scales anybody actually cares about,
the limit on what a fetch can carry is not the fetch -- it is whether there is a
first-through going that way this month.

So the Causeway has never had a *bandwidth* problem. It has a **courier**
problem, and it always will, and every argument about information on this
network is really an argument about somebody's rota.

Which is also why a fetch is quietly the most important piece of infrastructure
in human space. A fetch crosses at no burden to anybody, it has its own tissue,
and it is a person who can consent -- and that combination means a single glint
on a child's collarbone can carry the complete records of a civilisation and
walk through nine doors in a week.

Everyone who has ever wanted to move something they should not have moved has
worked this out. See [[Playing Fetches]]. Nobody at the Nine says it out loud,
because the five peoples are citizens and the sentence *your friend is a cargo
hold* is not one you get to say twice.

**And you can send the weights without sending the process.** A running mind
cannot cross -- it is not a living thing that can carry. A *description* of one
can. The minds have corresponded this way for centuries, at twenty-year round
trips, and nobody has ever been entirely comfortable about it. See
[[The Minds]].

## Everyone knows everything. Nobody has anything.

Which is the shape of the Causeway, stated plainly.

Every system has the same science, the same medicine, the same designs, the same
histories, mostly the same songs — because all of that fits in an animal and the
animal can walk. A child in the sunless cloud decks of Luhman 16 and a
child in the Coldwater grain are taught from the same text, and are.

And they live in entirely different worlds, because **knowing how to make a
coupling assembly is not the same as having one.** You need the ore, the plant,
the power and the hands. Barnard's has four. Coldwater has a council of five and
some very good soil.

Nobody is hoarding anything. There is no secret. There is only the gap between a
drawing and an object, and a door that will not pass the difference.

## Nothing crosses alone

A door is not a door until a first-through is standing in it.

The ring does nothing by itself. Walk at an unattended anchor and you walk into
cold housing and bruise your face. What makes a crossing happen is a living mind
that can take the crossing and hold it open while it is happening, and that is
the whole of what a first-through is **for**.

So every transit in human space has a child in it. Every memory, every message,
every passenger, every treaty, every piece of news for nine hundred years has
gone through in the company of somebody between twelve and twenty who opened the
way and paid for it out of themselves.

### Passengers

A first-through can take somebody through with them -- one person, in contact.
The crossing is instant, as every crossing is; what takes minutes is persuading
the door to open for two at all.

The cost falls on the carrier, not the carried, and it is heavy. It is also the
reason envoys are rare, assassination is the only way anybody reaches across
stars, and every conspiracy in human space needs a child who will agree to it.
See [[Carrying]].

### What that actually means

The limit on a door is not the door. It is the children.

A system's entire traffic with the rest of the species -- its news, its science,
its diplomacy, its rescues, its wars -- is capped by how many crossings its
first-throughs can afford to spend. And every one of those crossings comes out of
a named person's remaining years.

Follow the Tally's tolls all the way down and they are a tax on how long a
particular child is going to live.

Nobody puts it like that. Everybody has worked it out.

## Where the money actually is

What a fetch tells copies. Once it lands, that system has it forever and so does everyone
they tell. So nobody sells knowledge — **they sell being first.**

Value lives entirely in the gap between a thing being true somewhere and being
known here, and that gap is exactly as long as a child takes to walk it.

Which is why [[The Tally]] is the real power and the tolls are the least of it.
They stand at every door. They do not need to own the news; they only need to see
it half a day before anyone else, and they always do.

## And what that makes a courier

The entire nervous system of the species is children, walking, one at a time,
walking beside friends who remember everything we know.

Adults have been trying to think of a way round it for nine hundred years. There
is not one. There is only the next kid through the door, in a weave and a borrowed suit,
with a friend on their collarbone.
