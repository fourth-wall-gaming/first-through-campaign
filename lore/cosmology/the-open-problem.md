**Nobody can make a door stray, and nobody can make one go dark.**

People ask, mostly the ones who have lost a door. They ask whether it could be
done on purpose, and what it would take, and the honest answer is that nobody
knows enough to say what the question is.

A grash happens. Nobody does one. The difference is the whole of this page, and
it is not a difference of nerve or of money. It is that the thing is not yet a
procedure. It is an **open problem**, which is what scientists call a question
that is real, and hard, and unanswered, and sitting just past the edge of
everything anybody can do.

Three things stand in the way.

## 1. The mathematics does not exist

A mouth sits at a gravitational potential. See [[Straying]]. That potential is a
sum over every mass in the system, each weighted by how far away it is, plus a correction for the way
gravity bends time, and every term in it is moving.

Given a mouth, you can write the sum down. What nobody can do is **solve it
forward**: say where the matching surface will be next year, or whether there
will be a second one, or where the mouth will go when there is. Every stray in
nine hundred and seventy-five years was explained afterwards. None was predicted.
Nobody can predict where a mouth will go, only that it went.

And you cannot steer what you cannot predict. To make a mouth go somewhere on
purpose you would first have to know the shape of the sum well enough to see
which small change gives it a second answer, and then which change picks *which*
second answer. That is the mathematics nobody has. It is not a hard calculation.
It is a calculation nobody has worked out how to set up.

## 2. The mass budget is planetary

Suppose the mathematics turned up tomorrow. The potential is made of mass, and a
second solution needs the sum to change by enough to matter.

Not a little. The wells that pin a mouth are the wells of whole worlds and
stars, and whatever it would take to push one door's sum past the point where it
branches is, on the best rough estimates anybody has dared to write down, **a
planet's worth of moving**. No station can shift that. No government in human
space could pay for it. A tug and a very good day will move an asteroid; an
asteroid is a rounding error.

This obstacle is the one people mention first, because it sounds like safety.
It is not. It is only an estimate made without the mathematics, and an estimate
made without the mathematics can be wrong in either direction. The first
obstacle is the one that decides how big the second one is.

## 3. You cannot measure the inside of a mouth

This one is the campaign.

To work out how a mouth decides, you would want to watch it decide, with
instruments, from the inside of a housing, while it is deciding. Everyone who
has tried has met the same wall: **nothing dead crosses.** A clock does not
cross. A sensor does not cross. A probe built by the best hands in the Causeway
goes up to the frame and stops, because the frame will not take it.

So in nine hundred and seventy-five years there has never been an instrument in
a housing. The best data anybody has comes from outside, from stations timing
the draw on a door, and a station mind can time the draw and cannot time the
hesitation from the inside of it. See [[The Hesitation]].

There is exactly one kind of thing that can go inside a door and come back with
a number, and it is not a machine.

**The only probe that can go inside a door is a child.**

A first-through who has the Listen can put her hand to a frame and feel the
slope of the field around an unsettled mouth. See [[The Knacks]]. It is the only
reading from the inside that anybody has ever got. Which means that the people
the Causeway thinks of as couriers, the ones who carry its ladings, are the
only ones in human space who can make the measurement the science is waiting
for.

## The thesis

Put the three together and you get the strangest thing about the problem.

**The one problem humanity must solve is the one problem it is physically
incapable of collaborating on.**

Collaboration is minds meeting minds. It is a room, and a whiteboard, and
somebody saying *no, wait, that's wrong*, and somebody else saying *then try
it with the term you dropped*. It is how every hard problem humanity has ever
solved was solved.

And no mind has ever met another mind across a door. Each system is a room that
cannot be entered by a signal, a clock, or a camera. Nothing manufactured
crosses. Nothing dead crosses. What crosses is a living thing: a child, and
whatever living thing the child has agreed to carry. Data moves at **courier
speed**, which is the speed of one person walking through one door with a fetch
at their side, arriving the same afternoon.

So a mathematician at one star and a physicist at another cannot talk. A mind
composes its thinking and gives it to a fetch who agrees to bear it, and a child walks that **lading** through. It arrives months
out of date, in a living thing in a child's hands, and the reply goes back the
same way. By the time either has read the other, both have moved on, and the
two copies of the argument have drifted. The theory needs people at twenty-eight
systems to check each other. The measurements need to be taken inside
twenty-eight housings. Nothing joins them but a child willing to carry a friend.

Except that a fifteen-year-old can walk from one to the next in an afternoon and
remember what was said.

**A fifteen-year-old is the only thing in human space that can carry a
collaboration between minds that cannot meet.**

## Why this is not hopeless

It is not, because every piece of it is within reach.

The testimony exists, held in twenty-eight systems' minds and never once
assembled, plus one town at [[van Maanen's Star]] that has the longest unbroken
run of it in a single place. The mathematics is hard in the way a hard thing is hard before the
first person sees how to set it up: there are people who think they can see how
to set it up. The reading from the inside exists, as of this year, because
somebody sat on a deck and timed a door.

None of it is finished, and none of it is out of reach. It is the sort of thing
that could break open next year, if the right data reached the right mind, if
the right child stood in the right housing and held still for the right minute,
and the people who understand that are, at this moment, racing.

Nobody has it. Everybody who would use it badly knows exactly how little they
have. So far that is the only reason the doors are still open.
