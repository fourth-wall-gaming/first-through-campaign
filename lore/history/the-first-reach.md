Nine hundred and seventy-five years ago, a civilisation that we are not, and
cannot match, threw thousands of robot ships at the sky.

That is the one sentence everybody agrees on.

**What is well recorded.** The launch itself, in extraordinary detail. The
First Reach was rich in a way that is difficult to credit — it built ships by
the thousand, at a twentieth of lightspeed, each carrying one half of an anchor
pair, and it aimed them at every star within reach with no expectation of seeing
any of them arrive. Nobody alive at the launch could have lived to use a single
door. They did it anyway.

They also moved the **mates** around. Half of every pair had to sit somewhere,
and the First Reach spent two centuries distributing mates among its early
nodes by slow ship — which is why the Causeway is a web rather than a wheel, and
why [[Sirius, and the Nine]] holds nine doors while Sol holds seven.

**What is not recorded.** Why they stopped.

There was no war that anybody can find. No plague in the records, no collapse in
the accounts, no final message. The launches simply thin out over about sixty
years and then end, and the civilisation that made them goes quiet in its own
archives — not destroyed, as far as anyone can tell. Just finished.

The Threshold's official position is that this is an open historical question of
no operational relevance.

Everyone who has worked a failing anchor for ten years has a private opinion
about it, and almost all of those opinions are some version of the same
uncomfortable thought: *they knew something about these machines that we do
not.*
