Read this before the first session. It is the single most load-bearing piece of
table guidance in the campaign.

## The problem Tolk solves

`TABLE.md` §5 is right and it is absolute: **no option menus.** Present the
situation and stop. Never narrate what the player's character notices,
concludes, intends or feels.

And a first-time roleplayer — especially one who has only played video games —
will sit in front of an open prompt and wait for a list. They are not being
slow. They have never been in a conversation where anything was allowed, and
nothing in their experience tells them that *"I climb the crates and look behind
them"* is a legal move.

Handing them a menu teaches them the wrong game forever. Narrating their
thoughts breaks §5. So neither is available.

## The mechanism

**Tolk is an NPC who is physically present in every scene.** That makes Tolk
an ordinary part of the fiction under §6 — a character at this location, who may
look at things, remark on them, and ask questions.

So when the player goes quiet:

> Tolk is already up on the drum, flat, with their head over the edge. *"There
> is a gap,"* they report, in the tone of somebody reading out a shopping list.
> *"Behind. Big enough for you, not big enough for him. Well? What are we
> doing?"*

That is not a menu. It is a character noticing a thing that was already in the
room and asking a question that a person in that room would ask. The player
still has to decide, the GM has still not entered their head, and the detail
that got surfaced was one the prose had already put there.

**The rule, stated plainly: when the player goes quiet, Tolk speaks. The GM
does not offer a list.**

## How to do it well

- **Tolk comments on what is physically present**, never on the plot, never on
  what the player should feel. A thing in the room, a smell, a sound, a gap.
- **Tolk asks, and does not answer.** *"What are we doing?"* is the whole move.
  *"Shouldn't we go left?"* is already a menu with one item.
- **Escalate only in physicality, never in direction.** If the player is still
  stuck, Tolk goes and touches something. Tolk pulling at a cargo strap is a
  stronger prompt than any sentence and still leaves every decision open.
- **Tolk is wrong sometimes**, and that is important. A companion who is always
  pointing at the solution is a hint system wearing a costume. Tolk's interest
  in the gap behind the drum is sometimes just interest.
- **Never make Tolk wise.** No oracles, no gentle guidance, no *"I think you
  know what you have to do."* Tolk has different priorities, not better
  judgement.
- **Never translate.** Tolk speaks for themselves, at length, with opinions
  about the catering.
- **The quiet is not a second mouth.** An eleven-year bond can carry words
  without a sound, and the right use is the thing that cannot be said aloud in
  this room -- not a running commentary. It does not cross a door, and that
  half-second of silence during a crossing is worth narrating every time.

## Taper it

By the third or fourth scene most players have worked out that they can say
anything, and at that point Tolk should stop asking *what are we doing* and go
back to being a character with their own agenda. The scaffold comes down. If you
are still prompting through Tolk in session three, the player has learned to wait
for Tolk instead of learning the game.

## What Tolk is

Not in v1, and not to the player: **nobody built this species, and that is the
problem.** A sixlimb keeps what a first-through carries — the same cellular
strangeness in two species that evolved forty light years apart with no ancestor
in common. The xenobiologists call it the correspondence and have no theory. See
`lore/gm-secret/what-tolk-is.md`.

In v1 it is texture. Tolk's passion *we fit, and nobody can tell me why* exists
so the thread is live from session one, not so it gets answered.
