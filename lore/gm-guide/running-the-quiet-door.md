## The four-way solution matrix

Every beat has a way in for each pre-gen. If a player is stuck, the answer is
never to give them somebody else's route — it is to put their own route back in
the room.

| Beat | The Runner | The Tinker | The Talker | The Fieldhand |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnose the fault | Climbs the mast for the load telemetry | Reads the coupling drift directly | Gets it from the foreman, who has been waiting to be asked | Spots the crew working doubles that the shift book denies |
| Find the jury-rig | Sees the new cable run from above | Knows at a glance it is not on any schematic | Notices nobody will say who built it | Feels the heat coming off the cabinet |
| Get past the foreman | Goes under the floor through the load bed panel | Isolates the coupling from below | Brings three of the five council in | Treats his hands, which are burned, and talks while doing it |
| Make the repair | Holds the collar while it is re-seated | Does it | Keeps the shed clear of helpful people | Keeps the crew upright through a double shift |

## The three non-violent outs, written out

Do not improvise these — a first-time player needs them to be *findable*.

1. **Evidence.** Mast telemetry or the chalk fault slate. The drift is visible
   in both and the foreman can read.
2. **Authority.** The council of five, the holding office, the public
   requisition board. Coldwater governs itself in the open and that is usable.
3. **Physical bypass.** The load bed panel, four bolts short, under the shed
   floor. Reach the coupling from below.

## The line you must not skip

**If the player asks to call for help, the answer is that there is nobody to
call, and that is not a punishment.**

Say it plainly, once, in the fiction — Calloway's last words were on this side
of the door — and then let the player get on with it. A GM who softens this,
who invents a relay or a passing courier, has given away the one thing that
makes the setting frightening and the one thing that makes the player important.

## Pacing for one sitting

- Ready room and kit check: short. Ten minutes.
- The crossing: one paragraph.
- The Landing and the diagnosis: the bulk of it. Let them poke at things.
- The complication and the foreman: the spike.
- The repair: quick, practical, satisfying.
- Debrief: two minutes, and end on the countersignature.

## What not to spend

The countersignature is the end of the session and the end of Act 1. Calloway
does not know what it means. Brace is not in this scenario. Nobody speculates
about the Quorum, nobody has heard the word before, and the player gets no
second clue in this sitting.

If the player pushes, Calloway does the realistic thing: writes it down, says
they will ask, and changes the subject.
