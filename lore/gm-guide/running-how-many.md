Companion to [[Entry Point: How Many]].

## The two mistakes

**1. Do not let the player grind.** The scenario is not an endurance test and the
answer is not more carrying. If they are four carries deep and have not had the
turn, give them the nudge through Tolk and move on. A session spent failing to
find the idea is a session nobody enjoyed.

**2. Do not make the adults pitiable.** They are not frightened, they do not
plead, and they do not push in. They have had two days, they have done the
arithmetic, and they are getting their children across. Dignity is what makes
this land; desperation would make it squalid.

## The fraying clock

Track it openly. The player should feel each one.

| Carry | What it is like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hard. Harder than they expected. They are fine. |
| 2 | Hands shaking at the far end. Fine in ten minutes. |
| 3 | They sit down on the deck plate without deciding to. |
| 4 | Calloway says stop. Does not order it. Means it. |
| 5+ | Allowed. Each one is a real risk and should be rolled as one. |

Nobody has to carry five. If a player insists, let them, make it cost, and do not
punish the courage with a death they did not see coming.

## Four ways in, one per pre-gen

| | How they get there |
|---|---|
| **The Runner** | Fastest across Ladder, finds the nineteen, carries most |
| **The Tinker** | Reads the housing and can say roughly how long is left, which is the single most useful fact in the scenario |
| **The Talker** | Gets the real story out of Tace Orun, including how she chose the order |
| **The Fieldhand** | Knows which children will not survive being carried badly, and in what order they should go |

All four reach the turn. None of them reaches it faster than the others.

## Tace Orun's list

She has ordered the nineteen and will not explain unless asked properly.

It is not youngest first and it is not weakest first. It is **the ones whose
parents are already dead**, then siblings together, then the rest alphabetically
because she could not make herself choose between them and said so to the hall.

If the player asks, tell them. It is the best thing in the scenario and it should
be given, not withheld.

## The relay

Run it as a set piece and let it be loud. Name specific kids. Have the Coldwater
bunkroom turn up purely because the Wolf Gate lot did. Let a twenty-one with
nothing left make two crossings and be helped off the floor.

Calloway runs it from the edge of a hall she cannot enter. Let the player catch
her watching them, once, and say nothing about it.

## What not to spend

Nothing about [[The Quorum]], nothing about who knew. This stray is exactly what
it looks like.

What the session plants is one line from a twenty-one on the gallery, about 602,
and the player's own understanding — earned with their hands — of what being cut
off actually means.
