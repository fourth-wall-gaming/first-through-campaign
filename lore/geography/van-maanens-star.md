**14.1 light years. The door is six hundred and ninety-four years old.**

van Maanen's Star is the nearest white dwarf standing on its own — a dead sun
roughly the size of the Earth, still hot, with nothing in orbit that anybody has
found. It is also a crime scene, and the evidence is in the star's own light.

Run a spectrum and you find **iron**, **calcium** and **magnesium** in the
star's atmosphere. They have no business being there. Heavy elements sink in a
white dwarf, and they sink fast, so anything still visible arrived recently —
which means it is still arriving.

It had planets. It pulled them in, tore them apart, and it is swallowing the
pieces now, and will be for a long time yet.

**What people built here: a town that asks.**

Three thousand people live in the habitats strung around the white dwarf's
rock, and nearly every one of them does the same work. This is where **the
anchor corpus** is kept: six centuries of fault records from twenty-eight
systems, every transit and every test and every stutter of every door on the
Causeway. It is not a wall and it is not a vault. It is held by the town's
**mind**, and the mind has never once been asked a question about anything
else. It also has a school and a hydroponic ring.

They are kept here because nobody visits. That was presented as a security
measure and has worked beautifully as one: a quiet system, two doors, no
politics worth the name, and a dwarf's steady light to work by.

The work is **asking**, and the town is drowning in it. Fault records do not
travel on their own. A fetch reads an anchor's record where it stands, keeps
what it chose to keep, crosses beside a child, and arrives here holding some
part of the state of some door, months out of date, under no obligation to
volunteer any of it. Somebody then has to sit with that fetch and ask well, for
hours, and hand what they get to the mind.

That is the whole trade of three thousand people. There is one fetch in the
corpus house for every forty readers and the readers are the bottleneck,
because asking badly gets you an answer that is true and useless. By the time
one account is got out of one fetch, four more have come through the hall.
Nobody falls behind here. They start behind and stay there, and the long-running
joke is that the corpus is the only thing in human space that is always exactly
as old as the last question anybody thought to ask.

The Threshold keeps a small desk inside it, as it does in every system that
holds a door. **Sep Anwar and Meriel Koll** have held that desk between them for
six years. They did not ask to come and they have both stopped asking to leave.
