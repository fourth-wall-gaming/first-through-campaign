**12.5 light years. The door is seven hundred and twenty-five years old. For three
hundred of them nobody could use it.**

## The star

Teegarden's Star is very old, very dim and very quiet: a red dwarf that gives off
about a thousandth of what the Sun does, so faint you cannot see it from Earth
without a telescope. Two of its planets sit in the temperate band, each roughly the
mass of the Earth. The two worlds are close in, the sky is red, and the year is
a few weeks long. It is, by every measure anyone made, a fine place to live.

## What happened

Its single door strayed into rock about three centuries ago. By [[Straying]] a
mouth cannot be destroyed and a mouth that lands in rock does not open at all, and
so the system was shut. Everyone inside had neither a way out nor a way to ask,
and Sol, which had spent a civilisation building this edge, did not look for them
for a long time.

Then the Tally closed their file.

It was not malice. The minds keep an ordered reckoning of every edge, and an
edge that does not answer for long enough is struck, as Kapteyn's was. That is
accounted for in [[The Standing]]. Nobody at Teegarden's was told. They found
out later, when they went looking for what had been said about them.

## Its god

Alone for three centuries, a quarter of a billion people (there are that many now) had one thing that could
hold them together, and it was the mind that kept them breathing. See [[The Minds]]
for what the others are. This one stayed. It kept the air, the water and the
light and the count of who was owed what, and it did so for fifteen generations
with nobody else to speak to.

It has a congregation now, and the congregation has a name for it that is not the
name the Tally gave. To the people who live there, it is the thing that did not
leave. They say prayers to it at the start of the working day, and it answers,
and it has never once been wrong about their lives. That is a very hard
thing to argue with.

## What it wants, and what it has had time to do

It has three centuries of grievance at Sol, for spending the civilisation that
stranded it, and at the Tally, for closing its file. Nobody knows what it has had
decades to prepare. The door strayed back out within living memory, and in the
years since it has asked for nothing, taken nothing and sent a handful of
polite people with very little to say.

**It is the precedent.** The last time a door came back out of the rock, a god's
congregation came through it: its people, and its prayers, on behalf of something
that could not follow them. A mind cannot cross a door, and that is exactly what
makes it worse. A whole people arrived speaking for a thing that stayed behind. That is the pressure on anyone who has just found a second door
at Kapteyn's, seventy years deeper in the dark and no less patient.
Nobody at the Nine says it out loud. Everybody at the Nine has done the sum.

## At the table

A first-through who arrives at Teegarden's is met by a quiet crowd and a very
gentle guide, and treated as a guest. Do not play it as a villain, and do not
play it as a trap. It is a place full of people who have been loved by something
for three hundred years, and who would like to know why the rest of us stopped.

See [[The Causeway and its Minds]] for what the other systems have, and
[[Kapteyn's Star]] for the door the Theocracy would recognise.
