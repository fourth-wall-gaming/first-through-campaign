**0 light years from Sol, which is the joke people make and stop making.**

Earth is still there, and the most crowded thing in human space: eleven billion
people on one planet and the worlds around it, more than a third of everyone
alive. Cities that have been cities for six thousand years. A civilisation with
fusion torches in the Belt, a mind that has run the whole system since before
anyone's grandmother, and a civil service so good that it has lasted nine
centuries on seniority alone.

What it does not have is money, and that is the thing to understand about it. It
is the old country. It is magnificent, it is exhausted, and it is the tired adult
in the room.

## What was taken

[[The First Reach]] was paid for out of this planet and the worlds around it.
Not taxed out of it: **spent** out of it, down to the fittings, across sixty
years, by people who voted for it in public and knew exactly what they were
doing. The yards that built the ships were on Luna. The mirrors that powered the
yards were at Mercury. The mass came out of the Belt and the money came out of
here.

When it was finished there was a network and no civilisation left to use it, and
the eighty-five-year wait for the first door fell on a generation that had
inherited the bill and none of the argument.

Earth did not collapse. Nothing was wrecked, and it rebuilt what it needed. It
simply never got the Reach back, and it has spent nine hundred years as the
place that paid for everything and is still owed. It believes that makes the
Causeway theirs. That is historically true, and there is nobody on Earth who can
enforce it, because every door is held by a child and no army can be sent
through one.

## What is there now

Cities older than the Causeway, kept going. Farms that have been farmed for six
thousand years and are still the best land any human being has ever stood on.
Weather that happens by itself. An ocean you can walk into.

The rest of human space is a better-funded place to live, and it knows it. What
Earth has that nobody else does is *seniority*: the oldest archives of testimony,
the oldest customs, the oldest quarrels, and the longest memory of what the Reach
cost. Ask an Earth delegate at the Nine how things ought to be done and you will
get a precedent from the year 140.

Seven doors, more than any system but Sirius. One of them goes to the Nine and
carries more traffic than the other six together. The rest go out to the long
shots: the later doors, to systems that have grown up on the far side from
nothing. Traffic that matters still goes through the Nine.

## The First Hall

The seven anchors sit together in one room on Luna, in the yards that built the
ships. It is the oldest door-hall in human history and it is still working, and
it has a window.

Through the window is Earth.

People come a long way to stand in that room, and the Threshold keeps a roster of
first-throughs to host them, because otherwise the queue blocks the line.
First-throughs go once, in training, and are told nothing about it beforehand.

## Its three names

**Earth**, which is its name, and which is what it is called on it.

**The Remainder**, which is what the Tally's own charters call it. A remainder is
what is left when everything allocated has been taken out: the part nobody has a
claim on, carried forward. The usage started about four hundred years ago in the
minds' own shorthand for the long account of what the Reach had spent. It was
unkind then. It got used until it stopped being a joke and started being the
address.

**The Garden**, which is what people say.

Because everything grows there. Because it is the only world with an outdoors:
air nobody makes, water that falls out of the sky by itself, food that comes up
without a lamp over it. And because none of that travels. You cannot put weather
through a door. You cannot sell an ocean to a station on the other side of the
galaxy, and nothing would reach it if you did.

A spacer says *the Garden* with a kind of fondness, the way you talk about
somewhere you went once and would not live. Somebody from Earth says it flatly
and the conversation moves on.

And the word has got loose, the way words do. A holding that is rich in the
things that do not travel and poor in the things that do is said to be **going
garden**.

People use it about [[Gliese 887, and the Coldwater Holding]]. They do not use
it where the Coldwater people can hear.
