**12.83 light years. The door is six hundred and ninety-four years old and it
has been dark for three hundred and seventy-three of them.**

## The star

It does not belong here. Everything else on the chart was born in the disc and
goes round with it; Kapteyn's Star is a **halo** star, the nearest one there is,
and it is running the other way -- a retrograde orbit through the galaxy, cutting
across the traffic at a few hundred kilometres a second. When it was found it had
the largest proper motion of any star known, and held the title until
[[Barnard's Star]] took it in 1916. You can watch it move against the background
in a human lifetime.

It is also **old**. Something like eleven and a half billion years, which is
more than twice the age of Sol, with about a seventh of Sol's metals in it,
because it formed before there were many to have. The current reading is that it
was stripped out of a dwarf galaxy that the Milky Way ate, and has been crossing
on its own ever since.

A red subdwarf of a quarter of Sol's mass, turning once in about a hundred and
twenty-five days. Slow, cold, dim, and -- for a red star -- quiet.

## What is in orbit

Officially, nothing.

Two planets were announced in 2014 and became famous for a week, one of them in
the temperate band. Seven years later a careful re-analysis took both of them
away again: the signals were the star's own rotation and the long-lived blotches
on its face, and there is **no evidence of a planet here at all**.

The holding is this campaign's one **invented** world, and it is invented inside
the constraint: small, dry, below anybody's detection limit, and poor in metals
because its star is. Thin ores worked hard for centuries. The one thing it has
that [[Gliese 65, the Flare Twins]] does not is a sky that holds still.

## The door

The mate sat on that world for twenty-four years with nobody to open it, waiting
for the other half of the pair to finish its flight to
[[van Maanen's Star]]. It came into service in year 281, and for three hundred
and twenty-one years it was an ordinary edge on an ordinary run: eleven thousand
people, farms, foundries, a hall, a queue, a tallyman.

In the spring of **602** it stopped answering.

By [[Straying]] a mouth cannot be destroyed and cannot be re-pointed, and a
mouth that lands inside rock does not open at all. So the reading now -- it is
only about forty years old, and it is not what people believed for most of three
centuries -- is that the far mouth is exactly where the physics put it, underground,
closed. **Buried, and not lost.** Three hundred and eighty expeditions went
looking for somewhere it had gone, and if the reading is right, every one of them
was looking for the wrong thing.

## What people say about it

The sum everybody does in their head is fifteen generations, which is close
enough that a family name could have survived it, and that is the part nobody
enjoys.

On a clear watch at the Nine somebody will take you up to the gallery and point
out which dim star it is, and then watch your face. Everybody there has had it
done to them once.

The Threshold has kept a watch on the housing at van Maanen's since the year it
went dark. The rota has never been cut. It has never once reported anything.

And the line still says what a woman said out loud that spring, expecting to
supersede it by the afternoon. Ask the Old Man and he gives you her four words
in her own voice, because that is what an attestation is and there has never
been another. See [[The Standing]].
