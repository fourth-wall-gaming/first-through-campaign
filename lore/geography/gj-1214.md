**47.8 light years. The door is twenty years old.**

The newest door anybody alive has watched open, and the cautionary tale every
briefing about LHS 1140 begins with.

**GJ 1214 b** is a sub-Neptune: larger than Earth, smaller than Neptune, wrapped
in a thick atmosphere so hazy that for decades nobody could see down into it at
all. The best current reading says a great deal of water and a high-altitude
haze layer that defeats most attempts to look through it. It is one of the most
studied planets outside the solar system and it has still not finished
surprising anybody.

What it is not is somewhere you can stand.

**What people built here: an argument.**

The door opened twenty years ago to enormous public attention, because the
pre-arrival surveys had used the words *water world* and everybody had heard
them. What arrived was a hot, crushing, opaque atmosphere with no surface worth
the name and nothing to plant anything in.

There is a survey station, a mooring, and three competing claims that have been
in dispute since the week the door opened. The Tally took over the station's
tolls in the fifth year on the grounds that somebody had to.

Every honest briefing about LHS 1140 says the same thing: *we were sure last
time too.* Every dishonest one leaves it out.
