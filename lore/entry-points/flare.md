**One sitting. Combat-forward: three waves at most, on a clock, and nobody dies.**

This is the track for the player who came to fight. It exercises the Mythras
engine properly — initiative, action points, parry versus evade, hit locations,
and **special effects chosen by the player every single time they win an
exchange**. See [[Special Effects Are the Player's]], which is not optional
reading.

## The hook, to read aloud

> Calloway is already holding your suit.
>
> *"Gliese 65. The refinery has stopped answering, which refineries do, except
> that Gliese 65 is two flare stars and the count is climbing."* They push the
> suit at you. *"Twenty-two people on that station and their shelter is on the
> wrong side of their own corridor. Go now."*
>
> *"How long have I got?"*
>
> *"Nobody knows. That is the entire problem with flare stars. Go."*

## 1. The clock, and how to run it

**The flare arrives in about forty minutes of station time.** Write that number
down. Do not say it to the player as a number and do not count rounds out loud —
`TABLE.md` §3 keeps numbers out of prose.

Mark its passage in the fiction, every few beats, using things that are already
in [[The Refinery]]:

- the deck lighting browning out, at first every ninety seconds, then oftener
- the hull making a sound nobody in the room acknowledges
- the radio hiss rising until the crew stop using it
- the shuttered window on the upper processing deck, which somebody has opened

Three or four of those and the player will feel the clock without being handed
one. If they ask outright, the honest in-fiction answer is that nobody knows,
and that is worse.

## 2. The suit, and the guaranteed early success

Before they step through, run the kit seal as a real roll the player **cannot
fail** -- a Very Easy call on something they are already good at -- so the first
mechanics block they ever see is one that *worked*. Calloway is holding the suit
and there is no time, which is exactly why the drill is automatic.

This is spec section 8's fourth onboarding device and it matters more on this
track than on the other one. A player who picked Flare came for the dice. If the
first roll they ever see is a failure, they learn that the dice are something
that happens *to* them -- in the thirty seconds before their first fight, which
is the worst possible moment to learn it.

It costs half a minute. Do not skip it.

## 3. Step through into the brownout

Lights failing in a rhythm. Nobody in the anchor bay. A shock baton on the deck
that somebody dropped and did not come back for.

## 4. The Barricade

[[The Barricade]]: six metres of corridor emptied of a loading bay. The three
affordances are in its description and they are the scenario — the **cargo
strap** cross-bracing, which a knife goes through; the **maintenance crawl**
behind the left panel, which an adult cannot use and a fifteen-year-old can; and
the **full drums**, which mean nobody can open this fast from either side.

## 5. Why they built it

The crew are not hostile. [[Tallyman Captain Brace]] walked in that morning with
an **impound order** on the refinery's transit rights, which means the crew are
not permitted through their own door — and the shelter is on the far side of it.

So they emptied the loading bay into the corridor and sat down in front of it,
with four of Brace's tallymen still inside, because if they are not leaving then
nobody is.

Twenty-two frightened people, four [[Tallyman]]s doing procedure, and a star
getting ready.

## 6. The fight, and it is a real one

Three waves at most, escalating, each with a way through that is not fighting if
the player finds it:

1. **Two refinery hands on the stack.** Improvised: tools, fists, a length of
   strap. They do not want to hurt a child and will say so while swinging.
2. **Four tallymen, in grey, from behind.** Shock batons, restraint loops,
   AP 4 armour, and procedure. This is the real exchange and it should be
   frightening. They want the player **restrained**, not harmed.
3. **Brace himself**, if the player has pushed it that far — Tally Drill at 72,
   immovable, courteous, and entirely beatable by somebody who thinks.

Run every exchange properly: ask how they are defending before you resolve,
parry or evade, their choice, and hand them the special effect when they win it.
A kid who came here to fight has come for exactly that moment.

## 7. Everybody walks out

Nobody dies in this scenario, including the tallymen, and the reason is in
[[The Tally]]: a body is not a line on a manifest. Stunners, batons, restraint
loops, setting gel. Somebody on the floor and tied is how this resolves in both
directions. See `TABLE-ADDENDUM.md`.

**The two non-violent solutions are owned by two different pre-gens**, and both
are real wins:

- **The Tinker** can cut the cargo strap and bring the stack down controlled, or
  gel the hinge side and reroute through the ore hoist entirely.
- **The Runner** takes the maintenance crawl, comes out on the anchor side
  alone, and opens it from the back with nobody in front of them.
- **The Talker** can end the whole thing in one conversation: the impound order
  is void during a declared hazard, which the Tally's own procedure says, and
  Brace will honour his own rulebook if somebody reads it to him.
- **The Fieldhand** gets the hands and the hurt moving before the fight starts
  and takes the crew's reason for barricading away from them.

## 8. Debrief at the Nine

The flare comes. Everybody is behind mass. The refinery takes it and the player
watches the lights go hard white through a shuttered window.

Then the same thing as the other track: the impound order has a
**countersignature** on it, and it says *the Quorum* — and Brace, once, with
real unease, lets slip that he carried that order to the refinery the morning
**before** it was issued.

He cannot explain how. He changes the subject. **That is the whole of Act 1's
reveal: a word, and a timing nobody can account for.** Spend nothing else; see
`lore/gm-secret/the-quorum.md`.
