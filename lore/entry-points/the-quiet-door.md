**One sitting. Low violence: exactly one confrontation and it is avoidable.**

## The hook, to read aloud

> Calloway does not sit down, which is how you know.
>
> *"Coldwater,"* they say. *"Gliese 887. Four thousand people, one door, and the
> door has started stuttering. It passed its scheduled test four days ago and
> it has failed twice since."*
>
> They put a slate on the table and do not let go of it.
>
> *"You know what happens if it stops. Nothing happens. That is the whole
> horror of it — nothing happens, and four thousand people are still standing
> there, and we cannot ever reach them again. Go and find out why."*

## 1. The Nine, ready room

Establish the three things the campaign runs on: [[Calloway]], who tells the
truth and sends you anyway; [[Tolk]], who has opinions about the kettle; and the
fact that once you step through, nobody can reach you.

**The guaranteed early success lives here.** Run the kit check as a real roll
the player cannot fail — a Very Easy call on something they are already good at
— so that the first mechanics block they ever see is one that *worked*. Do not
skip this. It is the whole of spec section 8's fourth onboarding device, and it
costs thirty seconds.

If the player asks whether they can call back once they are through, the answer
is in [[Why Nobody Can Reach You]], and it is no, and it is not a punishment.

## 2. Step through

Give the crossing one short paragraph and make it undramatic. The light
changes. The gravity is lighter. It smells of grain and cold water instead of
hot metal. The door behind you is a shed door.

Tolk says something unhelpful.

## 3. The Landing — diagnosis

[[The Landing]] is a corrugated shed with the only door out of the system
standing in the middle of it like a farm implement. Put the three affordances in
the prose — the **load bed panel** with four bolts missing, the **jury-rig**
cabinet humming warm in a place no schematic shows it, and the **fault slate**
nailed to the post in a hand that gets less tidy down the column — and stop.

All four pre-gens get in from here, by different doors:

- **The Tinker** reads the fault directly and fastest. It is their scene.
- **The Runner** gets up the mast, where the last eight metres stop being a
  ladder, and brings down the load telemetry nobody else can reach.
- **The Fieldhand** notices the people: the maintenance crew are working double
  shifts and the shift book says they are not.
- **The Talker** gets it out of the foreman in one conversation, because the
  foreman has been waiting four days for somebody to ask.

## 4. The complication

The Holding has been **keeping the anchor warm**. The jury-rig is theirs: a
cabinet of non-standard parts wired into the coupling collar to hold the load
steady through a stutter, built by their own engineer, and it is *making the
failure worse* — it is masking exactly the drift a first-through would have
caught eight months ago.

Nobody here is a villain. See [[The Coldwater Holding]]. They did not gamble
with four thousand lives out of stupidity; they did it because being cut off is
the same as ending and they could not bear to stand still.

## 5. The confrontation, which is avoidable

The foreman will physically put himself between the player and the jury-rig's
isolator. He is frightened, he is not armed, and he is bigger than a
fifteen-year-old.

**Three ways past that are not a fight, and all three are real:**

1. **Show him the drift.** The telemetry off the mast or the fault record off
   the slate makes the case without anybody's opinion in it. He can read.
2. **Get the council.** Coldwater has five of them and a requisition board
   pinned up in public; the Talker can have three of them in the shed inside
   ten minutes.
3. **Isolate it from underneath.** The load bed panel is four bolts short and
   leads under the floor. The Tinker and the Runner can both reach the coupling
   from below while the argument continues above them, which is the funniest
   version of this scene and should be allowed to be.

If it does become a fight: the foreman grapples, nobody draws anything, and it
ends with somebody on the floor and a restraint loop and a great deal of shame.
See `TABLE-ADDENDUM.md`. **It never ends worse than that.**

## 6. The repair, and the cost

The door can be saved. Clean the housing, pull the jury-rig, re-seat the
coupling collar, re-balance the load bed, and Coldwater has months — see
[[Anchor Hardware]] for what is actually in a first-through's hands.

Months, not years. Fixing it properly needs a part that is not here and a
decision somebody well above the player's pay grade has been declining to make
for two quarters. Say so plainly and let the player carry it.

## 7. Debrief at the Nine

Calloway signs it off and means the thanks.

Then: the clearance that authorised the Coldwater part order — the one that was
declined twice — carries a **countersignature** on the bottom of the form.
Neither of them can explain it. It is not a Threshold mark and it is not a Tally
mark.

It says *the Quorum*.

**That is the whole of Act 1's reveal: a word, and no face.** Do not elaborate,
do not let Calloway speculate, and do not answer a single question about it. See
`lore/gm-secret/the-quorum.md` for what a GM should know and must not spend.
