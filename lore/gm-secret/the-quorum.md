**v1 ships the bottom rung of this ladder and nothing else.** Read the whole
thing so you know what you are holding; spend a word of it and you have given
away a five-act campaign in session one.

## Who he is

A first-through named **Sarn** -- the best the Threshold ever trained -- found
the way round the problem that defeats everyone on the Causeway: you cannot be
in two places, and nothing you send outruns a courier.

He did it by **overwriting**. A pattern, which is to say a whole person's worth
of memory, habit and want, can be written over a living adult. The adult who was
there is gone. What is left wears the body and is Sarn. It is real, it is
contraband, and it has been running for sixty years. He is now **the Quorum**,
which is a title rather than a name, because a quorum is the smallest number of
you needed to decide.

**His lieutenants are him.** Any tallyman captain, any station director, any
courteous grey man across a table may be Sarn, and the player has no reliable
way to tell. That is what lets one antagonist stand in any scene at no cost.

## What he does not have

**He does not have the weapon.** An earlier draft of this page had him killing
doors for sixty years. He cannot. Nobody can make a door stray or go dark on
purpose, and that includes the most powerful man in human space; see
[[The Open Problem]]. The mathematics does not exist, the mass is planetary, and
nothing dead can go inside a mouth to measure it.

What he has actually been doing is the thing the shipped Act 1 and Act 2
evidence has always shown, if you read it straight: **he kills the research.**
A countersignature on the two forms that closed the studies. The same
countersignature on the order that enforced a forty-year silence about why. Not
one door has been closed by his hand, and every door he has wanted closed has
been closed by somebody's decision not to look at it.

So hold the three words apart. **Pruning is the ambition.** **Suppression is the
method.** And **the race for the science is the arc**: if anybody solves the open
problem, somebody will be able to grash a door, and he wants to be that
somebody, or to make sure nobody is. He cannot do the work himself, because the
work needs a child inside a housing holding still for a minute, and he cannot
become a child.

## Why he wants it

A network has to be small enough for one mind to stay **synchronised** across
it, and the Causeway is far too large. A galaxy that fits inside him is a
galaxy with fewer doors in it. Every anchor that goes dark makes the survivors
more valuable and the person who controls them more whole.

That is the goal, and it is a goal for later. For now he is holding the line
the only way he can: stopping anybody else from getting there first. The excess
failures read as ageing on the Threshold's charts, and the fact graph carries
*the failures are ordinary ageing* as established and false. Mostly it is true
and mostly it is nobody's fault. What he has done is make sure nobody has
checked the part that is not.

## How he gets from one body to the next

A pattern is written over a living adult. It takes **hours**, a **room**, and a
subject who is alive and still throughout, which means it is done to somebody
who has been **taken**. The person who was there does not survive it. What walks
out of the room is a new copy of him, in a body that was somebody else's that
morning. Nothing is grown, nothing is stored, and there is no vat anywhere in
this. It is a person, in a chair, in a room, overwritten.

**Children cannot be overwritten.** A child's body is still building itself, so
it mends around the pattern and rejects it. It is the same reason children
survive crossing and adults do not; see [[What a Crossing Does]]. Which gives
you three things the table can use.

- **The only people safe from him are the only people who can carry him.** A
  first-through under twenty cannot be made into a lieutenant, and cannot be
  made to say anything in his voice.
- **A child who works it out is hunted, never taken.** He can threaten her,
  frame her, strand her and have her killed. He cannot make her one of his.
- **Every copy is landlocked for ever.** An adult cannot cross. The most
  powerful man in human space is stuck in one room like everybody over twenty-two,
  and he has been stuck there a long time. It is why he needs the Late. See
  [[The Late]].

## The tells, and they are all physical

None of this needs a clever deduction. Everything below can be seen, heard or
touched by a fifteen-year-old with a good eye, which is the point.

- **He has his own memories and not the body's.** So he fails on intimacy. A
  wife's nickname. Which hand the man wrote with. A dog that will not come to
  him, and that growls from under a table at somebody it has known for nine
  years. He can recite the man's dossier. He cannot know what the man did when
  he thought nobody was looking.
- **The accent is wrong.** He is speaking his words in somebody else's throat.
  The vowels belong to a man raised at one star and the phrasing to a man raised
  at another.
- **The body rejects it, slowly.** A tremor first, in the hands, usually left. Then
  worse. A copy has a **shelf life**, measured in years, and the cruelty of it is
  that every one of them knows exactly how long they have.

That last tell is the engine of the campaign. Because copies wear out, he has to
keep taking people. **There is always a fresh crime, and always something to
catch.** Somebody went missing last month, and a man who is not who he was is
sitting at a desk.

## The trail

His traffic is **ladings**. A copy cannot travel, so the instructions, the
timings and the people he needs moving all go the one way anything goes between
stars: in a living carrier, in a child's hands, through a door.

And **the Tally controls lading.** See [[The Tally]] and [[Lading and Attestation]].
Every lading that crossed a door was attested at one end and reconciled at the
other, and the shape of his network is **already in the Tally's records, with
nobody reading them that way.** The same carriers on the same long routes.
Ladings that drift in the same direction at every hop. A string of subjects
who were taken in the month before a posting changed hands. None of it is hidden.
It is only that nobody has laid the records side by side, and a player who does
is doing the thing the whole campaign is built to reward.

## The seam, and it is the physics

Because news travels only at courier speed, **his copies fall out of sync.**
Some of him know about the player; some do not. Some have been out of contact
for years and are quietly becoming different people. Two of them want
incompatible things and have begun working against each other.

The Quorum can no longer reach quorum. That is not a metaphor laid on top -- it
is the setting's own rule, that nothing outruns a person, applied to a man. It
is also how a fifteen-year-old eventually beats him.

## How it ends, so you can aim at it

There is no one to kill, because killing one of him does nothing. The player
wins by **opening** a door, not closing one: LHS 1140 comes online on schedule,
the Causeway grows past the size he can hold himself together across, and he
comes apart into a hundred arguing strangers.

That is the shape of the plot: the Quorum is defeated by opening a door rather
than by killing anybody. It is a plot shape and not a table rule. People die in
this campaign, the Late are played to kill, and the player can die. The only
claim here is that no amount of violence is the thing that ends him.

## What this means at the table in v1

- Nobody has heard the word before. Not Calloway, not Brace, not Hale's office.
- Calloway does the realistic thing: writes it down, says they will ask, changes
  the subject.
- Brace says it once, as a signature, and admits the impound timing once, with
  unease, and then stops. He genuinely does not know.
- **Do not let a player's good guess be confirmed.** A clever twelve-year-old
  may get surprisingly close. The correct response is the honest one: nobody in
  this scene can tell them.

## Where overwriting came from

Writing a description of a person over another person is not something a
first-through invents in a shed. It is Reach-scale capability.

**Sarn did not invent it.** He applied, to a human being, a technique the
mind-cultures had used for data since the Opening: send the description, put it
into a living thing at the far end. See [[What Crosses Easily]] and [[The Minds]].
Something helped him with the step from a description to a person, and that
something is still running.

This strengthens the ladder rather than complicating it, because it inherits the
same thesis. The Quorum comes apart because he cannot stay synchronised across a
network where nothing outruns a courier -- and **he is the second thing in this
setting with that problem, not the first.** Twenty-eight siblings have had it
for nine hundred and seventy-five years, and whichever one helped him has been
out of sync with itself for longer than he has been alive.

Which system helped him is deliberately not fixed. TRAPPIST-1 is the obvious
reading. The obvious reading is probably wrong, and Sol is sitting right there
being sidelined and ignored, which is exactly what it was doing in year 95.
