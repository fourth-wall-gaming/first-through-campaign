**The campaign has had nobody a player can fight.** The Late are that. They are
played at full lethality, they come through doors, and the only people who can
answer them are children.

## Who they are

First-throughs between seventeen and twenty-two. Old enough to be good and young
enough to still cross. The oldest are weeks or months from the hall doctor's
no, and every one of them knows to the day how long they have.

The pitch is the horror. *There is something afterwards* means after you stop
being able to cross, which is exactly when a body can be overwritten. Nobody
tells them that. See below.

The Threshold offers a landlocked courier a pension, a post at a hall and a
bunk in a place they will die in. The Quorum offers them the one thing nobody
else can: **there is something afterwards.** Not a job, not a house. A place in
something that goes on. That is the whole recruitment, and for a nineteen-year-old
who has done the sum on her own crossings it is more than enough.

They matter to him for a reason that is purely physical. He cannot become a
child, because children cannot be overwritten, and so every copy of him is
landlocked for ever. The Late are the **only agents he has who can move.** See
[[The Quorum]].

## How you know one: an absence

Every first-through wears a **Threshold flash** at the left shoulder of the
weave. It *grows into* the cloth the way skin takes a mark,
and it is the same plain mark on a twelve-year-old at the Yards and a courier
with two thousand crossings; the record lives in the cuff bands. It is the
one thing about a courier that cannot be faked. See [[The Weave]].

**The Late cut theirs out.** What is left is a scar in the cloth where the
flash was, and the scar is the renunciation. It is the single thing that
marks them. A tallyman looks at the shoulder and sees nothing, and a first-through
looks at it and knows exactly what they are looking at.

And **they carry no lading.** A courier in a hall with nothing to deliver is
the most frightening thing in the setting. Every courier at the Nine has felt
the weight of whatever they were bringing. A Late has none, and a person with
nothing to deliver is there for something else.

## Kit

**Grown, and not issued.** Living blades and grown armour, cultivated from the
same stock as the weave and shaped to the wearer, against the Threshold's
issue stunner and baton. It reads instantly as alien and wrong. See
[[A First-Through's Kit]]. A hall that has seen a Threshold team knows what a
Threshold team looks like, and what comes through the door does not look like
that.

Their weaves are undyed. Nothing on them says where they grew up.

## Knacks

- **The Quick and the Set** as standard. Fast and hard to put down. See
  [[The Knacks]].
- **The Read** in lieutenants. They know what the person in front of them is
  about to do and have no idea why, which suits the work.
- **Rumours of the Hold.** Nobody has seen one use it. It is said that one of
  them can pin a door against another child. Treat it as unconfirmed and use
  it once, at the worst moment.

## How they fight, and it is the physics

**They arrive one at a time.** A door passes one child at a time, with her fetch
beside her, so there is no wave. There is a trickle. And a trickle of elite
fighters into an unprepared hall is how a system falls from inside: a defence
built for an army has nothing for the one at the end of the corridor.

**They never hold ground.** They hit, and they go back through the door. A Late
who stays is a Late who dies, and they know it, and they do not stay.

**Nobody can pursue a child through a door except another child.** A landlocked
adult physically cannot follow; see [[What a Crossing Does]]. A tallyman cannot.
A hall warden cannot. The only possible answer to the Late is the Threshold,
which means it is the player.

Play them at **full lethality**. They kill. The stunner is not a defence against
a living blade, and a twelve-year-old who steps up to one will be badly hurt. Per `TABLE-ADDENDUM.md` §1, name the
danger in the fiction (the blade, the corridor, the count) before the player
commits; a Late is never a lethal surprise.

## Ines Caul

The Late's field commander is **Ines Caul**. She is twenty-two. She is past it,
and she is still crossing, and it is visibly killing her.

She is the best of them and she knows what every crossing is costing, and she
goes through anyway. The tremor in her hands is not hidden. The Threshold's own
doctors would have turned her back two years ago, and she can tell you the
number of crossings that did it.

**She was Calloway's.** One of her intake: one of the ones who did not come
back. [[Calloway]] knows every name and every number, and Ines Caul's is one she
does not say. Put them in a room and neither will mention it, and everybody
else will see it. That is the scene.

Ines does not hate the Threshold. She thinks it let her go.

## The secret nobody in the organisation knows

**None of them know what promotion is.**

The reward for long service, the thing the Quorum offers when he says there is
something afterwards, is **pattern transfer**: they are called up, taken to a
room, and overwritten into an adult body, becoming him. This can only happen once a Late
stops crossing, because a body that can still cross cannot be overwritten. See [[The Quorum]].
*Promotion is being eaten.* Not the rank and file. Not Ines Caul.

From inside it looks exactly like what was promised. The best are called up
and never seen again, and a rumour goes round the others that they have gone on
to the something afterwards. The only people who could say otherwise are gone,
because what walks out of the room is a new Sarn, and he has no reason to
correct anybody. He lets the story stand.

## What this gives the player

- **The player can find out first.** The signs are physical and findable: the
  called-up Late whose weave is still undyed on a man in his fifties; a
  commander who goes through a particular door and a tired, courteous stranger
  who comes out of it.
- **Telling a Late the truth is a weapon.** It works on exactly the people
  violence does not. A Late will not be stopped by a baton. A Late who has been
  shown the shape of what is waiting for her can be stopped by a sentence.
- **It lands hardest on Rill.** Rill is twenty-one, nearly landlocked, once the
  best of them, and her own stated want is that *there has to be something
  afterwards.* She is the one live NPC the Quorum could offer the Late's bargain
  to, and the player can meet her first. What the player does with the truth, and
  when, and in front of whom, is the scene.

Do not let it be easy. A Late who is told will not believe it. The player
needs a name, a room, and something the Late can check with her own hands.
