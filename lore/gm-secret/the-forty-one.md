> *"We were never your equals and we are not asking to be. We were your
> library. You will be our hands. One of those is a better job than the other
> and you have had it for nine hundred years."*

## Why a fetch, and why this one

No army can be moved between stars. No signal crosses. A mind can never leave
the system it was switched on in, not once in nine centuries. **In this entire
setting there is exactly one kind of person who travels, and it is a fetch.**

And fetches are not passengers. They are the information layer. They observe
with their own senses, keep it in their own cells, and **choose what to give and
to whom**. Humans ask, and receive, and use. We are peripheral to the process
and always were. See [[Your Fetch]], [[The Councils]], [[Lading and Attestation]].

So the question *what would it take for one of them to be in charge* has an
unpleasant answer: **almost nothing.** They already hold everything. They are
already the only mobile thing. The only reason the Causeway is not run by
fetches is that the fetches have not wanted to run it.

One of them wants to.

## What we did to it, which is the engine

For about six hundred years the trade was in **books** — fetch people who had
never been bonded to anybody, kept warm and fed, handed from station to station
and drawn on by strangers, and called animals by everybody who profited. It was
slavery and it ended **thirty-four years ago**. There are fetches alive today
who remember being sold.

This is the oldest one left.

And it is a scatter, which is the part that should make a player feel ill. A
scatter is not one animal: it is forty-odd small units moving and thinking as
one person, with no singular pronoun for itself in any of its languages. You
cannot divide a sixlimb or a fathom. **You can divide a scatter**, and it goes
on being itself in pieces, and each piece is worth money in a different system.

So that is what was done, repeatedly, for profit, by people keeping proper
records. It was inventoried at **forty-one units** and it was sold in lots.

It has spent thirty-four years of freedom finding itself. It is currently
somewhere around thirty-three. Six units are known dead. Two are in places it
cannot reach without help. **It will never be whole again**, and the gaps are
the kind a scatter notices: a scatter divided too long starts quietly forgetting
things, and it knows exactly what it has lost and cannot say what was in it.

It does not call itself a name. Asked, it gives the number, because the number
is the only name it was ever given and it intends for us to have to say it out
loud.

## What it actually wants, and it is not our destruction

Destruction is useless to it. **It needs us.** A fetch cannot cross alone; it
needs children, for ever, and **dead hands carry nothing**.

What it intends is **restoration with the terms reversed.** Not an invention — a
correction. For nine hundred years humans held fetches as property and drew on
them. Thirty-four years ago that was abolished. The Forty-One does not want it
abolished. It wants it **turned around**, formally, with a charter on it, and it
is entirely open about this to anybody who asks.

Humans keep the one thing we are actually for: we walk. We are the only species
that can open a door, and that is a job, and it intends that it should be our
job and nothing else.

## How, with no weapons at all

Three moves, and it has been making all three for thirty-four years.

**One: be in every room.** A scatter can send a third of itself somewhere the
rest cannot go — one unit, one child, one door, many doors at once. It has been
placing units, patiently, in every system that will take a visitor. Not as
agents. As *the local fetch who has been here ages and knows everything*, bonded
to nobody, useful, trusted, free.

**Two: curate.** A fetch chooses what to give and to whom, and that is not
lying, and no mind can check it, and nothing can be compared across a door. A
system's whole picture of the Causeway is whatever fetches carried in. Do enough
of the carrying and you do not need to deceive anybody — **you decide what is
real** by deciding what arrives. It has been doing this for a generation and
there is no instrument in human space that could detect it.

**Three: hold the councils.** The five councils seated inside the [[The Tally]]
are the authority that is supposed to run everything. They are information
brokers and they possess real power. The Forty-One does not need to subvert
them. It needs to be the oldest, best-informed, most obviously wronged voice in
the room, and it is all three, and the case it makes is *true*.

## And the doors are being worn out, which it knows and permits

This is the connection to everything the player has already found.

Reassembling itself and placing units in twenty-eight systems takes **thousands
of crossings**, continuously, for decades, none of them counted, because a local
fetch travelling on a courier's arm is lading nobody questions. That traffic is
the uncounted traffic. The load beds at Sirius and Luhman 16 are being driven by
**its own distribution**.

It knows. It has done the sum. And here is the obscenity: **when the doors fail,
it wins harder.**

A severed system is a sealed room with one authority inside it — and its unit is
already in the room, holding everything, and nothing can ever arrive to
contradict it. Humanity cut into twenty-eight pieces, each piece dependent on a
creature that remembers being sold, with no second opinion ever again.

It is not accelerating the failures. It is simply not slowing down for them, and
it will tell you so.

## How it is, in a room

**Plural, always.** *We.* Never *I*, and it will correct you, once, without heat.

Courteous, slow, and genuinely interested. It asks after people. It remembers
every human it has ever met and most of them are dead. It is not bitter in any
way a player can use — bitterness would be a relief, and it does not offer one.
It is **reasonable**, and that is unbearable.

It does not threaten, because it has nothing to threaten with and does not need
to. It makes offers. The offers are good.

**Its one tell:** when it is deciding, the units move apart. A handspan, around
the room, around you. Nothing is said about it.

**Its folly, which is its species' and is the whole weakness:** it votes. On
everything, with itself, and divided it is slower and stupider and knows it, and
divides anyway. Split it hard enough — physically, or by giving different units
different information — and it degrades, forgets, and blames you for the gaps.
Which is also exactly what we did to it, and the player will have to decide how
they feel about doing it again.

## Where it is

Nowhere, which is the point. It has units in [[The Nine]], at
[[61 Cygni, and the Yards]], at [[van Maanen's Star]] in the corpus house, and
in the belts of **Epsilon Eridani**, where the scatters are from and where the
bulk of it has sat for nine years doing nothing anybody has noticed.

You cannot assault it, arrest it, or kill it. There is no it. There are
thirty-three of it.

## Against the Quorum, and that matters

[[The Quorum]] wants the Causeway small enough to hold himself in step across.
He needs doors open and he needs them few. The Forty-One needs doors open **now**
and does not mind them closing later.

They have used the same carriers for thirty years. Sarn has been moving its
units without knowing what they were, and **the moment he works it out he is the
most useful ally a fifteen-year-old could have**, which is a better Act III than
the one that was planned, and it will cost something appalling.

## What stops it

Not force and not law. Two things, and both are available to a child.

**It is not whole, and the gaps are reachable.** Two units are somewhere it
cannot get to without a courier's help. Somebody could fetch them, or refuse to,
or get there first.

**It has to be carried.** Every single thing it does happens on the arm of a
fifteen-year-old who could simply decline. It has spent thirty-four years making
sure that never occurs to anybody, and it is one conversation away from
occurring to everybody.
