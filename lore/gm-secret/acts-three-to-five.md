**NOT IMPLEMENTED IN v1.** There are no agendas, beats, facts, characters or
locations in this repository for anything on this page. It is here so the arc is
on record and so a GM who runs Acts 1-2 knows what they are setting up.

## Act 3 -- the frontier

[[TRAPPIST-1]] and the newest doors. Somebody is running **pattern transfer**
as contraband -- the Second Hand, who are the Quorum's franchise. It still needs
an anchor, because the classical channel it depends on has to cross one; what it
bypasses is the **checkpoint**, since what steps through the door is data rather
than a person on a manifest.

It is illegal for good reasons and the reasons are the scenario. They keep
copies.

This is also the act in which the player meets the Quorum: a tired, courteous
man in his sixties, physically unremarkable, who offers them a job and is not
lying about any of it.

And the thread on what [[Tolk]] is opens properly. See
`lore/gm-secret/what-tolk-is.md`.

## Act 4 -- the countdown

[[LHS 1140]] opens in months. Every faction on the Causeway converges on the
one anchor everybody has waited nine hundred and seventy-five years for, and
[[Ferren]] has had his name in for the posting since before the player qualified.

The player works out how many of him there are -- and that two of them are
giving contradictory orders, because they have been out of contact for three
years.

## Act 5 -- through

What is on the other side, and **the door nobody launched**: one edge in the
network with no seedship, no launch record and no explanation, which means
somebody built anchors before the First Reach. Its mouths are relativistically
offset, so crossing it moves you in time -- confined to this one edge so
continuity stays holdable.

Behind it, the original body. And the reveal: **the original died decades ago.**
There is no Sarn. There is only the network.

Then the ending the whole thing was built for. The door opens on schedule, the
Causeway grows past the size the Quorum can hold himself across, and he comes
apart. The player wins by opening a door.

## The layer that stays loose on purpose

The anchors are failing partly from plain entropy -- true, and sad, and not
anybody's fault -- and the people who could repair them were not human. Neither
of those needs resolving to run Acts 1-4, and the campaign is better if the GM
does not decide early.
