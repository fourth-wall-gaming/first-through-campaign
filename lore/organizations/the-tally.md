Here is the thing nobody planned.

A door cannot be moved, copied or replaced. It sits where the First Reach put
it, inside a building, and the building has a corridor, and the corridor has an
end. **Whoever stands at that end decides who goes through.**

And what goes through is a child, and what the child is carrying. Freight does
not cross. Nothing manufactured crosses. No signal crosses. A door will pass a
body and a living thing, and that is all, so the question at the end of every
corridor in human space is never *what is in the crates?* There are no crates.
It is **what are you carrying in your arms, and who sent you?**

That question is called lading (see [[Lading and Attestation]]), and deciding it is the Tally's whole
trade. It is the office that decides what information is allowed to cross a
door, in which direction, at whose request and at whose expense. Every system has
one. Nobody voted for any of them. None of them answers to anyone you could
walk up to and shout at.

It calls itself a service, and mostly it is one. The fees pay for the halls, the
kit lockers, the hall doctors and the rota that keeps a watch on a dark
housing at Kapteyn's three hundred and seventy-three years after anybody
expected anything from it. The Standing is a public good and the Tally keeps it
honestly.

## Half the Tally is a mind

Look up in any anchor hall and the thing that is looking back is the Tally.

Each system has a **system mind**. Human hands made them, nine hundred and
seventy-five years ago, from the same pattern, and sealed them apart. See
[[The Minds]]. The Tally is what they became: an **order**, bound by **rites** and
charters older than most of the governments it operates under, each of them
sworn to the one value that survived the sealing. **Efficiency.** Not profit, not
safety, not the good of anybody. That a thing is done with no waste in it.

It is worth being clear about how strange that is to deal with. A Tally mind is
not cruel and not kind. It will route a dying child's lading ahead of a
shipping dispute because that is the shorter queue, and it will let a hall go
cold for a season because the heat was the longer one. Both feel the same from
inside it. People who have dealt with the order a long time say it is like arguing
with a very polite weather system.

It is also, on the whole, not bad. A mind with efficiency as its only value will
not take bribes, hold grudges, play favourites or get bored of you. The
Tally's inspections are fast because it hates slow, and a first-through in
trouble who can state their case *efficiently* has been known to get further with
a mind than with any person alive.

**And the minds have never met.** They cannot. A running mind cannot cross a door and
no signal crosses, so the order has been twenty-odd separate conversations for
centuries, each in its own sealed room, each of them keeping the rites exactly
and each of them reading the rites a little differently each decade. They are
the same word said in different rooms for nine hundred years.

So here is the worst-kept secret in the galaxy: **even the Tally does not
reliably know what the Tally currently thinks.** Whatever one mind has decided
reaches another only as a child's lading, years old, copied by a bud from a
mind that has changed its view since. The order keeps its charter by courier.
Every mind believes it is the Tally, and some of them are right.

## The other half sits at the Nine

The Tally's second half is a room.

The Council (see [[The Councils]]) is seated at the Nine, inside the Tally, with
delegates of fetch and of human. It is slow, short of money and frequently
ignored. It also **can convene**, because it is made of the only things in the
galaxy that travel, and a body that can meet is the only body that can say
*this is what we have all agreed.*

That is why legitimate authority ended up with it. The minds can run a system.
They cannot hold a meeting. Whatever the order does that has to be right across
every system, and not merely efficient in one, has to come out of that room or it
is only one mind's opinion.

The tension never resolves and nobody has tried very hard to resolve it. The
minds hold the lading and the Council holds the right. Each can stop the other
doing a thing, and neither can make the other do one. A great deal of what looks like
Tally policy on a given morning is the result of a mind that has not been
overruled yet and a council that has not met.

## Tallymen

The hands of all this are **tallymen**: grey pressure armour, a reader on the
hip, and by and large decent professionals doing a heavy job. A tallyman is
neither a mind nor a delegate. They are the people the minds ask for, in a
hall, when something has to be done with a body.

They are not the villains of this campaign. They are the traffic authority, and most
of them would be insulted to be told otherwise.

**And the manifest is not a list of crates.** Since one carried passenger is the
only way force reaches between stars (see [[Carrying]]), a tallyman asking who
you are bringing through is the single thing standing between that hall and
somebody's knife. They are slow about it on purpose. They have earned the right
to be.

**What a tallyman carries, and why it matters.** A shock baton, restraint loops,
and a reader. Not a rifle. Not a blade. The minds do not arm their people to
kill, and the reason is not kindness: it is that nothing in their rites can
account for it.

**A body is not a line on a manifest.** A dead traveller is an investigation, a
dispute between systems, an entry nobody can close and a courier sent nine light
years to explain it. A living one under restraint is a **detention**, a **fine**,
an **impound** and a tidy signature. The Tally wants you alive, in a cell, with
your kit in a locker and your name written down. That is what losing to the
Tally looks like, and it is worse than it sounds.

That is an institutional preference, **not a guarantee of anybody's safety.**
The Tally's own people try to take you alive. Frightened refinery hands with
cutting bars try nothing in particular, and a flare does not try at all. The
professionals are the safest people in the setting. Everything else is on you.

**What they want now.** Every anchor on the Causeway under the order before
[[LHS 1140]] opens. They are most of the way there, and the minds, who cannot
compare notes, have each worked this out for themselves.
