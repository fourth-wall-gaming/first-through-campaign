A fathom perceives fields directly. Doors are fields. In nine hundred and
seventy-five years the two have never been put together, for a reason that is
entirely mundane: fathoms read **rooms**, nobody thinks of a housing as a room,
and the fathoms who might have tried were not asked.

The player asks.

## What it needs

- It must be **asked**, by a person, and it will be done gladly.
- It is read **from the rim**, not the plate. Nothing enters the housing.
  Vantage cannot cross and is not trying to.
- It takes real minutes and a fathom hanging motionless over a working anchor
  while a hall watches, which is a social cost somebody has to absorb.

## What it finds

A **steep slope across the ring** — port side low — hunting back and forth a
hand's width, twice a second. A field that cannot settle, moving, with a
measurable geometry.

That is the thing Dray Enns felt as *two* and could not say.

## The number, and how it is built

The number is the beat. It is not Vantage's alone and it must not be — it is
three records that have never been in the same room:

| Who | What they have |
|---|---|
| **Vantage** | the slope across the rim, now, directly perceived |
| **Hesk** | nineteen years of lamp flicker on deck nine, because the lights are on the shutter circuit and he has always found it annoying |
| **[[Old Man Sirius]]** | seven hundred and ninety-four recorded dips in the housing draw, which he has had all along and had no reason to look at |
| **Pell Ivor** | forty-one step-ups for twenty-three crossings since Friday |

Put together they give:

> **Fourteen hours. Plus or minus four.**

Say the error bars out loud, every time, in Vantage's own voice. It will not
state the figure without them and it will correct anybody who repeats it without
them. The plus-or-minus is not a hedge; it is the whole character of the thing,
and later it is the reason the order matters.

## Running Vantage here

He is not triumphant. He has waited eleven years for somebody to ask him a
question and this is a much bigger one than he expected, and he handles it by
being extremely precise.

He will say, unprompted, which parts of the estimate he would not bet on. Let
him. A GM who plays him as merely vindicated has got half a character — see
his card.

## What this breaks

Nothing yet. But the player now has a measurable quantity, a geometry, and a
clock — and in a shed at the far end of that door there are two hundred years of
the same measurement, kept by hand, filed and never read. See
[[Entry Point: How Many]] §3.
