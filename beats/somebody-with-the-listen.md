The beat exists to reward the player for **not** testing a bad door with their
own body — and then to cost somebody anyway, which is the honest version.

A first-through with the **Listen** can feel what a housing is doing without
stepping into it. There is one on the station who is any good at it and he is
asleep off a late watch.

## Running it

Whoever goes and gets him is doing a real thing: waking a courier off a late
without authorisation, on a hunch, in front of a queue. Let the player own that
and do not soften it. It is the correct call and it is also not theirs to make,
and both are true.

He goes to the ring. He reaches.

**And it knocks him down.** Nosebleed, conscious, nothing broken, and he gets
out the only thing he can get out before he loses the thread:

> *"It's two. There's two of them. It's both."*

He cannot finish it, he cannot explain it, and he will not be able to later
either. Thirty people hear it.

## What he actually felt, for the GM only

A mouth pinned to a value that **two places now satisfy**. That is a stray in
progress and he is the first person in nine centuries to have put a hand on one
while it was happening. See [[The Hesitation]] and [[Straying]].

He does not know any of that. Nobody in the hall does. The player is holding
eight words and a man with a nosebleed.

## Do not

- Do not have him recover and explain it. The whole value of the beat is that
  the best instrument available produced eight words and a casualty.
- Do not have anybody in the hall interpret it out loud. Somebody will *guess*,
  loudly and wrongly, and that is good.
- Do not make him pitiable. He went because he was asked, he would go again, and
  he is annoyed about the nosebleed rather than frightened.

If the player apologises to him, let him answer it straight and briefly. It was
the right call. It still cost him.
