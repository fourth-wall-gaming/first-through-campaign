The mouth is going to arrive on a concrete apron in a shed at Ladder: a ring the
first survey crew put up in hope, which has never once opened, and which
children dare each other to stand in.

**And there is a fuel bowser parked on it.**

Not near it. On it. It has been the dry store for a hundred and ninety years and
the bowser has sunk **eleven centimetres** into the apron directly over the
ring, because a drain has been leaking under that slab for most of a decade and
nobody had any reason to care.

## Why this is the problem and not a delay

A mouth arrives where the potential says it arrives. It does not care what is
standing there. A ring with several tonnes of steel settled into the concrete
over it is a ring nobody can walk out of, and the people who need to walk out of
it are on the wrong side.

Clearing it is the whole problem, and it has to be solved by **the people who
are there**, with what is there, in the time that is left.

## The two halves of the answer, and who holds them

- **Hesk** diagnoses it without being on the rock: an undermined slab, from a
  drain, and that is why it sank at all. Give this to any engineer-minded
  character who asks the right question, or to Hesk off a description.
- **Rill** works out the move: you do not **roll** a sunken bowser off a slab
  you need intact. You cut the slab and **drop** it. Take the whole thing down
  into a hole of your own making and leave the ring standing in the pit wall.

Neither of them is the expert in the room. That is the point. The engineer who
has never seen the rock reads the cause; the twenty-one-year-old courier who
cannot be the one to carry much longer reads the move.

## The pit, which pays off twice

Cutting a hole the size of a bunkroom in the apron is the only way to clear the
ring, and it is also — four metres of rock between a person and the sky — the
only hardened shelter on Ladder.

So the thing that nearly stops the rescue is the thing that saves everybody when
the flare front arrives. **Do not point this out.** Let Tace Orun call it on the
day, off nineteen years of living under that sky, and let the player notice
afterwards or not at all.
