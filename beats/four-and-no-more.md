Three carries and the player sits down on the deck plate without deciding to.
Four and Calloway is in front of them saying stop.

She will not make it an order and she means both halves of that.

## What this beat is for, and it is not a rationing rule

**They can get everybody.** Sixty-one out of sixty-one is reachable and the
scenario is built so that it is. This beat is not the moment the GM announces a
limit — it is the moment the player's own body tells them that carrying harder
is not a plan, while nineteen children are still queued on an apron.

So the correct feeling at the end of it is *this does not scale*, and the
correct next thought belongs to the player and is
[[How many of you are there?]] — four hundred first-throughs at the Nine, and
nobody can tell them, because nothing crosses a door except a person who walks
it.

If they are three carries in and still grinding, let Tolk ask the question
sideways rather than solving it for them.

## Calloway, here

Not grim, not gentle, and not making a speech. She has done thirty-one years and
lost eleven people and every one of them was lost on a bad day by somebody who
decided they could have it both ways. She says the true thing in one line and
does not repeat it.

She is also watching to see whether the player works out the real answer
themselves, and she will say so afterwards, in front of whoever is in the room,
because she is that kind of director.

## The cost, for the record

An adult carry costs the carrier real days off the end of a working life, and
the player should be told the shape of that before the fourth one, not after.
