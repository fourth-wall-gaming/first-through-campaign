**Transit is zero and stays zero.** Nothing in this beat changes that, and a GM
who narrates a long crossing has broken the setting. See [[The Hesitation]].

What has changed is that door seven will not **open** on demand. A courier steps
up, gives it her weight, and it goes absolute — the way a wall is absolute. She
eases off, offers it again, and after a moment it has her, and the crossing
itself is instant, exactly as it has always been.

Every first-through already has a word for that happening once: **it sets**. What
nobody has a word for is it happening all week.

## How it presents, in order

1. **First watch.** One courier stands there a moment too long and is annoyed
   about it. Nobody writes anything down, because nobody logs a sticky door.
2. **By mid-morning** the queue at seven has stopped moving at the pace a queue
   moves. It is longer than it should be for the hour and the people in it are
   standing about rather than shuffling forward.
3. **The loaders have an explanation and it is wrong**, and it is the most
   reassuring thing anybody will say all day: *seven's sticky every spring, give
   it a second and it has you, come summer it stops, nobody thinks about it.*

## The trap, and do not disarm it

That explanation is true about the last twelve springs and false about this one,
and the player should be allowed to accept it and walk away. Let them. The clock
keeps running whether they looked or not, and the beat that catches it is
[[Pell Ivor's board]].

A player who believes the loaders and goes off to have a good day has not failed
at anything. They have been told a sensible thing by a competent adult, and the
scenario is built to come back for them.

## What is actually behind it

Ladder. Sixty-one people, nineteen of them children, on a rock with no air of
its own, a welded roof, and no hardened shelter — and the worst flare season in
the binary's cycle arriving with closest approach.

**The door is not closing.** It is going to move. See [[Entry Point: How Many]].
